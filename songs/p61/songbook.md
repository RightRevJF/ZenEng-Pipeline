# At a glance

**What it's about.** Time, felt four ways. A chair in a waiting room where the clock forgets how to move; a laugh at a table while the afternoon slips by; a stadium roar where the clock makes no sound; a fever that breaks at three in the morning. Under all of it runs one wire, every hour strung from the cradle to the stone. The bridge puts the clock down: none of it was lost, none of it was wasted. The song ends where it began, in the same chair, but the weight inside it is a grace now.

**How it goes.** Slow-build alternative rock in A major, for a baritone. It is written at double tempo with a half-time feel: the snare lands on beat 3 of every written bar, and every tempo you feel is half the one on the page. It starts at ♩ = 116 (felt 58) with one fingerpicked acoustic guitar and grows section by section (bass, then brushes, then the full kit, overdriven guitars and organ) while the tempo climbs to ♩ = 164 (felt 82) at the bridge. After the bridge, a bar of silence, four slow bars of guitar, and a final chorus at ♩ = 132 (felt 66), slower and steadier than before. The tag, at ♩ = 110 (felt 55), is slower than the opening.

**The hook.** "time is breaking through" ends every chorus. In the first three it lands on C♯4 and holds, still waiting. Only in the final chorus does it climb to E4, the song's top note, sung nowhere else. The harmony waits the same way: a full A major chord never sounds on a downbeat until that last "through."

# For each player

**Voice.** A baritone, A2 to E4. The verses are testimony: plain, close, unhurried, telling it, not selling it. Lean into the choruses but keep the held C♯4 on "through" open and unresolved; it is supposed to feel like waiting. In the bridge come right down, then let the second pass rise to D4. Save everything for the final chorus's E4; it is the one time the song reaches it.

**Harmony vocals.** Two parts, one above and one below: on the hook in every chorus, the first four lines of chorus 3, and every line of the final chorus (high F♯3–A4, low A2–C♯4). Blend under the lead.

**Acoustic guitar** (E2–F♯4). Steel-string. Fingerpicked in the intro, verse 1, pre-chorus 1, verse 2 and the bridge's first pass; strummed from pre-chorus 2 on; picked again in the approach and the tag. Alone in the intro, verse 1, the approach and the tag.

**Electric guitar, clean** (B3–D5). Swells in pre-chorus 1; a held chord in chorus 1; eighth-note arpeggios in choruses 2 and 3, the bridge (from bar 145) and the final chorus.

**Electric guitar, overdriven** (E2–D4). Root, fifth and octave. Palm-muted quarters in verse 2 and pre-chorus 2; open chords in chorus 2; palm-muted eighths in verse 3; eighths in chorus 3; in verse 4, two bars palm-muted, two bars open, in turn; in at bar 153 of the bridge; out at the final chorus's G, back on the Asus4.

**Organ** (A3–B4). A pad in every chorus, pre-chorus 3, verse 4 and the bridge's second pass; stays with the voice at the final chorus's G.

**Bass** (E1–E3). Held roots in pre-chorus 1; a line from chorus 1 on; held roots in the bridge's first pass from bar 143, the line again from bar 149; out at the final chorus's G; a low A under the release.

**Drums.** Half-time throughout: the snare (or cross-stick) on beat 3 of each written bar.

- *Intro, verse 1, pre-chorus 1, approach, tag:* out.
- *Chorus 1:* light: kick, cross-stick, hat in quarters.
- *Verse 2, pre-chorus 2:* brushes; a snare roll into chorus 2.
- *Chorus 2, verse 3, pre-chorus 3:* full kit; a fill at the end of pre-chorus 3.
- *Chorus 3:* the ride in eighths; crash on each line's downbeat.
- *Verse 4:* driving, hats in eighths.
- *Bridge:* kick alone for eight bars, then the kit building to a fill and a full stop.
- *Final chorus:* full, steady; out at the G; back with a crash on the Asus4 and another on the A.

# Arrangement

The tempo is the story. The song starts at a waiting-room pulse and speeds up through every verse and chorus, the band filling in as it goes: one guitar, then bass, brushes, the kit, overdrive and organ. The bridge is the peak and then the break: a bar of silence and four bars of one guitar, slowing, like lying still after a fever. The final chorus comes back slower than the choruses before it, full but steady, and saves the song's top note and its first full A chord for the last "through." The tag is the opening image again, slower than ever, on one guitar.

## Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** Testimony, not celebration. Someone who has waited, drifted, been carried away and been broken open, telling you that none of it was wasted. The build should feel earned and the ending should feel changed: the same chair, a different weight.

**The three references.**

- **Coldplay, "Fix You"** (*X&Y*, 2005): an organ-led ballad that builds into full alternative rock in its second half. Take the shape of the build and the organ's role.
- **Snow Patrol, "Run"** (*Final Straw*, 2003; single 2004): a slow guitar ballad that grows into an anthem. Take the patience of the climb and the big, steady last chorus.
- **Band of Horses, "The Funeral"** (*Everything All the Time*, 2006): quiet verses that break open into a loud, ringing chorus. Take the quiet-to-loud dynamics and the ringing guitars.

**The dynamic arc.**

| Section | Bars | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro, verse 1 | 1–20 | pp | one acoustic guitar and the voice |
| Pre-chorus 1 | 21–24 | p → mp | bass in; clean guitar swells; speeding up |
| Chorus 1 | 25–44 | mp | the kit in, light; organ; harmony on the hook |
| Verse 2, pre-chorus 2 | 45–64 | mp → mf | brushes; muted overdrive; a roll |
| Chorus 2 | 65–84 | mf | full kit; open overdrive |
| Verse 3, pre-chorus 3 | 85–104 | mf → f | full kit; C♯dim7 and a fill |
| Chorus 3 | 105–124 | f | the most so far; crashes |
| Verse 4 | 125–140 | f | driving |
| Bridge | 141–156 | p → ff | stripped to guitar and kick, then the build to a full stop |
| Approach | 157–164 | silence → pp | a bar of silence; one guitar, slowing |
| Final chorus | 165–186 | f, steady | thins at the G; the band back on Asus4; the release to A |
| Tag | 187–194 | pp | one guitar; the last A decays |

**The non-negotiables.** The tempo plan as written, no click track that fights it. The held C♯4 on "through" in choruses 1–3, unresolved over Dmaj7. The bar of silence after the bridge, and the four bars of one guitar after it. E4 sung only once, on the final "through," over Asus4 releasing to A. No full A chord on a downbeat before that moment. The tag slower than the opening.

**The mix priorities.** The voice forward and close in the verses, every word clear. The acoustic guitar at the centre of the first half; the overdriven guitar wide but under the voice in the second. The organ as glue, never a lead. In the silent bar, true silence: no reverb tails.

## Production brief

*The song.* A slow-build alternative rock song about four kinds of time, a wire running under all of them, and the moment it breaks through.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Coldplay, "Fix You."** From *X&Y* (Parlophone, 2005), written by all four members; built around an organ with piano and guitar in its first half and full alternative rock (electric guitar, bass, drums) in its second; released as the album's second single on 5 September 2005; number 4 in the UK.
- **Snow Patrol, "Run."** From *Final Straw* (2003), produced by Jacknife Lee; written by Gary Lightbody, Iain Archer, Mark McClelland, Nathan Connolly and Jonny Quinn; released as a single in early 2004; number 5 in the UK; the album version runs 5:56.
- **Band of Horses, "The Funeral."** From *Everything All the Time* (Sub Pop, March 21, 2006), produced by Phil Ek; released as a single in the US on May 21, 2006; 5:21.

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.
