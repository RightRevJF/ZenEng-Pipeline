# Nothing Else on Earth

*Songbook · Words and music: Jason Bryant*

Folk rock after The Battle of Evermore: D major, ♩ = 116, 110 bars, 3:48. A duet: Lark sings the verses, Briar sings the choruses.

## At a glance

**What it's about.** A walled garden where the food is clean, coded and tasteless. Lark watches an old man, Briar, who hasn't spoken in years. He leans in and says one thing: it used to taste like nothing else on earth. In the bridge a girl reaches through a gap in the wall and eats something wild. Nobody writes it down.

**How it plays.** Two worlds. The verses are folk: mandolin, acoustic guitar and hand percussion under Lark's low, close voice. At each chorus the band arrives like weather (drums, bass, electric guitar, strings) and Briar sings high and worn. Play the quiet parts truly quiet so the chorus can land.

## Section by section

### Intro (bars 1–8): D | C | G | D | D | C | G | D

The mandolin plays the riff alone for four bars: picked eighths, with the open D and A strings ringing on every eighth and a five-note top line (D5 F♯5 C♯6 F♯5 D5 over D), moved onto each chord. The acoustic guitar and hand percussion come in at bar 5.

'And the hands of someone gone' climbs the same way, to B4 on 'SOME-' (bar 31). 'You got up WHOLE' lands on B4 over D, its sixth (bar 37, beat 3).

## For the singers

| Singer | Sings | Range | The voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lark (mezzo) | verses, pre-choruses, bridge; harmony in chorus 2 and the last chorus; alone in bars 100–101 | A3–D5 (A3–F♯4 in the verses) | Close and conversational in the verses, as if telling it at a table. Open up in the bridge. |
| Briar (an older man, high and worn) | the choruses, the tag | D4–B4, at written pitch | A cry, not a belt. These are the first words he has said in years; let the B4 on 'NOTH-ing' crack a little if it wants to. |

**Lark.** The verses sit low on purpose: the chorus has to sound higher than anything before it. Keep the long verse lines even and unhurried, nearly spoken ('and they fold another leaf of the long table, and nobody asks why'). In the pre-chorus, pull back on 'years' (B3), so Briar's entrance is the first time the song opens.

**Briar.** Sing the chorus at the written pitch, the top of a tenor's range. 'Earth' on A4 lands on beat 1 every time (bars 26, 34, 58, 66, 90, 98, 104) and is held two to four beats, a whole bar the first time: hold it with the band underneath, not over it. The tag's last 'earth' (D4, bar 106) is the only time he comes home. Let it sit low and quiet against the ringing chord.

**The harmony.** In chorus 2 and the last chorus Lark sings a third above Briar on every line, F♯4 to D5, peaking at D5 on 'NOTH-ing'. Match his phrasing exactly, syllable for syllable. In bars 100–101 she takes the melody alone ('she sat down hungry and she got up whole'), landing on B4 for 'whole'. Then both voices return for the hook and the tag.

**Breaths.** Every line ends with at least an eighth of space before the next; breathe there. The longest unbroken phrase is verse 2's 'and they fold another leaf' line (bars 46–49): take a breath before 'and nobody asks why'.

## For each player

**Mandolin.** The riff is the song's signature: eight picked eighths a bar, the open D and A strings alternating on every eighth as a drone, and a top line on the eighths of beats 1, 1-and, 2-and, 3 and 3-and. Over D the top line is D5 F♯5 C♯6 F♯5 D5; it moves with each chord (over C it uses C natural and B). Play it in bars 1–8 (alone for the first four), 9–16 and 41–48 under Lark (softer, behind her), 81–82 and 87–88 at the edges of the solo, and 107–109. Everywhere else, strum the chord in eighths, accenting the downbeats; a little harder when the band is in. Bar 110: one D chord, let it ring.

**Acoustic guitar.** Steel-string, open chords, strummed eighths from bar 5 to the end: down, down, down-up, down, down-up, down-up (accent the downbeats). It is the floor of the folk sections and keeps going under the band. Bar 110: one D chord, ring.

**Hand percussion (folk sections).** Floor tom on beats 1 and 3, soft; tambourine on 2 and 4. In from bar 5, through both verses, the bridge and the outro. In each pre-chorus the floor tom plays every beat and grows louder bar by bar. Bar 110: one floor-tom hit.

**Drums (the band's sections).** The kit plays only in the choruses (bars 25–40, 57–72, 89–106) and the riff break (81–88). Kick on 1, 3 and the 'and' of 3; snare on 2 and 4; ride in eighths; crash on the first bar of each section, and every four bars in the last chorus. Fill into each storm in bars 24, 56 and 80: a crash on beat 3, then sixteenths down the snare and toms. Chorus 2 adds tambourine on 2 and 4; the last chorus adds tambourine in eighths. Play the break a notch under the choruses so the last chorus is the biggest moment.

**Bass.** Tacet in the intro and the verses. In each pre-chorus, roots in half notes (with an approach note into the next chord). Choruses and the break: eighths, root, root, fifth, root, root, octave, fifth, approach note into the next bar. Bridge: whole-note roots. Range A1 to B3. Bar 110: low D, ring.

**Electric rhythm guitar.** Choruses only: open power chords (root, fifth, octave) in eighths, lightly overdriven. In the riff break, quarter notes, to leave room for the solo.

**Lead guitar.** Short fills in the singers' gaps in the choruses (bars 28, 34, 37–38, 40–41, 70, 90, 94–95, 98): four to six notes, never over a sung note, ending on a chord tone. The eight-bar solo in bars 81–88 opens on the mandolin riff's top line and answers it a step higher, walks freely for four bars, and closes on the riff with the mandolin.

**Strings.** A pad of three-note chords from D4 up. Swell in through each pre-chorus; hold through the choruses, the bridge and the break; in the last chorus add an octave on top. Bar 110: one D chord, ring. Tacet in the verses.

## The arrangement

The song alternates between a folk band and a storm. The folk band (mandolin, acoustic, hand percussion) carries Lark's telling. The storm (kit, bass, electric guitar, strings) arrives only when Briar speaks. Each pre-chorus is the weather turning: the floor tom fills every beat, the bass and strings enter, and a drum fill opens the door.

| Section | Bars | Who plays | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–8 | mandolin; acoustic and hand percussion from bar 5 | low |
| Verse 1 | 9–20 | mandolin, acoustic, hand percussion | lowest |
| Pre-chorus 1 | 21–24 | + bass, strings swelling, tom on every beat; fill | rising |
| Chorus 1 | 25–40 | everyone | high |
| Verse 2 | 41–52 | the folk band again | low |
| Pre-chorus 2 | 53–56 | as pre-chorus 1 | rising |
| Chorus 2 | 57–72 | everyone, + tambourine, Lark's harmony | high |
| Bridge | 73–80 | folk band, strings, bass whole notes; fill | middle |
| Riff break | 81–88 | everyone; guitar solo | high, a notch under the choruses |
| Last chorus | 89–106 | everyone, tambourine eighths, strings an octave up, both voices | highest |
| Outro | 107–110 | mandolin riff, floor tom; one D chord | low, then ringing |

The drop back to the folk band at bars 41 and 73 matters as much as the arrivals: cut the kit, bass, electric rhythm guitar and strings clean on the downbeat (the lead guitar's last fill may ring over into bar 41), so the verse lands in sudden quiet.

## Production notes

### Producer one-sheet

- **Emotional target:** a hushed, almost fearful telling that breaks open into longing. The listener should feel the taste come back in the chorus.
- **References:** The Battle of Evermore (the duet and the mandolin), Ramble On (quiet acoustic verses, the band crashing into the chorus), Babe I'm Gonna Leave You (the quiet parts truly quiet). Details in the brief below.
- **Dynamic arc:** lowest in verse 1; three storms, each bigger; the bridge falls back; the last chorus is the summit; the outro returns to the mandolin alone.
- **Non-negotiables:** the verses with no kit and no bass; the hook's climb to B4 on 'NOTH-ing' and the held A4 on 'earth'; Lark alone on 'she sat down hungry and she got up whole'; the tag home to D4; the last D chord left to ring.
- **Mix priorities:** voices first; the mandolin riff second, bright and present in the intro and outro and tucked behind Lark in the verses; in the choruses the kit and electric guitar under Briar, never over him.

### Production brief

A folk-rock duet in the light-and-shade tradition of early-1970s Led Zeppelin: mandolin and acoustic for the telling, the full band for the memory.

### Session notes

- **Tempo and click:** ♩ = 116, straight eighths, one tempo throughout. Two bars of click before bar 1.
- **Key:** D major. Chords by bar: Intro and Outro D C G D; Verse D C G D D C G D Bm G A A; Pre-chorus G A Bm A; Chorus D A Bm G D A G A / D A Bm G D A G D (the last chorus adds G D); Bridge Bm G D A Bm G Em A; Riff break D C G D D C G D.
- **Ranges:** Lark A3–D5; Briar D4–B4 at pitch; mandolin top line to C♯6; bass A1–B3.
- **Tracking order:** mandolin riff and acoustic to the click first; hand percussion; Lark's guide; then the band for the choruses and break (kit, bass, electric rhythm, lead fills and solo); Briar; Lark's harmony as its own pass; strings last.
- **Separate passes:** the mandolin's riff and its strummed sections (one player, different parts); Lark's lead and her harmony (two passes); the lead guitar's fills and its solo.
- **Blend and panning:** voices centre, Lark slightly left of Briar for the harmony; mandolin right, acoustic left; electric rhythm left, lead right; kit and bass centre; strings wide and back.
