# Nothing Else on Earth — draft 3

*Words and music: Jason Bryant*

**Verse 1**
Six letters stamped on every pear,
a bell above the gate,
ivy someone planted so the stone would look like welcome,
a kind man at the window tells you what is safe to taste.
The food is very clean today.
Nobody looks fed.

**Pre-chorus 1**
You lean in, old as the vines,
and say the first thing you've said in years:

**Chorus 1**
It used to taste like nothing else on earth:
honey and black pepper, rain on a hot stone,
bread from an oven nobody owned,
and the hands of someone gone.
It used to taste like nothing else on earth,
the bitter and the sweet grown on one vine,
you sat down hungry and you got up whole,
and nobody asked what it was worth.

**Verse 2**
A grey coat, a careful bite,
a pleasant little nod:
"It reminds me of the gardens back home," and the whole room glows,
and they fold another leaf of the long table, and nobody asks why.
Every plum tastes like the plum before.
You close your eyes.

**Pre-chorus 2**
You turn the bottle no one ever opened
and say it to the cork instead of me:

**Chorus 2** (as Chorus 1)

**Bridge**
Round the back, where the wall falls into green,
a girl with thistle on her sleeve
puts her whole arm through the light,
takes the shadow and the shine in one bite;
her face goes winter, then goes June,
and no one writes it down.

**Last Chorus**
It used to taste like nothing else on earth:
honey and black pepper, rain on a hot stone,
bread from an oven nobody owned,
and the hands of someone gone.
It used to taste like nothing else on earth,
the bitter and the sweet grown on one vine,
she sat down hungry and she got up whole,
and it tastes like nothing else on earth.
Nothing else on earth.
