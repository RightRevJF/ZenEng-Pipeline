## What the song is about

It is still dark. Mary Magdalene comes to the tomb with the spices, her hair tied back for the work, because she knows how to wash the dead. The stone has been moved. Inside, the linen lies flat and the cloth that wrapped his head is folded by itself, as if he had made the bed. Two figures in white sit on either side of the empty stone. She turns, and someone is standing among the trees. She takes him for the gardener, the first one out at dawn, and asks him where he has put the body.

Then he says one word: "Mary." She answers "Rabboni," and the song sees what happened. God walked the first garden in the evening and called the man he had made by name. She walked this one in the dark and called, and he answered with her name. The last refrain turns her mistake into the truth: his hands in the earth again, come back to tend his own. *I took him for the gardener, and he was.*

The scene is John 20:1–18; the first garden is Genesis 2–3. The song stays in her body and her eyes: no angels named, no glory, no trumpets. A woman at a grave, a man she does not know, and her name.

**Playing it:**

- **Start in the dark.** Verse 1 is low and plain, over almost nothing.
- **The verses reach for the refrain and fall short.** Verses 1–3 open on the refrain's first notes (F, A, G, A) and turn down to F4. The refrain takes the same notes up to D5 on "gardener."
- **Bar 54 ("said my name"): everyone stops.** The voice sings "name" alone.
- **Bar 55 ("Mary"): D major arrives,** soft and full. The F♯ she sings there is the first in the song.
- **"Earth" (bar 72) is the summit; "and he was" (bar 78) is the close.** The last line ends on the tonic, settled.

## The song, section by section

Each line takes two bars of 6/8, felt in two (two dotted-quarter beats a bar). Chords are shown beside each line.

### Intro · bars 1–4

*Dm B♭ Gm A.* Piano alone: the left hand's walking figure in eighths (root, fifth, tenth | octave, tenth, fifth), a soft right-hand chord in bars 1 and 3.

### Verse 1 · bars 5–12

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Dm · B♭ | It was still dark. I brought the spices; |
| Gm · A | I knew how to wash the dead. |
| Dm · F | I'd tied my hair back for the work, |
| Gm · A | and the stone was moved instead. |

- **Melody:** low and close, D4–B♭4. "It was" climbs from D4; "still" F4 (bar 5), "dark" A4, "I" G4, "**brought**" B♭4 at the downbeat of bar 6: the refrain's opening shape, which then falls to F4 on "spices." "Dead" on A4 (bar 8). "Back" dips to D4 (bar 9). "Instead" lands on A4 over A (bar 12).
- **Accompaniment:** the piano's left hand alone, bare: the root and fifth in dotted quarters.

### Refrain 1 · bars 13–20

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Dm · B♭ | I took him for the gardener, |
| Gm · F | the first one out at dawn; |
| B♭ · C | I said, sir, if you carried him, |
| Gm · A | just tell me where he's gone. |

- **Melody:** F4–D5. "I took him for the" climbs F4, A4, G4, A4, C5, and "**gar-den-er**" sits on D5 over B♭ at the downbeat of bar 14, all three syllables on one note. "The first one out at dawn" falls from D5 ("out") to A4 on "dawn" over F (bar 16). "Car-ried" on C5 over C (bar 18). "Where" reaches D5 again (bar 19); "gone" settles on A4 over A (bar 20), a half cadence that does not close: she still does not know where he is.
- **Accompaniment:** the piano's walking figure returns, with a right-hand chord on each downbeat; the strings enter, very softly.

### Verse 2 · bars 21–28

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Dm · B♭ | I found his linen lying flat, |
| Gm · A | the cloth that wrapped his head |
| Dm · F | folded in a place by itself, |
| Gm · A | as if he'd made the bed. |

- **Melody:** D4–B♭4. The first line is the refrain's opening again (F4 A4 G4 A4), reaching B♭4 on "ly-" (bar 22) and falling to F4 on "flat." Then the verse sinks: "head" on E4 (bar 24), "a" on D4 (bar 25), "itself" on E4 (bar 26), "bed" on E4 (bar 28).
- **Accompaniment:** the cello enters, the root held a bar, and **answers** lines 2–4: as she lands, it plays her last three notes an octave below, in eighths (bars 24, 26 and 28).

### Refrain 2 · bars 29–36

As refrain 1, a little fuller, with the cello.

### Interlude · bars 37–40

*Dm B♭ Gm A.* The cello sings the refrain's first two lines an octave below the voice, over the piano's figure, with **one note changed**: "dawn" rises to C♯4 over A (bar 40) instead of the voice's A. It is the leading tone, the first lean toward D. The strings swell under it.

### Verse 3 · bars 41–48

| Gm · A | the stone between them clean; |
| Dm · F | they asked me why I cried. I turned, |

- **Melody:** D4–B♭4. The first line is the verses' opening once more: "ei-" on B♭4 (bar 42), "side" down to F4. "Clean" on E4 (bar 44). "Why" drops to D4 (bar 45). "I turned" rises to A4 (bar 46). "Trees" on A4 over A (bar 48).
- **Accompaniment:** piano and cello; the cello answers line 2 (bar 44).

### The Name · bars 49–58

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| B♭ · Gm | He asked me who I came to find; |
| Dm · A | I said, just give me back the body. |
| Gm · N.C. | And then he only said my name. |
| D · D · G · A | Mary. And I said, Rabboni. |

- **Melody:** "Who" on A4 (bar 49). "Give" reaches D5 (bar 51); "the body" falls back to A4 (bar 52). "Said" on C5 (bar 54) and **"name" on A4, sung alone.**
- **"Mary":** F♯5 over D at the downbeat of bar 55, held, both syllables on the one note, quietly, as he would say it. It is the first F♯ in the song: the music is in D major from here. Bar 56: silence in the voice. "And I said" on B4, B4, D5 over G (bar 57); "**Rab-bo-ni**" from A4 up to E5 over A (bar 58).
- **Accompaniment:** strings and cello build softly under the first two lines. **Bar 54: everyone is silent.** Bar 55: everyone back on D major, one soft chord a bar, no figure, held through the voice's silent bar.

### Verse 4 · bars 59–66 (D major)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| D · G | God walked the first garden in the evening |
| Em · A | and called the man he'd made by name; |
| D · G | I walked this one in the dark and called, |
| Bm · A | and he answered with my name. |

- **Melody:** G4–F♯5. "God" on A4, "walked" D5 (bar 59), down through C♯5 and B4 to A4 on "gar-," back up to D5 on "evening" (bar 60). "Made" on E5 (bar 62). "Dark" on D5 (bar 64) and "called" on E5, just before the beat, like a call. "An-swered" on **F♯5** (bar 65); "name" on C♯5 over A (bar 66).
- **Accompaniment:** everyone, fuller: the piano's right hand on both beats, cello, strings.

### Refrain 3 · bars 67–78 (broadening, ♩. = 48)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| D · G | I took him for the gardener, |
| G · D | the first one out at dawn, |
| G · D | his hands in the **earth** again, |
| Em · A | come back to tend his own. |
| D · G · A · D | I took him for the gardener, and he was. |

- **Melody:** the refrain's tune in major: "I" on F♯4, "the" on C♯5, "gar-den-er" on D5 over G (bar 68); "dawn" on A4 (bar 70). Then the climb: "his hands in the earth" rises D5, E5, F♯5 to **A5 on "earth," over D at the downbeat of bar 72**, the only A5 in the song; "again" falls through G5 to F♯5. "Come" on E5 (bar 73); "own" on C♯5 over A (bar 74).
- **The last line:** the title once more (bars 75–76, "gar-den-er" on D5 over G). **Bar 77: the voice is silent** while the strings alone hold A. Then "and he" on A4 and C♯5, and "**was**" on D5 over D at the downbeat of bar 78, held: closed, on the tonic.
- **Accompaniment:** the fullest of the song, with a held top line in the strings, until the last line thins; in bar 77 only the strings and one low A in the piano.

### Coda · bars 79–82 (♩. = 44)

*D G D D.* Piano alone. The right hand plays the refrain's first phrase once more in D major (F♯4, A4, G4, A4, C♯5, D5, the notes she sang in bar 67), so the song leaves on its own tune. Then D, and the last chord: D with an added E, **A4 on top**. Let it ring.

## For the players

- **Piano:** the walking figure throughout, the left hand rocking in eighths: root, fifth, tenth | octave, tenth, fifth (verse 1 bare: root and fifth in dotted quarters). The right hand adds a chord on each downbeat from refrain 1, held, and on both beats in verse 4 and the last refrain. **Bar 54: silent.** Bars 55–56: one soft D major chord a bar, no figure. Bar 77: one low A, nothing else. Coda alone: the refrain's first phrase in the right hand, then the last chord with A on top.
- **Cello:** in from verse 2, the root held a bar. Answer verse 2's lines 2–4 and verse 3's line 2 (bars 24, 26, 28, 44): the singer's last three notes an octave below, in eighths, as she lands. In the interlude, the melody: the refrain's first two lines an octave below the voice, with "dawn" on C♯ instead of A (bar 40). Silent in bar 54; back on "Mary." Held bass from verse 4; out in bar 77.
- **Strings:** a soft pad under the refrains; the swell in the interlude; building softly under the Name's first two lines; **silent in bar 54**; soft and full on "Mary"; full in verse 4 and the last refrain, with a held top line; alone in bar 77, holding A.

## For the singer

**Two co-equal editions: soprano (D4–A5, one A5) and mezzo (a minor third lower, B3–F♯5). Each has an ossia a minor third below the top note, on "earth."**

- **You are telling it afterward.** Every verse is a thing you saw. Let the details carry it: the spices, the hair tied back, the folded cloth.
- **The verses are low.** Don't push them. The refrain lifts to D5 on "gardener"; that lift is the first light.
- **"Just tell me where he's gone":** a plea. "Where" reaches up; "gone" settles and does not close.
- **"Name" (bar 54) is sung alone.** Take your time. Then "Mary" on F♯5, quietly, both syllables on one note, the way he would say it. Don't make it a high note; make it a name.
- **"Rabboni"** is the answer, rising to E5. Then verse 4 opens in the light.
- **The climb to "earth"** is the summit: D5 to A5 over one line. Breathe after "dawn." If the A5 isn't secure, take the F♯5 ossia; nothing else changes.
- **"And he was."** After a bar of silence, sing it plainly, and close on D5.

## Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** Grief that does not know it is about to end. The listener should feel the dark and the cold work of the first verses, hold their breath at "said my name," and feel the room change on "Mary." The last line should land as recognition, quiet and certain.

**References**

- **"In the Garden" (C. Austin Miles, 1912):** the same scene, in Mary's voice. Take the first person; leave the sentiment.
- **"I Know That My Redeemer Liveth" (Handel, *Messiah*, 1741):** the soprano's Easter solo. Take its plainness and its quiet end.
- **"Easter," from *Five Mystical Songs* (Vaughan Williams, 1911):** one voice reflecting on the Resurrection. Take the intimacy.

**Dynamic arc**

| Section (bars) | Level | What changes |
|--------------------|------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Intro, verse 1 (1–12) | p → pp | Piano alone; verse 1 over the bare left hand |
| Refrains 1–2, verse 2 (13–36) | mp → mf | Strings in at refrain 1; cello in at verse 2 |
| Verse 2 (21–28) | p | The cello answers lines 2–4 |
| Interlude (37–40) | mp | The cello sings the refrain, one note changed; strings swell |
| Verse 3 (41–48) | p | Piano and cello |
| The Name (49–58) | p, building | Bar 54 ("said my name"): total silence. Bar 55 ("Mary"): everyone back, D major, soft |
| Verse 4 (59–66) | f | The full ensemble |
| Refrain 3 (67–78) | f → p | Broadens; A5 on "earth" (bar 72); bar 77 only the strings; "and he was" closes |
| Coda (79–82) | p | Piano alone: the refrain's first phrase in major; the last chord rings |

**Non-negotiables**

1. In bar 54, nothing sounds but the voice.
2. The major arrives on "Mary," not before. No F♯ anywhere earlier.
3. One A5, on "earth" (F♯5 ossia). Nothing else above G5.
4. "And he was" closes on D, quiet and settled.
5. No sweetness: no portamento, no choir pads, no bells, no harp.
6. Verse 1 over the bare piano.
7. The verses open on the refrain's first notes; the coda ends on the refrain's tune.

**Mix priorities**

1. **The words.** "I knew how to wash the dead" and "as if he'd made the bed" must land. The voice is always in front.
2. **The piano's walking figure** is the pulse; keep it clear but soft.
3. **The cello** answers the singer in verses 2 and 3 and leads the interlude; elsewhere it supports.
4. **The strings** sit behind and wide.
5. **Never cover:** "name," "Mary," the A5, "and he was." No reverb tail into bar 54. Verse 1 dry and close; the last refrain with more room.

## Production brief

**The song.** An Easter concert solo in the voice of Mary Magdalene, told looking back: she came in the dark to wash a body, found the tomb empty, took the risen Christ for the gardener, and knew him when he said her name. One high voice over piano, cello and strings, in D minor until that name, and in D major after it. The last line turns her mistake into the truth: "I took him for the gardener, and he was." It must stay plain and bodily: no trumpets, no glory, one silence before the name.

**References**

- **C. Austin Miles, "In the Garden" (1912):** a gospel song written from Mary Magdalene's point of view after Miles read John 20. It is also often called overly sentimental. Take the scene and the first person; avoid the sweetness.
- **G. F. Handel, "I Know That My Redeemer Liveth" (*Messiah*, Part III, 1741):** the soprano air that opens Part III, a Larghetto in 3/4 that ends quietly. Take its plain faith in a high voice, and the quiet end.
- **Ralph Vaughan Williams, "Easter" (*Five Mystical Songs*, 1911, words by George Herbert):** written for solo baritone, with piano among the accompaniment options; the first song is an intimate reflection on the Resurrection. Take the scale: one voice, close.

**Non-negotiables**

1. **One silence.** In bar 54 ("said my name") the whole ensemble is silent; the voice sings "name" alone.
2. **The turn to D major happens on "Mary"** (bar 55, F♯5 in the voice over D). Don't anticipate it.
3. **The A5 on "earth" (bar 72) is the only note above G5.** The F♯5 ossia is for singers who need it; the mezzo edition sings everything a minor third lower.
4. **The last line closes on D.** Bar 77 holds only the strings; "and he was" lands on D5 over D.
5. **No sweetness.** No portamento into notes, no choir "oohs," no bells, no harp glissandos.
6. **Verse 1 over the bare piano.** Let the song begin in the dark.

**Range**

- **Voice:** two co-equal editions. Soprano: D4–A5, one A5 ("earth," bar 72; F♯5 ossia). Mezzo: B3–F♯5, one F♯5 (D♯5 ossia).
- **Verses 1–3:** D4–B♭4. **Refrains 1–2:** F4–D5. **The Name:** F4–E5, with the one F♯5 on "Mary." **Verse 4:** G4–F♯5. **Refrain 3:** F♯4–A5.

**Tracking**

1. **Piano first**, to the click with the tempo changes, or live with the singer if possible: the rubato around bars 54 and 77 matters more than the grid.
2. **Cello** next: the answers in verses 2 and 3 (play them as a duet with the voice) and the interlude's melody as a solo take.
3. **Strings** last, as a section or layered, keeping bar 54 completely silent; bar 77 is theirs alone.
4. **Voice:** close and dry for verse 1; open the room as the song grows; record "Mary" and the last line quietly, separately if needed.

**Blend and panning**

- **Voice** centre, always in front. **Piano** centre-left; **cello** centre-right; **strings** wide and behind.
- **The silence:** cut any reverb tails that would cover bar 54.
- **The ending:** let the coda's last chord ring out completely.
