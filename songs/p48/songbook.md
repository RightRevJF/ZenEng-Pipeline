# At a glance

**What it's about.** Two strangers on a crowded dance floor. She can't read the other's face in the strobe light, and she can't make herself heard over the bass. Every way of getting through to another person fails. Then she notices that, without either of them trying, they have been moving in time since the lights went down. The chorus never claims to see inside anyone. It asks a better question: were they ever apart? The bridge wonders who set the beat before anyone arrived, and the last chorus says it plainly: "It was always the same wall." The image under the song is two pendulum clocks hung on one wall, which fall into step with each other through the wall they share.

**How it goes.** Mid-tempo dance-pop, ♩ = 112, four on the floor, straight. The verses sit in C minor, close and narrow. The chorus opens out over them in E-flat major, the same notes heard from a new home. A synth pulse is the song's other character. It plays in dotted eighths against the kick in the intro and the verses, so it drifts off the beat and back. It locks to straight eighths when the chorus arrives. Under "Two clocks on the same wall" the band drops out and the pulse alone ticks between the two notes the singer is rocking between. The bridge takes the kick away for six bars, then the beat comes back for the last chorus.

# Section by section

Pitches are where the voice sounds. Bar numbers count from the first bar of the intro.

## Intro (bars 1–8)

The synth pulse alone for four bars, in dotted eighths over the chord changes: it lines up with the bar only every three beats. Kick, bass (in quarters) and pad come in at bar 5, with the hats doubling the pulse's dotted eighths.

## Verse 1 (bars 9–16)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Cm A♭ | Strobe light cuts you into stills |
| E♭ B♭ | A different face in every flare |
| Cm A♭ | You sing along with your eyes closed |
| B♭ Cm | To someone who isn't there |

The first three lines come in just after the downbeat on G4, the last on F4. All four stay inside a narrow band, G4 down to C4 or D4, in quick sixteenths. The first line ends on E♭4 on beat 4 of bar 9. The last line comes home to C4 on the "and" of beat 3 in bar 15: the verse closes in C minor. Keep it close and level. This is the narrow frame the chorus will open.

## Pre-chorus 1 (bars 17–20)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Fm Gm | We all walked in on our own |
| A♭ B♭ | Till the kick came up through the bone |

The first line hovers on G4 and A♭4 and lands on G4 in bar 18. The second line jumps up to C5 on "kick" and falls to C4 on "bone" in bar 20. Claps come in on 2 and 4. The pulse and hats lock to straight eighths in bar 20 with a snare build: the beat catches up with the words.

## Chorus 1 (bars 21–28)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| E♭ B♭ | I can't get behind your eyes |
| Cm A♭ | But your count keeps falling into mine |
| E♭ B♭ | Two clocks on the same wall |
| A♭ B♭ | Can't help keeping time, keeping time |

The first line starts on C5 on beat 2 of bar 21 and stays up on B♭4. The second starts on D5 and falls to A♭4. Then the hook, in two parts:

- **"Two clocks on the same wall" (bars 25–26).** The voice rocks between G4 and F4, one note per syllable: G, F, G, F, G, and F on "wall" on the downbeat of bar 26. In bar 25 everything stops except the pulse, which ticks the same two notes in eighths. The band comes back on "wall" with a crash.
- **"Can't help keeping time, keeping time" (bars 26–28).** "Can't help" is a pickup on beats 3 and 4 of bar 26 (A♭4, B♭4). "Keeping time" is one figure, B♭4 B♭4 C5, sung on beat 1 of bar 27. Then the same figure a step higher, C5 C5 D5, on beat 1 of bar 28. The last "time" is D5, the top of the song.

# For each player

**Voice.** Mezzo, C4 to D5. Sing the verses close and even, like someone watching rather than performing. Open up for the chorus. "Two clocks" should rock as evenly as a pendulum: no slides, no ornaments. Give the two "keeping time" figures the same shape, the second one step higher. The post-chorus is light and rhythmic. Land every "one" squarely on the beat.

**Drums.**

- *Intro:* tacet bars 1–4. From bar 5, kick on every beat and closed hats in dotted eighths with the pulse.
- *Verses:* kick and dotted-eighth hats; no claps.
- *Pre-choruses:* claps on 2 and 4. The hats lock to the beat in the last bar, with a snare build.
- *Choruses:* crash on 1, claps on 2 and 4, open hats on the off-beats, sixteenth closed hats. Bar 5 of each chorus (bars 25, 49, 69): out. Crash and back in on bar 6.
- *Post-choruses:* locked; kick, open hats, claps.
- *Bridge:* no kick for bars 57–62. Claps and a snare build in bars 63–64, eighths then sixteenths.
- *Last chorus:* everything, sixteenth hats throughout.
- *Outro:* kick and dotted-eighth hats; one crash with the last chord.

**Synth bass.** Quarter notes on the root while the pulse drifts (intro from bar 5, verses, outro). Eighths, root and octave alternating, once it locks (pre-chorus ends, choruses, post-choruses). Whole notes in the bridge, bars 57–62. Out in bar 5 of each chorus. One low E-flat to end. Range B♭1 to A♭3.

**Pulse.** The song's clock: a bright, short synth note, G4 to D6.

- In dotted eighths through the intro, the verses and the first bars of each pre-chorus, so it slips against the beat and meets it every three beats.
- Straight eighths from the last bar of each pre-chorus through the choruses and post-choruses.
- Alone in bar 5 of each chorus, ticking the clocks' two notes (G and F, an octave above the voice).
- Quarters in the bridge.
- Drifting again in the outro, ending on one held note.

**Pad.** A held chord each bar from bar 5, an octave wider in the choruses and the bridge. Out in bar 5 of each chorus. One E-flat chord to end. Range G3 to B♭4.

# Arrangement

The song is a demonstration as much as a lyric. In the verses, the pulse and the hats run in dotted eighths, so they are always slightly out of step with the kick and the voice. Each pre-chorus catches them up. In the chorus everything locks. The band then stops for one bar so the listener hears the two clocks alone, and comes back together on "wall." The bridge pulls the beat out entirely, leaving only space, and brings it back so the last chorus arrives as a homecoming. The outro lets the pulse drift once more and ends on a single E-flat chord: the new home.

## Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** A crowded floor, two strangers who can't reach each other in any way they try, and the slow surprise of finding they've been in step all along. Cool and watchful in the verses, warm and open in the chorus. Never preachy: the song shows rather than tells.

**The three references.**

- **Robyn, "Dancing On My Own"** (2010): the dance floor as a place to be alone among people, at about 117 BPM. Take the mid-tempo drive and the feeling of a sad song you can dance to, then turn it the other way: here the floor is where the loneliness ends.
- **Caribou, "Can't Do Without You"** (2014): no drop, a continuous rise, layered and hypnotic. Take the patience and the repetition, and the way the track seduces rather than bludgeons.
- **U2, "Where the Streets Have No Name"** (1987): a dotted-eighth delay turning one part into a galloping pulse, with the bass and drums arriving later. Take the dotted-eighth pulse as the model for this song's drifting synth.

**The dynamic arc.**

| Section | Bars | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–8 | mp → mf | the pulse alone, then kick, bass, pad |
| Verses | 9–16, 33–40 | mf | lean; pulse and hats drifting |
| Pre-choruses | 17–20, 41–44 | mf ↗ | claps; the lock and the snare build |
| Choruses, post-choruses | 21–32, 45–56 | f | locked and full; bar 5 drops to the pulse alone |
| Bridge | 57–64 | mp ↗ | no kick, space; the beat comes back |
| Last chorus | 65–72 | ff | everything |
| Last post-chorus | 73–76 | f | locked; home to E-flat |
| Outro | 77–80 | mf | the pulse drifts; one chord, let ring |

**The non-negotiables.**

- The pulse drifts in the verses and locks in the chorus. That's the whole idea, in sound.
- In bar 5 of each chorus, everything out but the pulse.
- "Two clocks on the same wall" rocks evenly on two notes. "Keeping time" goes twice, the second time a step higher.
- No kick in the bridge until the G chord.
- The words stay as written: nothing in them names what the song is about, and that is deliberate.

**The mix priorities.** The voice on top and close, every word clear. The kick and bass lock; the pulse sits bright and a little to one side, so its drift can be heard against the kick. In the one-bar drop, the pulse should feel exposed.

## Production brief

*The song.* A mid-tempo dance-pop song about two strangers on a dance floor who can't get through to each other in any way they try, and find they've been moving in time all along. Abstract by design.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Robyn, "Dancing On My Own."** Released in April 2010 on *Body Talk Pt. 1*; about 117 BPM. Widely heard as an energetic dance song about being lonely.
- **Caribou, "Can't Do Without You."** 2014. Described as having no drop, just a continuous rise; layered and melancholy, it seduces rather than bludgeons.
- **U2, "Where the Streets Have No Name."** 1987. The guitar arpeggio runs through a dotted-eighth delay; the bass and drums enter at 1:10.

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.

## Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** ♩ = 112, 4/4, straight. Click on. The drifting parts are written in dotted eighths against the click: play them on the grid, not loose.
- **Key.** C minor for the intro, verses and pre-choruses; E-flat major for the choruses, post-choruses and bridge. Three flats throughout. The G chord in the bridge (bars 63–64) brings a B natural.
- **Chords per bar.** One chord a bar, as in the form table and the chart.
- **Range.** Voice C4–D5. Synth bass B♭1–A♭3. Pulse G4–D6. Pad G3–B♭4.
- **Tracking.** Kick, bass and pulse first, to the click. Then the pad, then the voice. The one-bar drops (bars 25, 49, 69) are edits: everything but the pulse muted.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice up the middle and close. Kick and bass centred. Pulse slightly right, hats slightly left, so the dotted eighths can be heard against each other. Pad wide.
