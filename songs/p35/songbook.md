# The First Wood

*Words and music: Jason Bryant*

| Style | A Christmas carol for choir (SATB) and organ, for a congregation to sing |

### The form

| Bars | Section | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1–8 | Introduction | The organ plays the refrain |
| 9–32 | Stanza 1 | Everyone in unison on the tune (tenors and basses an octave below), with the organ in four parts |
| 33–56 | Stanza 2 | Four parts with the organ |
| 57–80 | Stanza 3 | Four parts, unaccompanied |
| 81–104 | Stanza 4 | Four parts with full organ, the tune doubled an octave higher; the last chord held to the end of the bar |

Each stanza is 24 bars: four lines of four bars, then the refrain in eight. Every line but the refrain's first starts with a pickup on beat 3.

## What the song is about

Joseph stands at the manger and looks at it the way a carpenter would. It's a feed-trough, cut from green pine, roughly squared, the blade wandering off the line, the edges never smoothed. He has built a hundred beds for other men's sons. He made neither this trough nor this child, and he is holding both.

In the third stanza he promises to teach the boy the trade: how to read the grain, set a mortise true, use the saw and the plane, the work his own father knew. In the fourth he knows what weight a beam will bear and how long a post will stand, and there is timber somewhere he will never square, waiting beyond his reach.

The refrain is a lullaby: "Sleep in the first wood, child." Its second line moves each time: "and I will hold you too," then "it holds you as it can," then "while I have arms to hold," and at last "it will not be the last." The song never names what the last wood is.

**Singing it:**

- **The tune sways in threes.** Most bars put a long note on the downbeat and a short one on beat 3. The stressed syllable takes the long note.
- **Every line but the refrain's first begins on a pickup.** Breathe before it.
- **The one high note is D5, on "first,"** in bar 18 of each stanza. Nothing else goes above C5.
- **The lines close in turn:** lines 1 and 3 on the dominant; line 2 and the refrain's first line on the tonic chord with A on top; line 4 and the refrain's end on F over F.

## For the singers

- **Sopranos:** the tune, all four stanzas. The one D5 is "first," in bar 18. Let the half notes swing like a cradle.
- **Altos:** the tune in stanza 1, then the harmony (B♭3–G4) in stanzas 2–4. The alto line moves mostly by step.
- **Tenors:** the tune an octave down in stanza 1, then the harmony (E3–E4).
- **Basses:** the tune an octave down in stanza 1, then the bass line (F2–G3). It walks by step more often than it leaps; keep it legato.
- **Stanza 3 is unaccompanied.** Hold the pitch through it, and hear the organ's chord for stanza 4 as a return.
- **Words:** every syllable gets one note except a few two-note slurs ("know," "blade," "rough-," "nev-," "I"). The refrain's first line is the same every time; the second line is the one to watch.

## For the organist

- **Introduction:** the refrain (the tune's bars 17–24) in four parts. Its last bar ends on beat 2; beat 3 is the pickup to stanza 1, a C7 over B♭.
- **Stanza 1:** four parts as written under the unison choir. **Stanza 2:** four parts.
- **Stanza 3:** tacet.
- **Stanza 4:** full organ, the tune doubled an octave higher in the right hand; hold the last chord to the end of the bar.
- **Pedal:** double the bass, an octave lower wherever it lies above C3.
- **Registration:** gentle 8′ foundations for stanzas 1–2; add 4′ and the octave for stanza 4.

## Lyric sheet

♩ = 92 in 3/4; F major. Stanzas begin in bars 9, 33, 57 and 81, each with a pickup.

**Introduction** (bars 1–8): the organ plays the refrain.

**1.** (bars 9–32; in unison)

I know this wood: a feed-trough, pine,
cut green and roughly squared;
the blade went wide along the line,
the edges never pared.
*Sleep in the first wood, child,*
*and I will hold you too.*

**2.** (bars 33–56; four parts with the organ)

These hands have joined a hundred beds
for other fathers' sons;
I made no part of trough or child;
I hold them both at once.
*Sleep in the first wood, child;*
*it holds you as it can.*

**3.** (bars 57–80; unaccompanied)

I'll teach you how to read the grain,
to set a mortise true;
your hands will learn the saw, the plane,
the work my father knew.
*Sleep in the first wood, child,*
*while I have arms to hold.*

**4.** (bars 81–104; full organ)

I know what weight a beam will bear,
how long a post will stand;
there's timber I will never square
that waits beyond my hand.
*Sleep in the first wood, child;*
*it will not be the last.*

> **The turn.** The refrain's first line never changes. Its second moves from Joseph's arms to the wood's limits to his own, and in the last stanza it says the trough will not be the last wood to hold the child, without naming the last.

## Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** Tenderness with weight under it. A father's lullaby that knows more than it says. The singers should sound like a congregation, not a concert choir: warm, plain and unhurried. The last stanza should arrive full and certain, and the last refrain line should land quietly in spite of the full organ.

**References**

- **"Joseph Dearest, Joseph Mine" (German, 14th–15th century):** the Nativity sung as a lullaby, with Joseph called to the cradle. Take the rocking.
- **"Away in a Manger," to Kirkpatrick's "Cradle Song" (1895):** a 3/4 cradle-song carol in F. Take the sway and the plainness.
- **"Once in Royal David's City," in the King's College tradition:** the texture grows stanza by stanza toward a full last verse. Take the arc; here it runs unison, four parts, unaccompanied, full.

**Dynamic arc**

| Section (bars) | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction (1–8) | mp | Organ alone |
| Stanza 1 (9–32) | mf | Unison choir, organ in four parts |
| Stanza 2 (33–56) | mf | Four-part choir, organ |
| Stanza 3 (57–80) | p | Choir alone |
| Stanza 4 (81–104) | f, the last line softer | Full organ, the tune an octave higher in the right hand |

**Non-negotiables**

1. The one D5, on "first," and nothing else above C5.
2. Stanza 3 unaccompanied.
3. The refrain's second line clear in every stanza, above all the last: "it will not be the last."
4. The pickups: every line but the refrain's first starts on beat 3.
5. The bass as a line: legato, with its stepwise climbs heard.

**Mix priorities**

1. **The words:** the choir in front of the organ.
2. **The tune:** the sopranos just forward of the other voices.
3. **The bass line:** present, not boomy; the pedal supports it, not covers it.
4. **The room:** a church acoustic, long but clear.
5. **Never cover:** "first" (bar 18) and the last refrain line.

## Production brief

**The song.** A congregational carol for Christmas Eve: Joseph at the manger, seeing it with a carpenter's eye. The trough is rough pine he would have built better. He made neither it nor the child, and he holds them both. He promises to teach the boy his trade, and he knows, without saying, that this is not the last wood that will hold him. Four stanzas of Common Metre and a lullaby refrain whose second line moves each time and turns at the end. Choir (SATB) and organ, in F, ♩ = 92 in 3/4.

**References**

- **"Joseph Dearest, Joseph Mine" (Joseph, lieber Joseph mein).** A carol from 14th- or 15th-century Germany, sung as a lullaby in a mystery play and first printed in 1544. It brings Joseph to the cradle; this song gives him the voice.
- **"Away in a Manger" to William J. Kirkpatrick's "Cradle Song" (1895).** The tune most sung in Britain: 3/4, in F, a simple cradle song. "The First Wood" shares its metre, its key and its sway.
- **"Once in Royal David's City" at King's College, Cambridge.** In A. H. Mann's arrangement, a solo treble sings the first verse, the choir the second, the congregation joins in the third, and the last verse often carries a descant. "The First Wood" builds the same way, through unison, harmony and an unaccompanied stanza to a full last verse.

**Non-negotiables**

1. **The texture plan:** unison, four parts with the organ, unaccompanied, full.
2. **The high point:** D5 once, on "first."
3. **The ending:** "it will not be the last," then the last chord held.

## Session notes

**Tempo and click.** 104 bars, about 3:23, ♩ = 92 in 3/4 throughout. A click is optional; if one is used, drop it for stanza 3 and give the choir a pitch at its pickup.

**Key.** F major throughout.

**Chords (one pass of the tune, for stanza 2)**

| Bars | Line | Chords |
|---|---|---|
| pickup, 1–4 | Line 1 | C7/B♭ · F/A B♭ · F/A E°/G · F/A B♭/D · C |
| pickup, 5–8 | Line 2 | F/A · Dm/F Gm/B♭ · C7 F B♭/D · F |
| pickup, 9–12 | Line 3 | C7/G · F Gm · C/E C7 · F/A Gm · C |
| pickup, 13–16 | Line 4 | F · Gm C7 · C7/B♭ F/A C7 · F |
| 17–20 | Refrain 1 | B♭ F/A · Gm C7/G · F |
| pickup, 21–24 | Refrain 2 | Am/C · Dm C7/E F · Gm C · F |

**Range**

- **Sopranos:** C4–D5 (D5 once). **Altos:** B♭3–G4. **Tenors:** E3–E4. **Basses:** F2–G3.
- **Organ pedal:** the bass, an octave lower above C3.

**Tracking**

1. **Organ first** (introduction, stanzas 1, 2 and 4), to the click.
2. **Choir:** each stanza as a full take. Stanza 3 unaccompanied, in the same room, with the pitch from the organ's last chord of stanza 2.
3. **Pickups:** every stanza begins on beat 3 of the previous stanza's last bar; keep the breath before it.

**Blend and panning**

- **Choir** in its natural layout, sopranos slightly left, basses slightly right, centred overall; **organ** behind and wide.
- **The room:** let the reverb carry stanza 3; don't add artificial reverb to it.
- **The end:** hold the last chord to the end of the bar, then let the room ring.

**About the demo.** The demo is a reference for the tune, the harmony and the form. Its voices are General MIDI "choir aahs" and cannot sing the words. The words are in the MIDI files as lyric events and in every score.
