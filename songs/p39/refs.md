- **Randy Newman, "Political Science" (1972).** The model for the chorus. Newman sings in the voice of the man he is mocking and lets him state his intent plainly, brazen and cheerful, a barbed message dressed in harmless music. The jingle here works the same way: the candidates sing their promise sweetly and the words give them away. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_Science_(song)); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/the-randy-newman-lyric-that-satirized-a-rock))
- **Creedence Clearwater Revival, "Fortunate Son" (1969).** The anger: a song about a privileged political and moneyed class that keeps itself safe while ordinary people pay, short, loud and direct. Take: the class grievance aimed at a class, not a party. ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/articles/the-critique-of-class-behind-fortunate-son-by-creedence-clearwater-revival/); [Louder](https://www.loudersound.com/music/tracks-singles/creedence-clearwater-revival-fortunate-son))
- **ZZ Top, "La Grange" (1973).** The groove: a traditional boogie shuffle of the kind John Lee Hooker used in "Boogie Chillen'", the Texas shuffle Billy Gibbons says the band has played at every show since. Take: the verses' boogie guitar and swung eighths, dry and greasy. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grange_(song)); [Louder](https://www.loudersound.com/features/zz-top-la-grange))
