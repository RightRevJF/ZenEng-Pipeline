## At a glance

*Fixed by Fall* is a sneering rock shuffle for one gritty baritone. An all-seeing narrator cuts between the rooms where both sides of the political class take the same money: the same fundraiser on consecutive nights, a minute of rage on the floor and then the cough drops passed along, the stock trade at 9:31, the bill written down the street, the bridge study paid to both families. The chorus is their campaign promise sung back as a bright jingle ("Vote for me, I'll fix it all"), with 'fix' turning from a promise to a rigged deal. The bridge follows them out of office to one cake, one board, one lake, one firm. In the last chorus the two candidates are the same singer on two tracks: they start apart and end in unison, "Vote for us", and the punchline lands on the listener. No party, colour, side or name is ever said.

**How to play it.** Two feels. The verses (and the intro, bridge, solo and outro) are a dry shuffle: swung eighths, kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, the guitar playing the boogie (root-fifth, root-sixth), the bass walking root, root, fifth, fifth, sixth, sixth, fifth, root, the piano on the 'and's. Every chorus snaps into a straight rally march: kick on every beat, a marching snare, straight eighths on the ride and the guitar, piano in quarters, a crash on 1. It should sound like the band switched into a campaign jingle. The bridge pulls back; the solo goes back to the shuffle; the last chorus is the fullest, with a tambourine and the duet; the outro is the shuffle again, ending on one hit in bar 90.

## For each player

**Drums.** Shuffle sections: kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, swung hats (the ride in the solo); a crash on bar 1. Choruses: straight, kick on every beat, the snare on 2 and 4 with a pickup on the 'and' of 4, straight eighths on the ride, a crash on each chorus's first bar; the last chorus adds a tambourine in eighths and a crash every four bars. A fill in the last two beats of every section (bars 4, 20, 28, 44, 52, 60, 68, 86). Bar 90: one hit.

**Bass.** In at the verse. Every bar starts on the root. Shuffle sections: the boogie, root, root, fifth, fifth, sixth, sixth, fifth, root, swung. Choruses: straight eighths (root, fifth, octave). Bridge: half notes, root and fifth. Roots E2–D3. Bar 90: one hit.

**Rhythm guitar.** The boogie in the shuffle sections (root-fifth, root-sixth, swung eighths); open power chords in straight eighths in the choruses; held chords in the bridge and under the solo. Bar 90: one E chord, let it ring.

**Piano.** Barroom: triads on the 'and's in the verses and outro (swung), quarter-note chords in the choruses like a rally piano, held chords in the bridge, out for the solo.

**Lead guitar.** The intro tune is a one-bar figure of four notes (E4 G♯4 C♯4 G♯4 over E), played in the four intro bars and again in the outro. Fills in the gaps after 'The bill was written down the street' (verse 2); 'once by his kid, and once by hers' (verse 2) (bars 30, 36); never over a sung word. The solo (bars 61–68) states the tune, answers it two steps higher, sings a line over the chorus changes in the shuffle and ends on a chord tone.

**Singer.** Range A2–E4. The verses sit low (A2–G♯3): sneer, lean on the off-beats, keep it dry. The chorus is bright and fake, a jingle; by chorus 2 let it go flat and bored. The E4 comes four times, always on 'Vote' over E: bar 21, beat 1; bar 45, beat 1; bar 69, beat 1; bar 77, beat 1. The next highest note is D4. Double-track chorus 2. For the last chorus, record two passes as two candidates (pass one sings line 1, pass two line 2, then a third under on lines 3–4), and sing the unison lines as nearly identical as you can: the point is that it's the same man.

**Arrangement.** Shuffle verses, march choruses: the switch is the joke, so make it clean. The bridge pulls back; the solo pushes in the shuffle; the last chorus is the fullest (tambourine, crashes every four bars, the duet), then the tag, then the shuffle out to one hit.

### Production brief

**The song.** An all-seeing narrator shows the political class on both sides as one business: the same donors, the same staged rage, the same trade, the same families paid, the same retirement. The chorus is their own jingle, sung back. It must not favour either side: the lyric names no party, colour, person or issue, and the production shouldn't either (no campaign samples of real people, no party colours in any artwork).

**References.**

- **Randy Newman, "Political Science" (1972).** The model for the chorus. Newman sings in the voice of the man he is mocking and lets him state his intent plainly, brazen and cheerful, a barbed message dressed in harmless music. The jingle here works the same way: the candidates sing their promise sweetly and the words give them away. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_Science_(song)); [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/the-randy-newman-lyric-that-satirized-a-rock))
- **Creedence Clearwater Revival, "Fortunate Son" (1969).** The anger: a song about a privileged political and moneyed class that keeps itself safe while ordinary people pay, short, loud and direct. Take: the class grievance aimed at a class, not a party. ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/articles/the-critique-of-class-behind-fortunate-son-by-creedence-clearwater-revival/); [Louder](https://www.loudersound.com/music/tracks-singles/creedence-clearwater-revival-fortunate-son))
- **ZZ Top, "La Grange" (1973).** The groove: a traditional boogie shuffle of the kind John Lee Hooker used in "Boogie Chillen'", the Texas shuffle Billy Gibbons says the band has played at every show since. Take: the verses' boogie guitar and swung eighths, dry and greasy. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grange_(song)); [Louder](https://www.loudersound.com/features/zz-top-la-grange))

**Non-negotiables.** The shuffle in the verses and the straight march in the choruses, switched cleanly. The E4 on 'VOTE', beat 1 over E, four times and nowhere else. The same singer on both tracks of the duet, ending in unison. The tag 'Fixed by fall' falling to E. One hit on bar 90. The voice in front (about 6 dB over the band in the demo).

### Session notes

- **Tempo and click:** ♩ = 108 throughout; 3:20 for 90 bars. Click in quarters; the shuffle sections swung (a triplet feel), the choruses straight. Count in one bar.
- **Key:** E major; no modulation.
- **Chords:** one per bar, root position (the form table lists every bar).
- **Range:** voice A2–E4; bass E2–D3 with octaves above.

- **Tracking order:** drums and bass to the click (the feel switch locked first), the boogie guitar (double it), piano, lead guitar, then the lead vocal, chorus 2's double, and the last chorus's second candidate.

- **Blend and panning:** kick, snare and bass centre; the boogie guitars wide; piano right; lead guitar slightly right. Lead vocal just left of centre; the second track just right, so the duet opens out and closes to the middle in the unison.

- **Separate passes:** chorus 2's double and the last chorus's second candidate are passes of their own.
