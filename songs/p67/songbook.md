# At a glance

**What it's about.** A night that goes too far out and a man who barely gets home. A voice like gravel and honey calls him down into the basement of the attic of his mind; he answers to a name that isn't his and carries his spark down there. He sits in with Jehovah's band across the sands of time, dancing barefoot on red dirt under the moon, until a county man in a sweat-stained cap laughs the drummer quiet, pinches out the spark and takes even the sound of his daughter's name. He comes to in a diner booth with cold coffee and no idea of the town. Then his daughter calls him by his own name, and the band he went looking for is in his kitchen: her bare feet keep the beat, she sings the angels' line over the frying pan, and he thanks God it followed him home.

**How it goes.** A Grateful Dead–style jam in E major at ♩ = 112, a loping 4/4 with a light swing, for one baritone. Two verses and two choruses, a bridge where everything goes out, a long jam that starts from silence and builds back, a third verse that comes home, and a last chorus that turns the cosmic into the kitchen.

**The title line.** "I been sitting in with Jehovah's band" opens every chorus and comes back as its fifth line. It starts on E3, reaches F♯3 on "been" and ends on C♯3 on "band". The outro sings the title three times.

# For each player

**Voice.** A baritone, C♯3 to D♯4. Tell it like it happened to you. Wonder in the choruses; the bridge quiet and frightened; verse 3 empty at first, then relieved; the last chorus thankful.

**Organ** (E3–B4). Intro: quarter notes; Chorus 1: steady eighths; Verse 2: quarter notes; Chorus 2: steady eighths; Jam: quarter notes; Chorus 3: steady eighths; Ending: quarter notes. Every voicing sits under the sung line. It is the first sound of the jam, alone.

**Rhythm guitar** (E2–F♯4). Intro: quarter notes; Verse 1: steady eighths; Jam: quarter notes; Verse 3: steady eighths. Kept under the sung line: open shapes (root, fifth, octave) where the voice is low.

**Lead guitar** (E3–E5). plays the chorus’s first line twice in the intro; short answers in the voice’s rests (bars 16, 20, 26, 36, 40, 54, 58, 110, 118, 122, 126); the solo from bar 75 of the jam to its end.

**Bass** (E1–B2). the root at every change, the line walking between chords, notes on the kicks. In the jam from bar 63.

**Drums**. snare on 2 and 4, hat eighths (ride in the last chorus); kick on 1, the and of 2, and 3, kick on every beat; a crash on each section’s first bar, a fill on its last. In the jam from bar 67.

# Arrangement

One curve carries the song: each chorus louder than its verse, the second chorus above the first, the last chorus the top, the outro a step down. The bridge keeps the band going while the chords lose E. Then the jam starts from nothing, one player at a time, and builds to where verse 3 comes in fresh. The organ is the first sound and the lead guitar the last to enter.

## Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** Wonder that tips into fear, then relief that feels like grace. The listener should feel the trip without ever being told what it was, and the kitchen at the end should feel bigger than the sands of time.

**The three references.**

- **"Eyes of the World"** (1973): a song that opens into a jam. Take the way the band breathes before it stretches out.
- **"Scarlet Begonias"** (1974): the loping groove and the story in particular pictures. Take the swing and the room around the voice.
- **"Uncle John's Band"** (1970): a chorus a room can sing. Take the plain, singable title line.

**The dynamic arc.**

**The non-negotiables.** The title line the same in every chorus. The bridge's last line, then silence. The jam built one player at a time from bar 59. Verse 3 coming in fresh at bar 91. The light swing.

**The mix priorities.** The voice forward and clear over the band in every chorus and on every title line. Organ left, rhythm guitar right, lead guitar right of centre; in the jam the lead comes forward as it enters. Nothing sits a second away from the sung note.

## Production brief

*The song.* A man's night out past the edge of his mind and his way back home, told in pictures: a basement voice, a cosmic band, a county man who puts the spark out, a diner at dawn, a daughter's voice.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Grateful Dead, "Eyes of the World."** Robert Hunter and Jerry Garcia; on *Wake of the Flood* (Grateful Dead Records, October 15, 1973), and its second single. A song built to open into a long jam.
- **Grateful Dead, "Scarlet Begonias."** Hunter and Garcia; on *From the Mars Hotel* (June 27, 1974). A loping groove, a story told in particular images, and an instrumental section that stretches out at the end.
- **Grateful Dead, "Uncle John's Band."** Hunter and Garcia; on *Workingman's Dead* (June 14, 1970); the single, an edit, reached number 69 on the Hot 100. A singable chorus that a whole room can join.

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.

## Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** ♩ = 112 throughout, 4/4, a light swing (eighths long-short, about 3 to 2).
- **Key.** E major, with D and Bm borrowed.
- **Chords per bar.** As in the section tables; a bar with two chords splits two beats each.
- **Range.** Voice C♯3–D♯4.
- **Tracking.** Drums, bass, organ and rhythm guitar together for the groove; the jam tracked live as a band, in the order the players enter; the lead guitar's fills after; the voice last.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice centre; organ left, rhythm guitar right, lead guitar right of centre; bass and kick centre.
- **The silence.** Bar 59: only the organ, quiet.
