# Your Yard Man

*Songbook and production notes · Words and music: Jason Bryant*

## At a glance

**What it's about.** A man who mows other people's yards for a living and wouldn't trade it. Up at a quarter past pitch black, coffee by the jug, half-ton and trailer, done by three, and no boss with a stopwatch. His daddy gave the mill thirty-one years and got a watch, a sheet cake and a heart that wouldn't wait; he spent his last summers watching his son cut his grass. So the son takes the summers his daddy never got to. On Sunday it's church, then the lake, and nobody can call him back.

**How it goes.** A country story song with a chorus built for a crowd to sing back, a neotraditional two-step in A major at ♩ = 120, for one baritone. Three verses, each followed by a chorus. The chorus's frame stays the same, while its middle lines change every time: Holland, then the gold watch his daddy got instead of summers, then Paris.

**The hook.** Every chorus opens with "I'm your yard man", which jumps A3, C♯4 to E4 on "yard", the chorus's top note. It closes with the same call and its answer, "But I'm my own man", on the same rhythm: E4, C♯4, B3, C♯4, A3, home on "man". A harmony voice joins both lines, like the crowd singing back.

**Form (102 bars, about 3:24).** Verse lines take two bars; in the chorus the frame lines take one bar each and the four middle lines two.

**The singer.** A baritone, B2 to E4. The verses sit low, mostly around E3, close to speech; the choruses sit higher, around A3, and E4 comes only in the hook.

## For each player

**Voice.** A baritone, B2 to E4. Plain and dry in verse 1, a working man telling you his day. Verse 2 is his daddy: quieter, no smile in it. Verse 3 is Sunday: easy. The choruses are proud; the call and answer belong to everybody.

**Harmony voice** (C♯4–A4). A third above him on the call and the answer in every chorus and the tag, and on chorus 3's lines 2–4. The crowd singing back; never louder than him.

**Acoustic guitar** (E2–B4). Steel-string. Boom-chick in the intro and verses: the root on 1, the fifth on 3, the chord on 2 and 4 with an upstroke after. Straight eighths in the choruses and the tag. The break's last bar (bar 68) is yours alone.

**Telecaster** (G♯3–D5). Short answers in the gaps, never under a sung word: three or four eighths down the major pentatonic, a double-stop on the first note. The choruses and the second half of verse 3.

**Fiddle** (D4–G♯5). The call and the answer in the intro and the break, the answer home in the ending. Long notes under chorus 3 and the tag.

**Pedal steel** (C♯4–C♯5). Answers in verse 1's gaps with a bend from the note above; long held notes under verse 2, quietly; swells under the choruses; the reply in the break.

**Piano** (A3–B4). Offbeat chords on 2 and 4 from chorus 2 on; plain half-note chords for the church pew at the start of verse 3; the last A rings.

**Bass** (E1–E3). A two-step: on 1 and 3 with the kick, roots and fifths with walk-ups into the changes. Root–five in the intro, the break and the ending. Out for the break's last bar and the tag's call.

**Drums.** A two-step with sticks: kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, hat in eighths.

- *Intro, verses 1 and 2:* cross-stick (verse 2 softer).
- *Choruses 1–2:* snare 2 and 4, crash on the first bar.
- *Break:* out in bar 68.
- *Verse 3:* cross-stick to bar 76, sticks after.
- *Chorus 3:* ride, a crash on every line.
- *Tag:* one kick and crash under the call, then back in.
- *Ending:* kick and crash on the last A.

## Arrangement

The band follows his life. Verse 1 is the work: boom-chick and a little steel. The choruses open up, each one bigger. Verse 2, his daddy, pulls back to cross-stick and held steel, and chorus 2 carries that weight into its middle lines. The break lets the air out, down to one guitar, and Sunday comes in fresh on plain piano chords before the lake brings the band back. Chorus 3 is the biggest; the tag stops the band so everyone can shout the call, then lands the answer together. The fiddle carries the call and answer at the start, the middle and the end.

### Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** Pride without bragging, and a grief he doesn't dwell on. A man who chose his life because he watched what the other one cost his daddy. Keep it light and sunny on top; let verse 2 and the gold-watch chorus land quietly, so the last "I'm my own man" sounds earned.

**The three references.**

- **Alan Jackson, "Little Man"** (1999): a working-class song about what was lost. Take the plain, warm delivery and the respect in it.
- **Zach Top, "I Never Lie"** (2024): today's traditional country with the steel out front. Take the steel's place and the dry humor sung straight.
- **George Strait, "Check Yes or No"** (1995): the steady two-step and a story told across verses.

**The dynamic arc.**

|--------|-----|----|----------------------------|
| Intro, verse 1 | 1–20 | mp | fiddle call and answer; boom-chick, cross-stick, steel |
| Chorus 1 | 21–34 | mf | snare 2 and 4, crash; steel, Telecaster; harmony on the hook |
| Verse 2 | 35–50 | p | his daddy: cross-stick, held steel |
| Chorus 2 | 51–64 | mf+ | piano in; the gold-watch lines |
| Turnaround (break) | 65–68 | mf → p | fiddle call and answer, steel reply; bar 68 the acoustic alone |
| Verse 3 | 69–84 | mp → mf | the pew on piano; the lake brings the sticks |
| Chorus 3 | 85–98 | f | ride, crashes, fiddle long notes, more harmony |
| Tag, ending | 99–102 | f | the call over a stop; the answer together; the last A rings |

**The non-negotiables.** The call jumping to E4 on "yard" and the answer coming home on A3, the same in every chorus. Verse 2 pulled back. The break's last bar on one guitar. The tag's call over a stop. The two-step, never a rock backbeat.

**The mix priorities.** His voice forward and dry, every word clear; the harmony on the hook wide and a step behind him, a crowd not a duet. Steel and fiddle wide and under the vocal, never over a sung word. In verse 2, the steel low and long.

### Production brief

*The song.* A country story song about a man who mows yards for a living, why he chose it (his daddy's thirty-one years at the mill), and the freedom of a Sunday nobody can take from him.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Alan Jackson, "Little Man."** Written by Alan Jackson, produced by Keith Stegall; released in May 1999 as the fourth and last single from *High Mileage*; number three on the Billboard country chart. A song for the family-owned shops pushed out of their towns.
- **Zach Top, "I Never Lie."** From *Cold Beer & Country Music* (Leo33, 2024), produced by Carson Chamberlain; neotraditional country with a comic lyric, compared to George Strait's "Ocean Front Property." Top called it the countriest song on the record, with steel guitar all over it.
- **George Strait, "Check Yes or No."** Written by Danny Wells and Dana Hunt Black; produced by Tony Brown and George Strait; released in September 1995 as the lead single from *Strait Out of the Box*; a story told across verses, number one on the Billboard country chart for four weeks.

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.

### Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** ♩ = 120 throughout, 4/4, straight (not swung). A two-step feel.
- **Key.** A major. Every note in the voice is in the scale.
- **Chords per bar.** As in the section tables; a bar with two chords splits two beats each.
- **Range.** Voice B2–E4; harmony C♯4–A4.
- **Tracking.** Bass, drums and acoustic together for the feel; then steel, fiddle, Telecaster and piano; the voice; the harmony as its own pass (double it for the crowd if you like). The fiddle's call and answer must match the voice's note for note.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice centre; harmony split wide; acoustic left of centre, Telecaster right; fiddle left, steel right; piano wide left, low.
- **The stops.** Bar 68: the acoustic alone. Bar 99: one hit, then voices only for the call.
