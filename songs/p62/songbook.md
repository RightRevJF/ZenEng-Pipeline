# At a glance

**What it's about.** Nora's husband Bernard has died in the sitting room. She keeps his cup, his chair and the book he left face down, and she moves her own chair to the north wall, where the plaster is warm the way only a south wall should be. She talks to him through it. One morning the room next door comes as close as a breath, and she understands what she had been too close to see: a wall with a door in it is not the same as a wall without one. The song is from the writer's parable "The Room Next Door" in *Wild Fruit*.

**How it goes.** An intimate folk ballad in 6/8, D major, at a dotted quarter of 54: count it in two, gently rocking. A fingerpicked acoustic guitar carries it; soft piano and an acoustic bass come in at the first chorus, a cello at the second. The hook, "there's a door in the wall," lifts to the song's top note, D5, on "door" and leaves "wall" open. The bridge drops to piano and cello alone. At the very end the hook is sung twice, and the second time it comes home.

**Form (84 bars, about 3:07).** Every sung line takes two bars of 6/8, twelve eighth notes, which is about one syllable to an eighth.

**The singer.** An alto, B3 to D5. The verses sit low (around E4 on average); the choruses sit about 3 semitones higher (around G4). D5 comes only on "door."

# For each player

**Voice.** An alto, B3 to D5. Sing it as Nora would talk to Bernard through the wall: close, unhurried, not performed. Let the 6/8 rock you; the stressed words fall on the two beats. Open up on "door" and leave "wall" unfinished in choruses 1 and 2. In the bridge come right down until "and you were there." Save the settled, final "wall" for the very end.

**Harmony vocals.** Two parts, one above and one below, on the hook in every chorus and on every line of the final chorus (high F4–F♯5, low A3–B4). Blend under the lead.

**Acoustic guitar** (E2–F♯4). Steel-string, fingerpicked in six eighths throughout: the bass note on the first and fourth, the chord between. Out in the bridge; back for verse 4.

**Piano** (D3–B4). On the two beats of each bar in the choruses, soft; answers at the line ends in verse 2; held chords in the bridge; high and quiet in verse 4.

**Cello** (D2–F♯4). Long notes from chorus 2; the hook's tune in the interlude; held notes in verse 3; with the piano in the bridge; a countermelody of long notes in the final chorus; the last D.

**Acoustic bass** (A1–D3). In at chorus 1: the root on the first beat, the fifth or a step toward the next chord on the second. Out in the bridge; back for verse 4.

**Drums.** None.

# Arrangement

The song is a woman alone in a quiet house, so it starts with one guitar and never gets loud. Each chorus adds one voice: piano and bass, then the cello. The interlude gives the hook to the cello, as if the house were answering her. The bridge takes the floor away (no guitar, no bass) so the morning arrives in near silence. The final chorus is the fullest, and the one chord from outside the key, B♭maj7, sits under the line where the door opens. The last "wall" is the only one that comes home.

## Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** Grief that has found its footing. Not despair, not consolation handed down, but a widow's quiet certainty that the wall has a door in it. Warm, close, unforced.

**The three references.**

- **Leonard Cohen, "Hallelujah"** (*Various Positions*, 1984): a hymn-like song in a rolling compound meter. Take the slow rock of the meter and the hush.
- **Alison Krauss, "Ghost in This House"** (*Forget About It*, 1999; written by Hugh Prestwood): a woman alone in a house after a loss, sung close. Take the closeness of the voice.
- **Steve Wariner, "Holes in the Floor of Heaven"** (*Burnin' the Roadhouse Down*, 1998): a ballad about the dead still near. Take the plain, unembarrassed tenderness.

**The dynamic arc.**

| Section | Bars | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro, verse 1 | 1–12 | pp | one guitar and the voice |
| Chorus 1 | 13–22 | p | piano and bass in; harmony on the hook |
| Verse 2 | 23–30 | p | piano answers |
| Chorus 2 | 31–40 | mp | the cello joins |
| Interlude | 41–44 | mp | the cello sings the hook |
| Verse 3 | 45–52 | p | the cello held |
| Bridge | 53–60 | pp | guitar and bass out; piano and cello |
| Verse 4 | 61–68 | p | the guitar back |
| Final chorus | 69–80 | mp+ | the fullest; harmony on every line; B♭maj7; the hook home |
| Outro | 81–84 | pp | guitar and cello, slowing |

**The non-negotiables.** The 6/8 rock, felt in two. D5 only on "door." "Wall" open in choruses 1 and 2, home on D4 only at the end. The bridge without guitar or bass. B♭maj7 under "and some morning it opens." Nothing louder than mezzo-forte.

**The mix priorities.** The voice close and dry enough to hear the breath. The guitar's picking at the centre, slightly left; the cello right; the piano soft and behind. In the bridge, room sound around the piano and cello.

## Production brief

*The song.* A widow talks to her husband through the north wall of the sitting room, and comes to know the wall has a door in it.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Leonard Cohen, "Hallelujah."** From *Various Positions* (1984), recorded June 1984, produced by John Lissauer; the original is in 12/8, a rolling triple meter of the kind found in gospel and folk.
- **Alison Krauss, "Ghost in This House."** Written by Hugh Prestwood and first a 1990 single for Shenandoah; Krauss's version is on *Forget About It* (Rounder, August 3, 1999), 4:04.
- **Steve Wariner, "Holes in the Floor of Heaven."** From *Burnin' the Roadhouse Down* (Capitol Nashville), released as a single on March 24, 1998; written by Steve Wariner and Billy Kirsch; Song of the Year from the CMA and the ACM in 1998.

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.

## Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** Dotted quarter = 54 (quarter = 81), 6/8 throughout; click on the dotted quarters, two a bar. The outro slows a little to its last chord: follow the guitar, no click.
- **Key.** D major. The voice stays in the scale; the B♭maj7 and Cadd9 are in the band only.
- **Chords per bar.** One chord a bar, as in the section tables.
- **Range.** Voice B3–D5; harmony as above.
- **Tracking.** Guitar and a guide vocal to the click first; then bass and piano; then cello; the lead vocal; the harmonies with the finished lead in the headphones.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice centre; guitar slightly left; cello right; piano right of centre; bass centre; harmonies slightly left and right.
- **The bridge.** Guitar and bass silent for all eight bars.
