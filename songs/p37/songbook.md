## At a glance

*Windows Down* is a heartland rock song for a male baritone. A man walks out of his plant on its last day after twenty-six years: the time clock that always ran four minutes fast, her picture peeled off his locker door, the gate's squeal. He calls his wife from the lot with oil on his thumb, and she doesn't ask what comes next. He drives home with the windows down to air the cutting oil out of his clothes, in November, with the heater on. The job ended; he didn't.

**How to play it.** Straight eighths everywhere: the bass drives eighths on the root, the rhythm guitar plays power chords in eighths (palm-muted in the verses, open from the pre-chorus on), the drums keep kick on 1 and 3 with the 'and' of 3 and the snare on 2 and 4. The intro is the lead guitar's hook alone for four bars, then the band. Every section is a multiple of four bars and every sung line two bars. Each chorus is the 8-bar chorus sung twice; its fourth line opens on D, where the singer hits his top note, and lands on A. The bridge drops to half-time and builds; the solo takes the chorus changes for 16 bars; the last chorus is the fullest; the outro rides the chorus changes with the hook and ends on one hit in bar 112.

## For each player

**Drums.** Bars 1–4 tacet; a crash and in at bar 5. Kick on 1, 3 and the 'and' of 3; snare on 2 and 4; eighth hats in the verses and pre-choruses, eighths on the ride in the choruses. A tom fill in the last two beats of each pre-chorus (bars 20 and 48). Bridge (bars 65–72): half-time, kick on 1 and snare on 3, through bar 70, then a snare build over bars 71–72. Solo (bars 73–88): hats for eight bars, the ride from bar 81. Last chorus: a crash every four bars and the open hat on every 'and'. Bar 112: one hit with the crash.

**Bass.** From bar 5, driving eighths on the root; in the choruses, the solo and the outro, the octave on the 'and' of 4. Bridge bars 65–70: half notes. The roots run E2–D3. Bar 112: one hit, root and octave.

**Rhythm guitar.** Power chords (root, fifth, octave) in eighths from bar 5: palm-muted in the intro and verses, open from each pre-chorus through the chorus, the solo and the outro. Held chords for bridge bars 65–70.

**Organ.** Held triads in the pre-choruses, the choruses and the bridge; in from bar 81 for the second half of the solo; the outro. Voiced higher in the last chorus.

**Lead guitar.** A one-bar hook of its own, four notes (A4 E5 B4 A4 over A): the 'and' of 1, beat 2, its 'and', and the 'and' of 3, a jump up a fifth and back down. It plays alone for the first four bars, then over the band to bar 8; in the holds after chorus lines where two beats or more are free (it moves with each bar's chord and never plays over a sung word); and in outro bars 105–111. The solo (bars 73–88) states the hook, answers it a third higher, sings a line over the chorus changes that climbs to the middle and comes back, and ends with the hook landing on A.

**Singer.** Range D3–F♯4; most of the song lives between E3 and D4, close and conversational in the verses. Every line ends on a held note (a quarter, a dotted quarter or a half) with a breath after it. The F♯4 comes five times, always on 'HOL-lering' over D: bar 27, beat 1; bar 35, beat 1; bar 55, beat 1; bar 63, beat 1; bar 95, beat 1. Every other note is E4 or lower. In the last line, hold the first 'home' (a half note) before the second 'I'm almost home'.

**Arrangement.** The guitar's hook alone, then the band. Verse 1 tight and muted; each pre-chorus opens up and fills into the chorus; each chorus rides. The bridge drops to half-time and builds to the solo; the solo builds again to the last chorus, the fullest section. Outro: the chorus changes with the hook, one final hit on A.

### Production brief

**The song.** A man's last shift at a plant that's closing, and the drive home to his wife. The verses are the small, exact things of the last day; the chorus is the drive, loud and a little reckless, defiant without being angry. It is not a protest song and it names no one. The bridge is the marriage: they had his hands for twenty-six years, she had him the whole way. The last chorus lets him keep a little of the smell.

**References.**

- John Mellencamp, "Small Town" (1985), about 123 BPM: the tempo, the straight-eighth drive and a plain, particular lyric about where a man is from.
- Tom Petty, "I Won't Back Down" (1989), about 114 BPM: defiance said plainly, with no anger in it.
- Bruce Springsteen, "My Hometown" (1984), a slow, synthesizer-based heartland song: a mill closing told through one family, with no one blamed by name.

**Non-negotiables.** Straight eighths on the bass and the rhythm guitar. The guitar's hook opens the song alone. The F♯4 on 'HOL-lering' over D, five times, and nowhere else. The bridge drops to half-time and builds. The hook never plays over a sung word. The last line holds the first 'home' and ends on A; one hit in bar 112. The voice in front (it reads about 6 dB over the band in the demo).

### Session notes

- **Tempo and click:** ♩ = 122 throughout; 3:40 for 112 bars. Count in one bar; the guitar starts alone.
- **Key:** A major; no modulation.
- **Chords:** one per bar, root position (the form table lists every bar).
- **Range:** voice D3–F♯4; bass E2–D3 with octaves above.

- **Tracking order:** drums and bass to the click, then the rhythm guitar (double it, left and right, if the room allows), organ, the lead guitar's hook and solo, then the lead vocal.

- **Blend and panning:** kick, snare and bass centre; rhythm guitars wide; organ right of centre; lead guitar slightly right; lead vocal centre and on top (at least 3 dB over the band).
