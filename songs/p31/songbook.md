# Nothing On

*Words and music: Jason Bryant*

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Style | Disco-funk pop; one female lead, a backing voice, and a band (kit and percussion, bass, two guitars, Rhodes, horns) |
| Tempo | ♩ = 116 throughout |
| Metre | 4/4, straight sixteenths |
| Length | 76 bars, about 2:37 |
| Key | F major signature, but the song never lands on F until the bridge. Verses loop Gm7–Am7; choruses B♭maj7–Am7 / Gm7–C. The song ends on B♭maj7 |
| Voice | Female: A3–C5, D5 once (“off” in the last chorus) |
| Feel | Sly and in control. A tight sixteenth groove with a lot of air in it. At the top of every chorus the band cuts out, as if the power went, and she sings the title alone |

## What the song is about

A power cut, and what she decides to do when the power comes back.

At ten-fifteen the whole street blinks out. The fridge stops, the fan winds down, her phone is on nine percent and she lets it die. The only thing glowing is him. Everybody else is waiting for the lights to come back; she hopes they never do. They find a candle from his birthday, he pulls her down to the floor and tells her things he'd never say with the lights on. The candle gives out. She can hear him smile. She says *say my name till I find where you are*.

At two in the morning the house wakes up. The fridge clears its throat, the windows on the street open one eye, the microwave blinks like it caught them. Her hand finds the switch. The last chorus says what she did: her phone woke up and she let it ring; the lights came back and she turned them off.

The song never says what happens in the dark. Everything is told through the outage: “nothing on” means the TV, the lights and the Wi-Fi, and it means the other thing too. It should be fun and unmistakable, and never crude.

**Playing it:**

- **The home chord is the power.** F is played only in the bridge, when the lights come back. Everywhere else the loop never lands. Don't resolve it, ever, including the last chord.
- **The cut is the hook.** At the end of each pre-chorus everything bends down (a tape stop) and goes silent on beat 3. The chorus's first bar is hers alone. The band comes back on bar 2 with a crash. Commit to the silence.
- **Off the beat.** Every “nothing on” starts on the “and” of beat 1. The downbeat belongs to the silence.

## The song, section by section

Chords are shown beside each line, two bars a line. Melody notes are written at the pitch they sound.

### Intro · bars 1–4

Gm7 · Am7 · Gm7 · Am7. The clean guitar plays the riff, which is the chorus's “nothing on, nothing on” tune with sixteenth fills, over sixteenth hats alone. A snare pickup leads to bar 3, where the kit, bass, muted guitar and Rhodes come in.

### Verse 1 · bars 5–12

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Gm7 · Am7 | ten-fifteen and the whole street blinks |
| Gm7 · Am7 | the fridge goes quiet and the fan spins down |
| Gm7 · Am7 | my phone's on nine and I'm letting it die |
| Gm7 · Am7 | the only thing glowing is you |

Low and close to speech, between C4 and G4. “Ten-fif-teen” sits on D4 and the line rises to G4 on “street” before landing on E4 on “blinks” (bar 6). “Die” (bar 10) and “you” (bar 12) settle on C4, held, each on the downbeat of an Am7 bar.

### Pre-chorus · bars 13–16

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| B♭maj7 · C | the air went off and the room got warm |
| B♭maj7 · C | so quiet I can hear you breathe |

The melody climbs: F4 through the first line, then A4 on “hear”, and B♭4 on “breathe” on beat 4 of bar 15, held over C. On beat 3 of bar 16 the band bends down and stops. She is holding “breathe” when the room goes silent.

### Chorus 1 · bars 17–24

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| B♭maj7 · Am7 | nothing on, nothing on |
| Gm7 · C | everybody's waiting on the lights |
| B♭maj7 · Am7 | I hope they never come back on |
| Gm7 · C | nothing on, nothing on |

Bar 17 is silent except for her. “NOTH” on A4 on the “and” of 1, “ing” G4, “ON” leaps up to C5 on the “and” of 2 and holds across beat 3. Bar 18, the band crashes in: “NOTH-ing” C5–A4, “ON” falls to E4 on beat 3. “Everybody's waiting” drops low (C4–E4) and lifts to G4 on “lights” (bar 20). “Come back on” climbs through A4 and ends on G4 (bar 22). The last line repeats the title, but closes C5, B♭4, G4 over C (bar 24). It stays open and never comes home.

### Post-chorus · bars 25–28

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Gm7 · Am7 | nothing on but the moon |
| Gm7 · Am7 | (nothing on) nothing on but you |

The hook after the hook. “On” on C5, then down by step: B♭4 “but”, A4 “the”, G4 “moon” on beat 4 of bar 25, held. In bar 27 the backing voice sings “(nothing on)” a third under. She answers on the “and” of 3 and lands “you” on A4 (bar 28).

### Verse 2 · bars 29–36

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|

| Gm7 · Am7 | you told me things you'd never say with the lights on |

Verse 1's tune with more words. The first line is quick and low on D4 and C4, rising to G4 on “kitchen”. “Floor” lands on C4 (bar 32). The third line climbs to A4 on “never” and “say”, then drops to C4 on “lights on”. The last line dips to B♭3 on “you” before “see” (bar 35) and settles on C4 (bar 36). The riff guitar answers in bar 34, the one place a line leaves it a bar.

Pre-chorus 1's shape, lower: “smile” on D4 (bar 38), “are” on G4 on beat 2 of bar 40. The horns swell under it. The same tape stop on beat 3 of bar 40.

| Chords | Line |

### Post-chorus · bars 49–52

As before.

### Bridge · bars 53–60

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| F · F | two a.m. and the fridge clears its throat |
| Am · B♭ | every window on the street opens one eye |
| F · B♭ | the microwave's blinking like it caught us |
| Gm7 · C | my hand finds the switch and I hold my breath |

The power comes back. Bar 53 is the first F chord in the song, under a low hum and Rhodes alone. The bass joins at bar 55, kick and hats at bar 57, and the guitar and a snare build at bar 59. The melody starts at the bottom of the range (A3 to D4, “throat” on C4 in bar 54) and rises: F4 and G4 for “microwave's”, A4 on “switch” and “hold”, then C5 on “breath” on the downbeat of bar 60. The whole band hits that downbeat and stops, and she holds “breath” over silence.

### Chorus 3 · bars 61–68

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| B♭maj7 · Am7 | nothing on, nothing on |
| Gm7 · C | my phone woke up and I let it ring |
| B♭maj7 · Gm7 | the lights came back and I turned them off |
| Gm7 · C | nothing on, nothing on |

Bar 61 is silent again: she has switched them off. Bar 62, everything is back, now with tambourine sixteenths, a third horn stab, and the backing voice a third under every title line. “Ring” lands on G4 (bar 64). The payoff line runs on F4, A4 on “back”, G4 on “them”, then leaps a fifth to **D5 on “off”**, the downbeat of bar 66, held. It is the only D5 in the song, and the chord under it is Gm7.

### Post-chorus · bars 69–74

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Gm7 · Am7 | nothing on but the moon |
| Gm7 · Am7 | (nothing on) nothing on but the moon |
| B♭maj7 · B♭maj7 | and the only thing glowing is you |

The hook twice at full groove. Then at bar 73 the band strips to Rhodes and bass. The last line is verse 1's “the only thing glowing is you” sung again over B♭maj7, ending on D4 on the downbeat of bar 74, held into the outro.

### Outro · bars 75–76

B♭maj7. The riff's first bar, the Rhodes ringing. The song ends on B♭maj7: the lights stay off.

## For the players

- **Drums (live-sounding kit):** intro, sixteenth hats and a snare pickup into bar 3. Verses: kick on 1, the “a” of 2 and 3; snare 2 and 4 with ghost notes; sixteenth hats. Pre-choruses: four on the floor, open hats on the off-beats, snare eighths in the last bar up to beat 3, then **out**. Choruses and posts: **tacet in the chorus's first bar**, crash on the re-entry, four on the floor, snare and handclaps on 2 and 4, sixteenth hats with the open hat on the off-beats, a three-note snare pickup at the end of each chorus. Bridge: out for four bars, kick and hats from bar 57, snare eighths in bar 59, hit 1 in bar 60 and stop. Out for the last line.
- **Percussion:** bongos in the post-choruses; tambourine eighths in chorus 2, sixteenths in the last chorus and post.
- **Bass (finger P-bass, flatwounds):** verses, a syncopated root line (root, octave on the “and” of 2, fifth on 4). From each pre-chorus, disco octaves in eighths with a sixteenth push on beat 2. Out with the band at every cut. Bridge: in at bar 55 in whole notes, eighths from bar 59. Whole notes under the last line; hold the last B♭.
- **Funk guitar (muted):** choked off-beat eighths in the verses; the sixteenth pattern, accents on the off-beats, from each pre-chorus through the choruses. In at bar 59 in the bridge.
- **Riff guitar (clean):** the riff in the intro; answers in verse 2 where the singer leaves a bar; the riff's first bar in the outro, last note ringing. In the choruses the singer has the riff, so stay off it.
- **Rhodes:** held chords in the verses, half notes in the pre-choruses, three syncopated stabs a bar in the choruses. In the bridge you are the first sound when the power returns. You and the bass alone under the last line; ring the last B♭maj7.
- **Horns (trumpets, tenor sax, trombone):** two short stabs, beat 4 and its “and”, in every chorus and post bar where the singer is holding a note. In the last chorus, a third stab on the “and” of 2. A swell through pre-chorus 2.
- **Pad:** bridge bars 53–56 only: a low F and C, the fridge's hum, growing a little each bar.

## For the singer

**Female: A3–C5, D5 once.**

- **Sly, not breathy-sexy.** The words do the flirting. Sing it like you're telling a friend a secret you're pleased about.
- **The verses talk.** They sit low, C4 to G4. Quick syllables, then a held word on the downbeat.
- **The pre-chorus climbs to the cut.** Hold “breathe” (B♭4) and “are” (G4) through the silence. The room going quiet is the point.
- **Bar 1 of every chorus is yours alone.** Start “nothing” on the “and” of 1, not the downbeat. Make the leap to C5 on the first “on” clean and light, and let the band land under the second.
- **The post-chorus is a sing-along.** “Nothing on but the moon” walks down C5–B♭4–A4–G4. Keep it easy.
- **The bridge starts at the bottom** (A3) and rises to C5 on “breath”. Hold it over the silence, then breathe before the last chorus.
- **The D5 on “off” (bar 66)** is the only time you go above C5. Leap to it from G4, land it on the downbeat, and hold it. It's the line the song is for.
- **The last line is quiet.** Just Rhodes and bass: “and the only thing glowing is you,” close to the mic, ending on D4.

## Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** A woman in complete control of a happy accident. The listener should be grinning by the first post-chorus and catch the double meaning without being told. The cut at each chorus should feel like a held breath; the bridge's return should feel like a threat; “I turned them off” should feel like a wink.

**References**

- **Sabrina Carpenter, “Espresso” (2024):** a two-chord loop where neither chord is the home chord, and a melody that sits loosely on the chords. Take the restless, never-resolving loop and the knowing delivery.
- **Bruno Mars, “I Just Might” (2026):** the groove built from buzzy funk guitar, bongos and handclaps, with trumpets on the hook and a P-bass with flatwounds. Take the live, old-school band sound.
- **DNCE, “Cake by the Ocean” (2015):** a 1970s-inspired disco-funk song at 119 BPM that is about sex without saying so. Take the tempo, the minor-leaning bounce and the euphemism played with a straight face.

**Dynamic arc**

| Section (bars) | Level | What changes |
|--------------------|------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Intro, verse 1 (1–12) | mp | Riff, then a light funk groove |
| Pre-chorus (13–16) | mf, rising | Four on the floor; the tape stop on beat 3 of bar 16 |
| Chorus 1, post (17–28) | silence → f | Voice alone for a bar, then everything; horn stabs; bongos |
| Verse 2, pre 2 (29–40) | mp → mf | Riff answers; horn swell; the tape stop again |
| Chorus 2, post (41–52) | silence → f | As chorus 1, with tambourine |
| Bridge (53–60) | p → ff | The hum and Rhodes; then bass, kit, guitar; everyone hits bar 60 and stops |
| Chorus 3 (61–68) | silence → ff | Voice alone; then the biggest chorus: tambourine sixteenths, three horn stabs, backing voice, D5 on “off” |
| Last line, outro (73–76) | p | Rhodes and bass only; end on B♭maj7, ringing |

**Non-negotiables**

1. The band is silent in the first bar of every chorus.
2. No F chord outside the bridge. The song ends on B♭maj7.
3. The title tune never changes: A4, G4, C5 held | C5, A4, E4.
4. One D5, on “off” in bar 66. Nothing else above C5.
5. Every “nothing on” starts on the “and” of 1.
6. Under 3 minutes. No added intro, no extra chorus.

**Mix priorities**

1. **The words.** The jokes must land on first hearing. The vocal is always in front.
2. **The silences.** The cuts are as loud as the choruses. No reverb tail, no pad, no click bleed in bars 17, 41 and 61.
3. **Kick, bass and the muted guitar** are the groove; keep them tight and dry.
4. **Horns** are punctuation, bright and short.
5. **Never cover:** the first “nothing on” of each chorus, “I heard you smile,” “hold my breath,” the D5 on “off,” the last line.

## Production brief

**The song.** A power cut on a summer night, and a woman who decides she likes it better in the dark. Sex told entirely through the outage: nothing on the TV, no Wi-Fi, nothing on. It's disco-funk pop at 116 for one female voice, a backing voice and a live band, under three minutes. It should feel playful and confident, with the band literally losing power at the top of every chorus.

**References**

- **Sabrina Carpenter, “Espresso” (2024):** its loop sits on the iv and v chords and neither is the tonic; its chorus phrases end on the home note over chords that aren't home. Take the restless loop. Ours goes Gm7–Am7 and B♭maj7–C and never sounds F until the bridge.
- **Bruno Mars, “I Just Might” (2026):** a disco-pop, funk song that debuted at #1, built from buzzy funk guitar, bongos and handclaps, with trumpets on the hook and a P-style bass with flatwounds. Take the band: live kit, flatwound bass, choked guitar, short brass.
- **DNCE, “Cake by the Ocean” (2015):** a 1970s-inspired disco-funk song about sex, in E minor at 119 BPM. Take the tempo, the bounce and the straight-faced euphemism.

**Non-negotiables**

1. **♩ = 116, straight sixteenths.** No tempo changes.
2. **The cuts:** a tape stop on beat 3 of bars 16 and 40, silence through bars 17 and 41, and the same after the hit on bar 60's downbeat through bar 61.
3. **F only in the bridge** (bars 53–54 and 57). End on B♭maj7.
4. **The title tune:** A4, G4, C5 | C5, A4, E4, starting on the “and” of 1.
5. **D5 on “off” (bar 66)** is the only note above C5.

## Session notes

**Tempo and metre.** 76 bars, about 2:37. 4/4 at ♩ = 116, straight sixteenths. Keep the click running through the cuts in the players' phones, but no click in the singer's phones for the silent bars, and none on the record.

**Key.** F major signature. The home chord, F, is played only in the bridge.

**Range**

- **Voice:** female, A3–C5, with one D5 (“off”, bar 66).
- **Verses:** B♭3–A4, mostly C4–G4.
- **Pre-choruses:** D4–B♭4.
- **Choruses and posts:** C4–C5.
- **Bridge:** A3–C5.

**Tracking**

1. **Kit, bass and muted guitar together** to the click. Play the cuts live: everyone stops on beat 3 of bars 16 and 40 and after the hit on the downbeat of bar 60, and nobody plays bars 17, 41 and 61.
2. **Rhodes and riff guitar.**
3. **Lead vocal.** Comp for the jokes first. Bars 17, 41 and 61 are a cappella, so take those carefully.
4. **Backing voice:** the echo in bars 27, 51 and 71, and a third under the title lines in the last chorus.
5. **Horns, percussion (bongos, tambourine, claps) and the bridge pad last.**
6. **Any part one player can't play at once** (Rhodes plus pad in the bridge; claps doubling the snare) goes down as its own pass.

**Blend and panning**

- **Lead vocal:** centre and on top; short plate. Dry at the cuts.
- **Backing voice:** left, tucked under; doubled if needed.
- **Muted guitar:** left; **riff guitar:** right; **horns:** right of centre; **Rhodes:** slightly left.
- **Kit:** tight and natural; claps on the snare; bongos and tambourine wide.
- **The tape stop:** pitch the whole band (not the vocal) down about an octave over the last beat before the silence.
- **The bridge:** start with the pad and Rhodes alone and dry; open the room as each instrument returns.
