# Nothing On — test report (ZenEng v64, v65 fixes as ports)

This is the engine's record of song 31, kept apart from the songbook, which is for musicians only. The writer's brief: "Let's write our next hit. An easy subject but hard to land — sex," in the lyrical vein of "Cake by the Ocean": engaging euphemism, evocative and fun, never graphic or comical. Before the music he asked for a deep dive into contemporary hit music, to be "familiar enough to not be immediately rejected" while "offering enough deviation from the pack to be memorable". The song is disco-funk pop for one female lead, a backing echo and a band, 2:37, with three designed departures.

Nothing from either list is sung.

## The research (8 Nov), as the run's hit rules

- **Optimal differentiation:** a study of about 27,000 Hot 100 songs (Askin and Mauskapf 2017) found that the most typical songs do worse and that slightly-below-average typicality does best. Built: three departures; everything else inside the corpus's middle band.
- **2025's top 10:** pop back on top; retro influences in about a third (the 1980s the favourite decade for six years); acoustic-sounding drums overtook electronic for the first time since 2014; synths down; a post-chorus hook in 63%; staying-power hits shorter (under about 3:20, often under 3:00) with 11-second intros. Built: live-sounding kit, funk guitars, horns, Rhodes; a post-chorus after every chorus; 2:37; an 8.3-second intro; the title 25 seconds after the singing starts.
- **Exemplars:** "Espresso" (a loop on iv and v that never sounds the tonic, phrases on weak beats); "Cake by the Ocean" (119 BPM, disco-funk euphemism); "Manchild" (123, country-disco); Bruno Mars's "I Just Might" (2026 #1 debut: funk guitar, bongos, claps, trumpets, flatwound P-bass). Built: 116 BPM, that band, and the withheld home chord.
- **Harmony corpus (Hooktheory):** IV and V appear in more progressions than I. Built: the song lives on ii, iii, IV and V.
- **Earworms (Jakubowski 2016):** a common contour plus one distinctive leap or repeated note. Built: the title cell rises a fourth to C5, repeats C5, falls a fourth to E4.
- **Repetition (Nunes 2015):** more chorus repetition, better #1 odds. Built: the title sung 6 or 7 times in every chorus and post (with the echo).
- **The local melody corpus (515 pop melodies):** chorus range median 14 semitones (12–17 the middle half); chorus 3 semitones above the verse at the median; syncopation median 0.10 (75th percentile 0.17, 95th 0.32). Built: range 12, lift +5, syncopation 0.21 (deliberately between the 75th and 95th percentiles), steps 0.50 and leaps 0.14 (both in the middle half).

## Findings

80. **The engine has no corpus profile with chosen departures.** The research's rule (familiar in most features, deliberately atypical in two or three) had to be wired by hand: the chorus's range, lift, steps, leaps and syncopation were measured against the 515-melody corpus in `evaluate`, with syncopation scored for landing between the 75th and 95th percentiles and the rest for the middle half. Density fell between the 5th and 25th percentiles as a side effect of the designed rests and was let stand. **Proposed fix 80:** a corpus-profile block in intake: per feature, a target band (middle half by default); the writer names the two or three features to push outside it. The evaluation scores against the bands and the test report prints them.
81. **No field for a withheld home chord.** The key is F major, but F is designed to sound only in the bridge. The engine's "tonic" rules (a line before the midpoint stays open) still read F; the check that F appears only in the bridge was written by hand. **Proposed fix 81:** a harmonic-plan field, `tonic_withheld_until` (a section or bar), read by the chord plan, the Nashville footer and consistency.
82. **No field for cuts (extends proposed fix 76).** The band's silences (a tape stop into a silent bar at every chorus, a hit-and-stop at the bridge's end) lived in the build as hand rules, plus a cut list in the record that the demo, lead sheet and parts read for their marks. The tape stop needed the pitch-bend range set to an octave (GM's default is ±2 semitones). **Proposed fix 82:** fold into 76 a `cuts` list (kind: drop, stop, tape stop; start; silent-from; back) that the build enforces, the demo renders, the chart and parts mark, and consistency checks.
83. **More particle and pronoun stress at a phrase's end (extends proposed fix 73).** "Nothing ON", "come back ON", "glowing is YOU", "where you ARE" were read weak. **The run's port:** "on" closing a phrase after "nothing", "back" or "light"; a line-final "you" or "are" after a function word. **Proposed fix 83:** in the single stress function (fix 45), a phrase-final particle or object pronoun after an unstressed word carries the stress.
84. **A parenthetical in the lyric is a backing part.** "(nothing on) nothing on but you" needed a backing echo with its own notes, words, staff, part and MIDI track, all set by hand; the lyric file holds the lead line only. **Proposed fix 84:** parse a parenthetical in a sung line as an echo (singer, a third under or at unison, placed in the lead's gap), carried through the record to the lead sheet, parts and consistency.
85. **No form for an evolving chorus.** The chorus keeps its title lines but changes its middle lines each time (a promise kept: hope → if → did). The engine treats a chorus as repeated verbatim; choruses 2 and 3 were refitted line by line, and the payoff's top note was aimed by hand. **Proposed fix 85:** an "evolving chorus" form: fixed hook lines; per-repeat lines refitted to the first chorus; an optional payoff aim on the last repeat.
86. **10.20 cannot see a payoff.** The writer's named thesis line, "the lights came back and I turned them off", ranks last under both regimes: it is sung once, plainly worded and late. Its force is structural (it completes chorus 2's "I'll turn them off" and pays off the whole song). **Proposed fix 86:** a payoff feature (a line that completes an earlier conditional or plant scores), or let the writer name a thesis line that the blind pick must include.
87. **9.12 penalizes a designed silence.** The cut bar at the top of every chorus lowers the chorus's average, so chorus 2 beat verse 2 by 0.01 only after verse 2 was thinned. **Proposed fix 87:** 9.12 reads a section with a designed cut from its re-entry, or leaves the cut bar out of the average.
88. **The parts compress pages.** Dense sixteenth guitar and drum parts at 116 overfill letter pages and LilyPond compresses them (legible, but tight). **Proposed fix 88:** for parts with sixteenth density above a threshold, set four bars a system and a smaller staff size, or allow tabloid.
