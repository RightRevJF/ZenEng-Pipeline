# At a glance

**What it's about.** A man loves a wild river. It floods the low field every spring, and his family curses it at supper and swims in it after dark. He's young, he has a slide rule, and he dams it. The town gets its lights and calls the quiet peace. Below the wall the valley dries up: the willows, the corn, the mill wheel, the rope swing. Then his daughter asks him what a river sounds like, and he can't answer. That night he takes her up the wall and opens the gate. The water goes down the valley shouting, and it takes his fence and the plaque with his name on it. The song never says what it means. The chorus says it: *the water used to sing; now it only shines.* By the end, *living water doesn't shine. It sings.*

**How it goes.** An Americana shuffle at ♩ = 96 in G. The eighths are swung (long-short). It's written straight, with a swing mark. The verses use brushes and the choruses use sticks. The bridge strips down to almost nothing (the dry valley). The break is the gate opening, a guitar solo that climbs. Verse 3 starts on brushes and the band comes in at "then the whole lake leaned against the gate." The last chorus is the biggest, with an organ line over the voice. The song ends on "It sings," held, and the band plays out on the lick.

**The singer.** A warm, weathered baritone, singing every line, G2 to A3 as it sounds. The lead sheet writes the voice an octave higher on a treble clef with an 8 below it.

# Section by section

Pitches are where the voice sounds. Bar numbers count from the first bar of the intro.

## Intro (bars 1–8)

The acoustic guitar shuffles from bar 1. The electric guitar plays the lick on the "and" of 1, beat 2, beat 3 and the "and" of 3: root, fifth, root, fifth, starting on G3. It moves with the chords (G C G D). The bass and brushes come in at bar 5.

## Verse 1 (bars 9–16)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| G G | It came off the ridge in a hurry, |
| C | loud as a county fair, |
| G Em | took the low field every April, |
| C | gave it back black by June; |
| D | we cursed it over supper |
| D | and swam in it after dark. |

The first line starts on the "and" of beat 2 in bar 9. It climbs from A2 through a chromatic E3–F3–F♯3 to A3 on "hur-ry" (bar 10, beat 3). The short lines get one bar each and fall back to A2 at the end ("June," bar 14; "supper," bar 15; "dark," bar 16). "Fair" (bar 11) lands on a bluesy B♭2 over C; sing it as written. The verse ends on A2 over D, off the tonic, and leans into the pre-chorus.

| C D | and I knew what to do with stone: |

Both lines start on A3 and step down to A2 ("rea-son," bar 18; "stone," bar 20, beat 1, over D).

## Chorus 1 (bars 21–28)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| C G | The water used to sing |
| Em D | the whole night under my window, |
| C G | the water used to sing. |
| F G | Now it only shines. |

The hook starts on the "and" of 3 in bar 21. It moves around E3 and D3 and falls to "sing" on G2, the tonic, on beat 3 of bar 22. The hook is sung twice, the same both times (bars 21–22 and 25–26). "Window" rises to F♯3 (bar 24). "Shines" comes down to G2 on beat 4 of bar 27, over the F chord. That F is the ♭7, the one chord in the song from outside the key, and it's the shine.

## Verse 2 (bars 29–36) and pre-chorus 2 (bars 37–40)

Same chords and the same shape of tune, with the rhythm fitted to the new words. The long line "and the river lay down like a sheepdog" gets two bars (32–33) and lands "sheep-dog" on B2 over Em. "Grey" (bar 31) takes the B♭2 that "fair" took. Pre 2: "The whole town called the quiet peace, / and I slept like a stone:" comes down to A2 on "stone" (bar 40).

## Chorus 2 (bars 41–48)

Exactly as chorus 1.

## Bridge (bars 49–60)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Em C | Below the wall the willows went to paper, |
| G D | the corn came up in matchsticks, |
| Em C | the mill wheel stopped like a dead man's watch, |

Every line gets two bars. The bridge sits lower than the verse, with its middle around B2, and it never goes above G3. "Paper" falls to G2 (bar 50). "Watch" sits on B♭2 (bar 54). The girl's question climbs to G3 on "riv-er" and ends on E3, "like?", over C (bar 58), unanswered. "Answer" ends on F♯3 over D (bar 60) and is left hanging into the break.

## Break, "the gate" (bars 61–68)

The electric guitar solos for eight bars. It plays the lick, then the lick a step higher, then four bars climbing (E4 up toward B5), then the lick again. Sticks, boogie bass, the electric rhythm part chopping low.

The second pass of the hook changes its words ("the water's singing now") but keeps its tune, down to G2 on "now" (bar 86). "It took the fence, it took the road" comes down to G2 (bar 90). "Door" stays up on E3 (bar 92). "Shine" falls to B2 over C (bar 93). Then two bars of the band alone. "It sings" lands on G2, beat 3 of bar 96, and is held through the first bar of the outro.

## Outro (bars 97–104)

The lick and the band for seven bars. One G chord on beat 1 of bar 104, everyone, let it ring.

# For each player

**Voice.** Baritone, G2 to A3, written an octave higher. Most of the song lives between A2 and A3. Sing the verses conversationally: lots of short words on swung eighths, with a breath at each line's end. The hook falls to the low G every time; let it be low and plain. Hold "It sings" as long as the band allows.

**Acoustic guitar.** Shuffle eighths on open chords all the way through, swung: down on the beat, up on the "and" (the up-strokes lighter, on the top strings). In the bridge, only two strokes a bar, on 1 and 3, let ring. At the end, one G chord.

**Electric guitar.** Two passes on the record (one player can't do both at once):

- *Rhythm pass:* short chord chops on 2 and 4 in the choruses and from bar 77. In the break, chop the same shapes an octave lower, under the solo.
- *Lead pass:* the lick in the intro and outro; four short fills in the choruses, where the voice rests (bar 44, bars 84–85, 88 and 94), four to six notes each, ending on a chord tone; and the solo, bars 61–68.

Clean, a little twang, a touch of slap-back.

**Organ.** Drawbar organ, a soft Leslie. Swells in bars 19–20. Held chords from verse 2 on, louder in the choruses. In the bridge, only a low open fifth (E–B, then A–E at "and my girl said," then D–A). In the last chorus, hold the chords low with the left hand and play the countermelody with the right: G4 to E5, above the voice. It moves where the voice holds and holds where the voice moves.

**Bass.** In at bar 5. In the verses and pre-choruses, root and fifth in half notes. In the choruses, the break, verse 3 from bar 77 and the outro, the boogie in quarter notes: root, third, fifth, sixth (on Em and Am: root, minor third, fifth, flat seventh). Whole notes in the bridge. The root is always on beat 1.

**Drums.** Brushes in the intro (from bar 5), the verses, the pre-choruses, the bridge and verse 3's first four bars: kick on 1 and 3, a brush tap on 2 and 4, the swirl on every beat. The bridge is sparse, with the swirl every other bar. Sticks in the choruses, the break, verse 3 from bar 77, the last chorus and the outro: kick on 1, 3 and the swung "and" of 4, snare on 2 and 4, hi-hat eighths swung (ride in the last chorus), and a crash at each section's start and every four bars of the last chorus. A fill in the last bar of every section, brush taps or snare and toms. One hit with the final chord.

# Arrangement

The song grows twice. It builds from the first verse to the second chorus. Then it drops to the dry bridge and builds again through the gate to the last chorus. Each chorus is bigger than the verse before it, and the last chorus is the biggest. The bridge is the quietest part of the song, with no electric guitar at all. That's the silence the girl's question lands in. The organ's countermelody saves itself for the last chorus, when the water is back.

## Production brief

*The song.* A storyteller's shuffle about a man who tamed a river and killed what he loved about it, and then let it go. It should feel lived-in and unhurried, never preachy. The chorus does the meaning, and the arrangement does the drama: brushes to sticks, the dry bridge, the gate.

*References.*

- **Drive-By Truckers, "TVA"** (Jason Isbell; *The Dirty South*, 2004). Isbell's song about three generations, the New Deal and the Wilson Dam. It's the kin of this story: a dam, a family and what the work cost.
- **Stevie Ray Vaughan and Double Trouble, "Pride and Joy"** (1983). Called a classic Texas shuffle. Use it for the feel: the walking quarter-note bass under the swung eighths, the drums and bass locked. Ours is lighter and slower, but the swing is the same kind.
- **The Band, "Up on Cripple Creek"** (1969). For the Americana band sound and the organ: Garth Hudson played clavinet and Lowrey organ on it, with Levon Helm singing from behind the drums. A loose, rolling groove under a story.

*Non-negotiables.* The swing (long-short eighths, never straight). The hook falls to the low G. The bridge is bare: no electric guitar, organ only a low fifth. The band comes in at bar 77, on "it went down the valley shouting." The ♭7 F under "Now it only shines." "It sings" is held, and bar 104 is one chord left to ring.

## Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** ♩ = 96, 4/4 throughout. Give the click as a swung eighth (triplet feel) so the players lock to the swing.
- **Key.** G major. The F chord (♭7) appears in the chorus, and the voice touches B♭ as a blue note on "fair," "grey," "watch" and "mule."
- **Chords per bar.** As in the form table and the chart: one chord a bar.
- **Range.** Voice G2–A3. Organ countermelody G4–E5. Solo E4–B5. Bass E1–B2.
- **Tracking.** Bass, drums and acoustic first, to the swung click, with a guide vocal. Then the electric guitar's rhythm pass and, separately, its lead pass (the lick, fills and solo); one player can't do both at once. Organ chords and then the countermelody, as one or two passes. The lead vocal last.
- **Drums.** Change from brushes to sticks between sections (at bar 21, back at bar 29, and so on); verse 3 changes at bar 77. If one drummer tracks it, record the brush and stick sections as separate passes.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice up the middle, clearly on top (at least 3 dB over the band). Acoustic left, electric right, organ left of centre, the countermelody right of centre, bass and kick in the middle. A little room on the drums; keep the bridge dry and close.
