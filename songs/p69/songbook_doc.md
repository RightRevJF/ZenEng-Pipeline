# Before the Bread Goes Cold (first edition): songbook and production notes

Words and music: Jason Bryant

## At a glance

**What it is about.** Someone stands in the doorway of a loud, warm room, counting and measuring the night instead of living it. The song is sung to them by a friend at the table: put the pen down, pull the chair out, come in before the bread goes cold. It is an invitation, urgent and glad, not a lecture.

**How to play it.** A major, ♩ = 120, 4/4, heartland rock. 120 bars, about 4:00. One baritone voice (G♯2 to E4), a harmony voice in the choruses, rhythm guitar, a lead guitar in the break, bass and drums.

## For each player

**Voice.** A baritone, G♯2 to E4. Tell it plainly in the verses; the choruses sit about 3 semitones higher, so let them open a little without pushing. Sing the title as written, in bar 8 of the chorus, from the and of beat 1: E3 C♯3 C♯3 E3 E3 F♯3. The song’s top note, E4, comes 3 times: on “you're” in the bridge (bar 94); on “holding” in the bridge (bar 94); on “air” in the bridge (bar 95).

**Harmony voice.** Under the lead in all three choruses, in his rhythm and on his words; 55 in 100 of its notes sit a third to a fifth below him. Range E2 to F♯3: the lowest notes want a true bass, or take them up an octave. Keep it soft; he leads.

**Lead guitar.** Out until the break (bars 79–86), where it plays verse 1's tune over the verse's chords, an octave above where he sings it. Let it sing the tune; it is not a solo.

**Rhythm guitar.** Steady eighth-note strums from bar 1 to the end, under his voice; where a chord tone would sit a step from the note he sings, leave it out. Range B2 to F♯4.

**Bass.** Quarter notes on every beat: roots and fifths, with chromatic passing notes a half step from the next root. Range E1 to F♯2. Lock with the kick.

**Drums.** Kick on 1, the and of 2, and 3; snare on 2 and 4; hat eighths, from bar 2 to the end. A tom fill in the bar before each chorus (bars 30, 68, 98). Drive it, but never rush it.

## For the singer

Breathe between lines: his phrases run 3.2 seconds at the median. Some lines run straight into the next with less than a beat between them (into bars 25, 28, 31, 32, 33, 51, 57, 63 and others); take a quick breath in the gap or sing through, as feels natural; the longest stretch without half a beat's rest is 7.0 seconds. Lines that end on a long note (in verse 1: “table”, “measure”) want to be held to their length and released, not clipped. The verse lines are long, three bars each: keep them conversational, as if leaning in to someone, and let the chorus's short lines land hard. The bridge's last line, “to be fed”, stands alone: give it the space. The last chorus ends on the title twice; leave the last word open.

## The arrangement

The band drives one groove the whole way: the rhythm guitar strums eighths from the first bar to the last, the bass on every beat, the kick on 1, the and of 2, and 3 under the snare on 2 and 4. What changes is the harmony voice in the choruses, a tom fill leading into each chorus, the lead guitar taking verse 1's tune in the break after chorus 2, and the last chorus, four bars longer than the others. After chorus 1 the band plays four bars alone. Keep the verses tight and close; let the choruses open wide.

### Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** A friend at a crowded table calling someone in from the doorway, where they have been measuring the night instead of living it. It should feel like an open door and a full table: urgent and glad, never a lecture.

**References.**

- Bruce Springsteen, “Thunder Road” (Born to Run, 1975): take the song as an invitation, sung straight to one person, with the urgency of a door about to close.
- Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers, “The Waiting” (Hard Promises, 1981): take the ringing guitars and a chorus about waiting that never drags.
- The War on Drugs, “Red Eyes” (Lost in the Dream, 2014): take the steady, driving pulse that keeps lifting without speeding up.

**Non-negotiables.**

- ♩ = 120, driving and steady; no push into the choruses.
- The title as written every time: in bar 8 of the chorus, from the and of beat 1.
- The harmony voice below the lead, never above.
- The guitar under him, never a step from the note he sings.
- The last chorus’s last bar: A D A.

**Mix priorities.** The voice in front and centre. Kick and bass locked together, driving. The rhythm guitar left, wide; the lead guitar right, up front only in the break. The harmony voice tucked under the lead in the choruses. Nothing may cover the title line.
