# The Word Without a Word

*Words and music: Jason Bryant*

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Style | A concert carol for one high voice, piano, cello and strings |
| Voice | Written for soprano: D♭4–A♭5, with one B♭5 ("world," the last refrain). A tenor sings it an octave lower |
| Tempo | ♩ = 66 in 3/4; the last refrain and coda broaden to ♩ = 56 |
| Length | 80 bars, about 3:44 |
| Key | D♭ major; a whole step up to E♭ from verse 4 |
| Feel | Begins almost whispered at the cradle and ends in full voice, then falls back to breathing. Never sweet; always plain |

## What the song is about

The voice that said "Let there be light," and light came running, now lies in a feeding trough without a single word. The eternal Word can't speak. His mother has to teach him the names of the things he made: bread softened on his tongue, rain in his cupped hand, the stars he hung. He works milk and spit into half a sound, loses it, tries again, while she says "ma" until he finds it. When at last a whole word comes out, it's one he never had to learn: he reaches up past both of them and says "Abba," and Joseph turns around.

Then the cry, red-faced and hoarse against her ear, and the thought the song has been walking toward: all that God has left to say is crying, and his mother hears. The Word without a word is what God meant to say. And the breath that made the world goes in, and out, and in.

The idea is old: in 1618 Lancelot Andrewes preached on *Verbum infans*, "the Word without a word." The song keeps it in the body. No angels, no glory, no sentiment: a mother, a child, a word.

**Playing it:**

- **Start almost in silence.** Verse 1 is barely sung and barely accompanied.
- **"Ma" and "Abba" are the song's anchors.** Under "ma" nothing moves. Under "Abba" there's nothing at all: the whole band is silent.
- **"And Joseph turns"** is where the music turns too: everyone comes back on B♭, and the song steps up into E♭.
- **The B♭5 on "world" is the summit; the last line is a whisper.** The song ends on the fifth, open, like breathing that goes on.

*D♭ G♭ D♭ A♭.* Piano alone: the left hand's cradle figure in eighths (root, fifth, tenth, octave, tenth, fifth), a soft right-hand chord in bars 1 and 3.

As refrain 1, a little fuller, with the cello.

*G♭ A♭ Fm B♭m G♭ D♭ E♭m F.* Her lullaby: the cello sings the refrain's tune an octave below the voice, at half voice, over the piano's cradle figure. In the last two bars the strings swell over E♭m and F, leaning toward the bridge.

- **Melody:** D♭4–B♭4. "Mount" falls to D♭4 (bar 46); "sound" held on A♭4 (bar 48). **"Ma"** is held on B♭4 for two beats at the downbeat of bar 51.

### Bridge · bars 53–60

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| B♭m · G♭ | And when he gets a whole word out, |
| E♭m · A♭ | it's not a word he had to learn; |
| B♭m · G♭ | he reaches up past both of them. |
| Fm · B♭ | Now: *Abba*. And Joseph turns. |

- **Melody:** up to D♭5 ("gets," bar 53; "up," bar 57). "Learn" settles on F4 and holds (bar 55).

- **Accompaniment:** the strings build under the piano; the cello holds the bass. **Bar 59: everyone is silent.** Bar 60: everyone back on B♭.

| Chords | Line |

- **Melody:** F4–E♭5. "Shakes" reaches E♭5 (bar 62); "God" and "crying" on C5 (bars 65, 67); "hears" on B♭4 (bar 68).
- **Accompaniment:** the full ensemble for the first time: both hands of the piano, cello, strings.

### Refrain 3 · bars 69–76 (broadening, ♩ = 56)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| A♭ · B♭ | The Word without a word |
| Gm · Cm | is what God meant to say: |
| A♭ · E♭ | the breath that made the **world** |
| Fm · B♭ | goes in, and out, and in. |

- **Melody:** "word" on F5 (bar 70). Then the climb: "the breath that made the world" rises E♭5, F5, G5, A♭5 to **B♭5 on "world," over E♭ at the downbeat of bar 74**, the only B♭5 in the song. Then "goes in, and out, and in" falls through D5, C5 and B♭4 to G4, and ends on **B♭4 over B♭** (bar 76), held and open.
- **Accompaniment:** the fullest of the song, with a held top line in the strings, until the last line, which thins to piano and strings, pianissimo.

### Coda · bars 77–80

*A♭ Fm E♭ E♭.* Piano alone. The last chord is E♭ with an added F, left to ring.

## For the players

- **Piano:** the cradle figure throughout, the left hand flowing in eighths: root, fifth, tenth, octave, tenth, fifth (verse 1 thinned to three notes a bar). The right hand adds a chord on beat 1, held, in the refrains, the bridge and verse 4, and in bars 1 and 3 of the intro. Under "ma," no new note. In bar 59, silence. Coda alone.
- **Cello:** in from verse 2, the root held a bar. In the interlude, the melody: the refrain's tune an octave below the voice, at half voice. Silent in bar 59.
- **Strings:** a soft pad under the refrains; the swell in the interlude's last two bars; building through the bridge; full in verse 4 and the last refrain, with a held top line; thinning to pianissimo for the last line. Silent in bar 59.

## For the singer

**Written for soprano, D♭4–A♭5, one B♭5. A tenor sings it an octave lower.**

- **Verse 1 is barely sung.** Let the listener lean in.
- **Sing the verses as things you've watched.** Bread, rain, spit, the cry. The pictures do the work.
- **"Ma":** hold it, plainly. Nothing under you moves.
- **"Abba":** the falling third of a child calling out. Sing it into silence, then let "and Joseph turns" lift you into E♭.
- **The climb in the last refrain** is the summit of the song: E♭5 to B♭5 over one line. Breathe before "the breath."
- **Then let it go.** "Goes in, and out, and in" is almost spoken. End on B♭4 and don't resolve it.

## Production brief

**The song.** A concert carol on the Incarnation as the Word who cannot yet speak: the voice of creation, now a newborn learning his mother's language, whose first word is the name he has always called his Father. One high voice over piano, cello and strings, rising from a whisper at the cradle to a single B♭5 on "world," and ending on an open breath. It must never turn sweet: plain words, real bodies, two silences where the music holds its breath.

**References**

- **Adolphe Adam, "O Holy Night" (*Cantique de Noël*, 1847):** the high-voice Christmas showpiece, built on a long climb to its famous high note. Take the shape: one great ascent, earned over the whole song.
- **Pietro Yon, "Gesù Bambino" (1917):** an Italian carol that is a lullaby for the Child and has long been a solo concert piece. Take the cradle-song inside the showpiece.
- **Peter Warlock, "Bethlehem Down" (1927, words by Bruce Blunt):** a carol in D minor, introverted and restrained, which Warlock later arranged for solo voice. Take its restraint, and its plain English.

**Non-negotiables**

1. **Two silences.** Under "ma" (bar 51) nothing moves. In bar 59 ("Now: Abba") the whole ensemble is silent.
2. **The turn into E♭ happens on "and Joseph turns"** (bar 60, B♭ chord, D5 in the voice). Don't anticipate it.
3. **The B♭5 on "world" (bar 74) is the only note above A♭5.**
4. **The last line is quiet and the voice ends on B♭4, open.** No added final tonic in the voice.
5. **No sweetness.** No portamento into notes, no choir "oohs," no bells or sleigh sounds, no harp glissandos.
6. **Verse 1 is nearly unaccompanied.** Let the song grow from almost nothing.

## Session notes

**Tempo and click.** 80 bars, about 3:44. ♩ = 66 in 3/4 for bars 1–68; ♩ = 56 from bar 69 (the last refrain) to the end. Program the tempo change; let the conductor or pianist shape a little rubato around it.

**Key.** D♭ major (five flats) for bars 1–60; E♭ major (three flats) from bar 61.

**Range**

- **Voice:** written for soprano, D♭4–A♭5, one B♭5 ("world," bar 74). Tenor an octave lower: D♭3–A♭4, one B♭4.
- **Verses 1–3:** D♭4–B♭4. **Refrains 1–2:** F4–E♭5. **Bridge:** up to D♭5, with one D5. **Verse 4:** F4–E♭5. **Refrain 3:** G4–B♭5.

**Tracking**

1. **Piano first**, to the click with the tempo change, or live with the singer if possible: the rubato matters more than the grid.
2. **Cello** next, including the interlude's melody as a solo take.
3. **Strings** last, as a section or layered, keeping bar 59 completely silent.
4. **Voice:** close and dry for verse 1; open the room as the song grows; record the last line separately if needed, very quietly.

**Blend and panning**

- **Voice** centre, always in front. **Piano** centre-left; **cello** centre-right; **strings** wide and behind.
- **The silences:** cut any reverb tails that would cover bar 59.
- **The ending:** let the coda's last chord ring out completely.
