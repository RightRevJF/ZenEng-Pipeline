# Sadie

*Words and music: Jason Bryant*

## What the song is about

Four seconds left, tied at ten. A father stands in the bleachers with his coffee cup chewed to the rim, watching his daughter, number two with eye black on her cheeks, line up the kick that decides the game. She's the only girl on the team. He's wearing the jacket he never earned a letter in, and somebody behind him starts the chant: "Sadie! Sadie!" Two thousand people are hollering the name he used to say softly so she would sleep, the name that was once only his to say.

She learned the kick in the backyard, over the clothesline, while he held the ball until his hands went raw. The Miller boy laughed at her on her first day; now he's the one kneeling to hold it for her. The snap comes back, the laces spin, his lips are moving, and he can't hear a sound. It's up.

The song never says whether it goes through. The last chorus is the whole crowd with her name, and for him it's never going to sound the same.

**Playing it:**

- **The riff is the title.** The lead guitar's opening notes are the chorus's "SA-die! SA-die!", so the hook is in the ear before anyone sings it.
- **The verses are tight and low:** palm-muted guitar, the singer near the bottom of his range. The lead guitar answers each line in the gap after it.
- **The choruses open up:** four on the floor, the gang on "Sadie! Sadie!"
- **The drop is the song's silence.** Everyone hits on beat 1 of bar 61 and stops. "It's up." Then nothing but the kick drum's heartbeat for three bars. Don't fill it.
- **The last chorus belongs to the crowd,** and its last line takes the song's one high note.

## The song, section by section

Each line takes two bars. Chords are shown beside each line.

### Intro · bars 1–8

*A A D A A A D E.* Bars 1–4: the lead guitar alone with the riff, following the chords. Bar 1 is the chorus's opening notes, E, C♯, E (pushed onto the "and" of beat 2), C♯, A, B; bar 2 is a low open-A chug with a lick. Bar 5: the band comes in (kick, snare, eighth hats, palm-muted rhythm guitar, bass on the root).

### Verse 1 · bars 9–16

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| A · A | Four seconds left and it's tied at ten, |
| D · A | the bleachers rumble under every boot; |
| F♯m · D | I've chewed my coffee cup down to the rim, |
| E · E | and that's my daughter, eye black, number two. |

- **Melody:** low and close, C♯3–A3, mostly E3, F♯3 and G♯3, like talking. "Seconds" on F♯3 (bar 9); "ten" on F♯3 (bar 10); "boot" on E3 (bar 12). "My" dips to C♯3 (bar 15) and "eye" lifts to A3, the top of the verse; "two" on F♯3 over E (bar 16).
- **Accompaniment:** palm-muted power chords in eighths, bass on the root, kick 1 and 3, snare 2 and 4. The lead guitar answers lines 1, 2 and 4 with the line's last two notes an octave up, in the gap before the next line (bars 10, 12, 16; bar 14 leaves no room, so no answer).

### Pre-chorus · bars 17–20

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| D · E | I'm in the jacket that I never lettered in, |
| F♯m · E | and somebody behind me starts it up: |

- **Melody:** up a notch, G♯3–C♯4. "Jacket" on B3 (bar 17). "Behind" reaches C♯4 (bar 19), and "up" lands on B3 over E at the downbeat of bar 20 and holds into the chorus.
- **Accompaniment:** floor tom in eighths, snare on 2 and 4, the rhythm guitar opens up, the organ enters. A snare fill in bar 20.

### Chorus · bars 21–28 (and 45–52, 65–72)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| A · E | Sadie! Sadie! Feet pounding the aluminum, |
| F♯m · D | two thousand hollering her name; |
| A · E | I used to say it soft so she would sleep, |
| F♯m · D | and back then it was only mine to say. |

- **Melody:** F♯3–E4. "**SA**-die! **SA**-die!" on E4 and C♯4, the top of the chorus, the second "SA" pushed onto the "and" of beat 2 (bar 21); the gang shouts it with him. "Feet" on A3, "pound-" on B3 pushed into bar 22, and "a-LU-mi-num" settles on B3 over E. "Thousand" and "hollering" touch D4 (bar 23); "name" on B3 over D (bar 24). "Soft" on B3, "sleep" on A3 (bar 26): the quiet line, sung lower. "Back" leaps up to C♯4; "say" falls to F♯3 over D (bar 28), the chorus ending open.
- **Accompaniment:** four on the floor, snare 2 and 4, open hats on the off-beats, crash on bar 1; bass root-fifth-octave eighths; acoustic guitar strumming eighths; organ pad. A tambourine joins in choruses 2 and 3.

### Riff · bars 29–32

*A A D E.* The riff again over the verse groove.

### Verse 2 · bars 33–40

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| A · A | She kicked a ball over the clothesline in the yard, |
| D · A | I held it for her till my hands went raw; |
| F♯m · D | her first day out, the Miller boy laughed hard, |
| E · E | now he's the one down on a knee to hold the ball. |

- **Melody:** the lowest part of the song, C♯3–A3. "Over" lifts to A3 (bar 33) and "yard" lands on E3 (bar 34). "Held it for her" sits on D3 and C♯3 (bar 35). "Out" on A3 (bar 37), "hard" on A3 (bar 38). "Ball" on E3 over E (bar 40).
- **Accompaniment:** as verse 1. The lead guitar answers every line: three notes after line 1 (bar 34), two after the others (bars 36, 38, 40).

### Pre-chorus · bars 41–44 and Chorus · bars 45–52

As before, the chorus with a tambourine on the off-beats.

### Bridge · bars 53–60

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| F♯m · D | The snap comes back and the band goes quiet, |
| A · E | the holder spins the laces round; |
| F♯m · D | I don't pray much, but my lips are moving, |
| Bm · E | I'm on my feet and I can't hear a sound. |

- **Melody:** low again, C♯3–A3, almost spoken. "Snap" on C♯3 (bar 53). "Much" reaches A3 (bar 57). "I'm on my feet" sits on D3 and C♯3, and "sound" lands on E3 over E (bar 60).
- **Accompaniment:** half time: kick on 1, a rim click on 3 for four bars, the rhythm guitar ringing one chord a bar, bass half notes. Bar 57: the full kit comes in and builds; a snare roll in bar 60.

### Drop · bars 61–64

*E, then N.C.* Bar 61: everyone hits E on beat 1 and stops. The singer, alone: "It's **up**," G♯3 rising to B3 on beat 3, held. Bars 62–64: nothing but the kick drum's heartbeat, lub-dub on beats 1 and 3. No fills, no cymbal swells.

### Chorus 3 · bars 65–72

Everyone back in on beat 1 with a crash. As chorus 2.

### Last chorus · bars 73–80

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| A · E | Sadie! Sadie! Feet pounding the aluminum, |
| F♯m · D | two thousand hollering her name; |
| A · E | I used to say it soft so she would sleep, |
| F♯m · D | and it's never gonna sound the same. |

- **Melody:** lines 1–3 as before; the gang sings all of it with him. The last line changes: "and it's" on C♯4 and D4, then **"nev-er" on F♯4 over F♯m (bar 79)**, the only F♯4 in the song, falling through E4, D4, C♯4 and B3 to "same" on A3 over D at the downbeat of bar 80, held.
- **Accompaniment:** the fullest of the song: tambourine in eighths, crashes on bars 73 and 77, the lead guitar doubling "Sadie! Sadie!" an octave up.

### Outro · bars 81–88

*A A D E A A D A.* The riff over the band; the gang shouts "Sadie! Sadie!" in bars 83 and 87. Bar 88: everyone on A, let it ring.

## For the players

- **Drums:** verses, intro (from bar 5), riff break and outro: kick on 1 and 3 and the "and" of 3, snare on 2 and 4, closed hats in eighths. Pre-choruses: floor tom eighths, a snare fill in the last bar. Choruses: four on the floor, snare 2 and 4, open hats on the off-beats, crash on bar 1; tambourine on the off-beats in choruses 2 and 3, in eighths in the last chorus. Bridge: half time, rim clicks for four bars, then the kit builds. **The drop:** one hit (kick and crash) on bar 61, then only the heartbeat, kick on beats 1 and 3 with a softer kick an eighth after each, in bars 62–64.
- **Bass:** tacet for the first four bars. Eighths on the root (verses, pre-choruses, riff break, outro); root-fifth-octave eighths in the choruses; half notes in the bridge; one hit at the drop, then tacet to bar 65.
- **Rhythm guitar (distorted):** palm-muted power chords in eighths in the verses and the riff break; open from the pre-chorus; one ringing chord a bar in the bridge; one hit at the drop, then tacet.
- **Lead guitar (overdriven):** the riff in the intro, the riff break and the outro. In the verses, answer each line in the gap after it, as written: two notes where the gap holds two, three where it holds three, none where there's no room. In the last chorus, double "Sadie! Sadie!" an octave up.
- **Acoustic guitar:** strummed eighths in the choruses, the bridge and the outro.
- **Organ:** a pad, one chord a bar, in the pre-choruses, choruses and bridge.
- **Gang:** "Sadie! Sadie!" at the top of every chorus, in unison and the octave below; all of the last chorus; two shouts in the outro.

## For the singer

**Baritone, C♯3–F♯4, one F♯4.**

- **The verses are close to speech.** You're a dad in the stands talking to the person next to you. Keep them low and plain.
- **The pre-chorus climbs toward the chant.** Let "starts it up" hand the song to the crowd.
- **"Sadie! Sadie!" is shared.** The gang shouts it with you; lean in, don't oversing.
- **"I used to say it soft" is the quiet heart of the chorus.** Sing it softer than the line before.
- **The bridge is almost a whisper over half time.** "I can't hear a sound" goes straight into the drop.
- **"It's up."** Alone, two notes, then wait three bars.
- **The last line** takes the one F♯4 on "never." Sing it with the crowd behind you, and land "same" on A3.

## Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** A father's pride, too big to say, carried by a crowd. The listener should feel the cold and the nerves in the verses, shout "Sadie!" by the second chorus, hold their breath in the drop, and come out of the last chorus not needing to know the score.

**References**

- **"The Boys of Fall" (Kenny Chesney, 2010):** the subject at its most familiar. Take the town's weight; leave its words.
- **"Beer Never Broke My Heart" (Luke Combs, 2019):** a riff-led country-rock record. Take the guitar riff up front.
- **"Springsteen" (Eric Church, 2012):** a crowd singalong built into the song. Take the gang.

**Dynamic arc**

| Section (bars) | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Intro (1–8) | mf | The riff alone, then the band at bar 5 |
| Verses (9–16, 33–40) | mf | Palm-muted, tight; the lead guitar answers |
| Pre-choruses (17–20, 41–44) | f, building | Toms, organ, the fill |
| Choruses 1–2 (21–28, 45–52) | f | Four on the floor; the gang on "Sadie!"; tambourine from chorus 2 |
| Bridge (53–60) | mp → f | Half time, rim clicks, then the build |
| Drop (61–64) | one hit, then pp | The heartbeat only |
| Choruses 3–4 (65–80) | ff | Everyone back; the crowd sings the last chorus; the one F♯4 |
| Outro (81–88) | ff | The riff, two shouts, the last chord rings |

**Non-negotiables**

1. Bars 62–64 are silent but for the kick's heartbeat. No fills, no swells, no reverb tails.
2. The song never says whether the kick is good.
3. The riff is the title's notes and opens the song.
4. One F♯4, on "never," in the last chorus.
5. The gang is a crowd, not a choir: unison and octave, a little rough.
6. The verses stay tight and palm-muted.
7. The lead guitar answers the verse lines only where the gap allows; never over the voice.

**Mix priorities**

1. **The words.** The lead is always in front; "I used to say it soft" must read even under four on the floor.
2. **The gang** on "Sadie!" is the hook: wide, in front of the band, behind the lead.
3. **The kick** carries the choruses and is alone in the drop: make the heartbeat felt.
4. **The guitars:** the riff up front in the intro and outro; the rhythm guitar wide.
5. **Never cover:** "It's up," the heartbeat, "never."

## Production brief

**The song.** A country-rock anthem from the stands: a father watches his daughter, the team's only girl, line up the kick that decides the game, and the whole crowd starts chanting her name, the name he used to whisper so she'd sleep. A baritone lead, a gang, and a band. Tight verses, a chanted chorus, a half-time bridge, and four bars of near silence (the ball in the air) before the last two choruses. The song never tells you if the kick is good.

**References**

- **Kenny Chesney, "The Boys of Fall" (2010):** a country No. 1 about high school football and the towns behind it. Take its weight; avoid its words. This song is told from the bleachers by one father, about one name.
- **Luke Combs, "Beer Never Broke My Heart" (2019):** a country chart-topper built on a guitar riff that owes as much to hard rock as to country. Take the riff-led drive.
- **Eric Church, "Springsteen" (2012):** a country and heartland-rock No. 1 whose "whoa" chant crowds sing back. Take the singalong built into the arrangement: here the chant is a name.

**Non-negotiables**

1. **The drop.** Bar 61: one hit on E, "It's up" alone. Bars 62–64: the kick's heartbeat only (beats 1 and 3, a softer kick an eighth after each). Everyone back on bar 65.
2. **The riff** opens the song alone for four bars; it is the chorus's opening notes.
3. **The gang** shouts "Sadie! Sadie!" in every chorus and sings all of the last chorus.
4. **One F♯4,** on "never," bar 79.
5. **No resolution of the kick:** no crowd roar, no whistle, no sound effect after the drop.

## Session notes

**Tempo and click.** 88 bars, about 2:50. ♩ = 124 in 4/4, straight eighths, throughout. Keep the click running through the drop (bars 61–64); the heartbeat is played to it.

**Key.** A major throughout.

**Range**

- **Voice:** baritone, C♯3–F♯4, one F♯4 ("never," bar 79).
- **Verses:** C♯3–A3. **Pre-choruses:** G♯3–C♯4. **Choruses:** F♯3–E4. **Bridge:** C♯3–A3. **Last line:** up to F♯4.

**Tracking**

1. **Drums and bass** to the click, the drop's heartbeat included.
2. **Rhythm guitar:** double-track the palm-muted verses and the open choruses, panned wide.
3. **Lead guitar:** the riff and the answers as a separate pass; the answers must sit in the gaps.
4. **Acoustic and organ.**
5. **Lead vocal,** then **the gang:** several voices, unison and the octave below, two or three passes stacked; let it sound like a crowd.

**Blend and panning**

- **Lead vocal** centre, in front. **Gang** wide, just behind the lead. **Rhythm guitars** hard left and right; **lead guitar** right of centre; **acoustic** left of centre; **organ** behind.
- **The drop:** cut every tail at bar 61; the heartbeat dry and close.
- **The end:** let the last A ring out.
