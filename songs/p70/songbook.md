# Everything That Moves (first edition): songbook and production notes

Words and music: Jason Bryant

## At a glance

**What it is about.** A party where every creature on earth is on the dance floor: the nightingale at midnight, the whale in the deep, the frog in the ditch, the cricket under the porch light, the mole cricket singing from his burrow, the earthworm in the garden. They never met and they are worlds apart, but they all keep the same time. In the bridge the beat turns out to be the one in your own chest, and the last chorus pulls you onto the floor with them. It is glad and a little absurd: even the worms in the dirt got the groove.

**How to play it.** A major, ♩ = 116, 4/4, funk-disco. 104 bars, about 3:35. One baritone voice (A2 to B3), a harmony voice in the choruses, rhythm guitar, horns, bass and drums.

## For each player

**Voice.** A baritone, A2 to B3. The verses are half-spoken, funk-style, mostly on E3 (70 notes in 100): ride the groove and let the words bounce, as if telling a good story across the bar. The choruses climb, to A3 on “groove”. Sing the title as written, “Ev-” on beat 4 of the bar before the chorus, the rest of “Everything” on its downbeat: E3 E3 E3 E3 E3. The song’s top note, B3, comes once: on “before” in the bridge (bar 76).

**Harmony voice.** Under the lead in all three choruses, in his rhythm and on his words; 61 in 100 of its notes sit a third to a fifth below him. Range E2 to G♯4: the lowest notes want a true bass, or take them up an octave. In bar 95 one note, G♯4, sits above him: sing it an octave down. Keep it soft; he leads.

**Horns.** Short, dry stabs on beat 1 and the and of 2 in every chorus (the guitar's top two notes), and verse 1's tune over the break (bars 63–70). Out everywhere else. Range D4 to E5. A trumpet and a tenor sax, or a trumpet, sax and trombone, all play it.

**Rhythm guitar.** Muted eighth-note chops from bar 1 to the end, under his voice; where a chord tone would sit a step from the note he sings, leave it out. Range E3 to G♯4.

**Bass.** Disco octaves in eighths: the root on the beat, its octave on the and. Where the octave would come near his voice it stays on the root. Range E1 to F♯3. Lock with the kick.

**Drums.** Four on the floor: kick on every beat, snare on 2 and 4, hats in sixteenths, from bar 2 to the end. A tom fill in the bar before each chorus (bars 22, 52, 84). Keep it in the pocket, never rushed.

## For the singer

Breathe between lines: his phrases run 3.9 seconds at the median. Some lines run straight into the next with less than a beat between them (into bars 11, 15, 22, 23, 28, 41, 47, 51 and others); take a quick breath in the gap or sing through, as feels natural; the longest stretch without half a beat's rest is 6.7 seconds. Lines that end on a long note (in verse 1: “band”, “wide”, “night”) want to be held to their length and released, not clipped. The verses sit low and close: talk them on the beat, with a grin. Let “everything that moves” land hard and the climb to “groove” open up. In the bridge, slow your voice down inside the groove, not the band. The last chorus brings in “even you, even me”; then the worms get the last word.

## The arrangement

One groove the whole way: four on the floor, the guitar chopping eighths, the bass in octaves, the hats in sixteenths. What changes is the horns stabbing in the choruses, the harmony voice under him there, a tom fill leading into each chorus, the horns taking verse 1's tune in the break after chorus 2, and the last chorus, two bars longer than the others. After chorus 1 the band plays four bars alone. Keep the verses tight and dry; let the choruses open wide.

### Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** The whole living world on one dance floor, every creature in the same groove, and then you. Glad, a little absurd, impossible to sit still through: a party, never a lecture.

**References.**

- Chic, “Good Times” (Risqué, 1979): take the locked bass and chopped guitar, a rhythm section that grooves on its own.
- Earth, Wind & Fire, “September” (The Best of Earth, Wind & Fire, Vol. 1, 1978): take the joy and the horn stabs.
- The Weather Girls, “It's Raining Men” (Success, 1983): take verses told close and low that break open into a big chorus.

**Dynamic arc.**

| Verse 1 | 7–22 | light | talky voice |
| Chorus 1 | 23–32 | full | horns, harmony |

| Verse 2 | 37–52 | light | as verse 1 |
| Chorus 2 | 53–62 | full | as chorus 1 |
| Break | 63–70 | medium | horns: verse tune |
| Bridge | 71–84 | strong | voice higher |
| Last Chorus | 85–96 | full | two bars longer |
| Outro | 97–104 | light | band to the end |

**Non-negotiables.**

- ♩ = 116, in the pocket; no push into the choruses.
- The title as written every time: “Ev-” on beat 4 of the bar before the chorus, the rest of “Everything” on its downbeat.
- The harmony voice below the lead, never above.
- Horns short and dry; bass and kick locked, four on the floor.
- The last chorus’s last bar: A D A.

**Mix priorities.** Voice in front and centre. Kick and bass loud and locked: a dance record. Guitar left; horns right, up front in the break. Harmony tucked under the lead. Nothing covers the title line.

### Session notes

- Key: A major. Chords bar by bar are on the number chart and above the lead sheet.
- Range: voice A2 to B3; harmony E2 to G♯4.

- Blend and panning: voice centre; harmony slightly right; rhythm guitar left; horns right; bass and kick centre.
- Every part can be played by one player at once; no extra passes are needed.
