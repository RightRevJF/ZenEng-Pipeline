The tests below are read on a song's mean over its builds. A single kept song has two judged windows, and when it is read the same way it seldom passes them. The figures use the kept windows' standard deviations by section (zeneng_v122_whole_song.json). Each kept song is taken as two windows.

- **400 / 408 (turns per move within 0.04 of 0.587).** A kept song's mean has a spread of 0.10, so about 30% of kept songs pass.
- **424 (fourths and fifths between the collections' means, or within a fifth of a sd of the nearer).** A kept song's mean has a spread of 0.05 against a band of ±0.02, so about 30% pass.
- **424 / 456 (the window's last note on the tonic, 27.7–32.9%, or within 0.09 of them).** A kept song's two windows read 0, 0.5 or 1, so no kept song passes. The test means something only on the mean of many builds.
- **453 (the window's range inside the kept middle half).** Read window by window, half of the kept windows pass, by the middle half's definition. Read on a song's mean, a kept song's spread is 2.7 semitones, against a middle half of 8–13.
- **415 / 459 / 331 (verse 2's median share within ten points of 50%).** Rolling Stone's own median share is 0.375 (Window 142), so the collection itself fails by 12.5 points. Billboard's is 0.50.
- **390 / 413 (unit returns within ten points of the pooled kept rates).** A window either returns its unit or not, so a kept song reads 0, 0.5 or 1 and cannot be within ten points of 16–30%.

These tests are read on 1,000 builds, where the mean is stable, and are kept as they stand. They should not be read as tests a kept song would pass.
