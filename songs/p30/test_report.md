## The research (7 Nov), as the run's hit rules

- **Length and intro:** 2025's top-10 songs with staying power averaged 3:17 (against 3:29) and 11-second intros. Built: 2:51, with an 11.4-second intro.
- **Post-chorus hook:** in 63% of 2025's top 10. Built: the lick after choruses 1 and 2.
- **Repetition:** each extra chorus repetition raised the odds of a #1 (Nunes 2015). Built: the title sung seven times, the first by bar 12 (about 31 seconds).
- **Melody:** earworms have a common contour plus one distinctive leap (Jakubowski 2016). Built: the title cell rises and falls (A–D–B–D–A); the chorus's second line leaps a fifth to E4.
- **Harmony:** pleasure comes from predictable chords with one surprise (Cheung 2019). Built: I–IV–V–vi throughout, with one borrowed iv (G minor) and B♭ on "thumb".
- **Delivery and arrangement:** restraint, held notes against quick syllables, an intro guitar hook that returns in the vocal ("Choosin' Texas"). Built: the lick is the title cell up an octave; the chorus holds; the drop before the flip.
- **Novelty:** a fresh take on a familiar theme. The porch light is well worn ("Every Light in the House", "Meanwhile Back at Mama's", many AI-generated porch-light songs). The fresh angle is the phone app, and "Mama's Porch" on the screen.

## Findings

71. **The engine has no market layer.** The hit brief came from outside research: a length under about 3:20, a short intro, the title inside 30 seconds, a post-chorus hook, the chorus count and one surprise chord. **The run's port:** HIT_RULES in the intake, met by design and checked by hand. **Proposed fix 71:** an optional "commercial profile" in intake (target length, intro seconds, title-by seconds, chorus repetitions, post-chorus yes/no, ending type). The form question prints it, and consistency checks it against the record.
72. **Q9 bans words, not images.** The porch light passed every list, yet it is one of country's most-used images, and AI-generated porch-light songs are already common. **The run's port:** a search for the title and central image in released songs before drafting, and a fresh-angle question (the writer chose the phone app). **Proposed fix 72:** a prior-art step at intake. Search the title and central image; if well worn, require a "fresh angle" field before draft 1.
73. **A phrasal-verb particle at line end reads weak.** In "left the light ON", "on" was read unstressed. **The run's port:** a line-final "on" after "light" is stressed. **Proposed fix 73:** a line-final particle completing a phrasal verb (on, off, up, out, in, down, back) carries the stress. Fold it into fix 45's single stress function.
74. **The refit refuses when stress counts differ by two or more.** Verse 2's lines 1–2 differ from verse 1's by one (with a long pickup) and by three. Fix 59's refit refused both, so they were re-walked and verse 2 is not quite verse 1's tune. Refits caused 120 of 428 attempts' deaths. **Proposed fix 74:** when counts differ by more than one, merge two old stresses into one held note (or split one into two eighths) before refusing. Allow a pickup of up to five sixteenths at 84 or slower.
75. **No field for the hook foreshadowed.** Verse 1's last line sings the title cell's pitches in a compressed rhythm before the chorus sings it in full. **The run's port:** a kernel variant (same pitches, its own onsets). **Proposed fix 75:** a "foreshadow" field on a hook cell, a compressed copy placed at a verse end, checked for pitch identity.
76. **No field for the drop.** The research's subtraction (the band out before the last chorus) and the turn line sung over it are arrangement design. **The run's port:** hand rules in the build, plus consistency checks. **Proposed fix 76:** an arrangement "drop" field (bars, what remains, the fill into the next downbeat), read by the build, the chart's cues and consistency.
77. **No field for the ending's kind.** 2025's staying-power hits favoured short, abrupt or unresolved endings. The song ends on IV, ringing. **Proposed fix 77:** an "ending" field (resolved, open, abrupt) checked against the last bar's chord and the outro length.
78. **The demo cannot sound like country radio.** General MIDI has no pedal steel or fiddle, and the guide voice is a clarinet. For a song aimed at the market, the research says a real voice is needed before anyone outside hears it. **Proposed fix 78:** allow a country soundfont route (pedal steel, fiddle, banjo). For any song with a commercial profile, have the production notes call for a human vocal demo.
79. **v65 fixes used as ports:** 9 and 24 (stress), 13 and 56 (form lengths), 25 (voice options), 26 (ASCII track names), 29 (line-final stress on a beat), 30 (aimed notes exempt), 32 (fitted cells clear the pickup), 33 (MIDI tempo in quarters), 34 (spelling from the key: sharps, and B♭ from the borrowed chord), 36 (production notes in the songbook), 47 (breath ceiling 7.5 s), 48 (9.12 per four beats), 59 (refit). From song 29's proposals: 69 (the producer one-sheet).
