# At a glance

**What it's about.** A believer sings to God about the day his own song ends. He knows his life the way you know a tune you've hummed forever: a heart that never kept perfect time, a mother who swears he hummed before he talked, the flat high note from the third pew. One day he'll draw a breath to sing and the next line won't come. The song's answer is in its title. Lazarus came back to the same old town and died again; Christ walked out into a morning the walls can't hold, and kept his scars. So the song can stop, because the singer doesn't. The last chorus lands on the point of it all: God will sing him again, flat high part and all, "and it will still be me."

**How it goes.** Acoustic-driven modern Christian music at ♩ = 84, in G major, built on a steel-string guitar's soft sixteenth-note strum. The verses are wordy and quick, close to speech. The chorus opens out without getting big. The hook is the first two lines: "The song can stop" falls to the tonic and the voice stops for a beat; "But the singer doesn't stop" sings the same little figure a step higher and runs straight on into the next line. The bridge comes twice: the first time with guitar and voice alone, the second a whole step higher (A major), climbing to A4 on "Yes You do" before falling home. The band stays small throughout.

# Section by section

Pitches are where the voice sounds. Bar numbers count from the first bar of the intro.

## Intro (bars 1–4)

The acoustic guitar alone, a soft sixteenth-note strum over G, D, E minor and C.

## Verse 1 (bars 5–12)

| Bars | Chords | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 5–6 | G | The doctor says a healthy heart never keeps perfect time |
| 7–8 | Em C | Mama swears I hummed before I talked, and I've been humming ever since |
| 9–10 | G D | I sing flat on the high part from the third pew on the left |
| 11–12 | C D | But one day I'll draw a breath to sing, and the next line won't come |

Conversational and quick. Each line starts just after the downbeat on E4 and talks its way down through C4 and B3 in eighths and sixteenths, landing on beat 3 of its second bar. The Mama line is the wordiest, eighteen syllables in two bars: don't rush it, let the sixteenths fall where the words fall. In the last line "breath" drops suddenly to D3 (bar 11, beat 4), the lowest note of the song, before "the next line won't come" climbs back to D4. Guitar and voice only.

## Chorus 1 (bars 13–24)

| Bars | Chords | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 13–14 | C G | The song can stop |
| 15–16 | Am D | But the singer doesn't stop |
| 17–18 | Em C | You remember every note I ever sang |
| 19–20 | C D | Every heartbeat out of time |
| 21–22 | Em C | You will sing me again |
| 23–24 | D G | 'Cause the singer doesn't stop |

The hook. "The song can stop": "The" is a pickup on D4, then "song" (bar 14, held two beats), "can" and "stop" fall B3, A3, G3, and "stop" is short, followed by silence. "But the singer doesn't stop" climbs G3, A3, B3, D4, then sings the same figure a step higher on "does-n't stop" (C4, B3, A3), and "stop" is held right into "You remember" with no breath. The music does what the words say. "You remember every note I ever sang" reaches F♯4 on "-ber"; "Every heartbeat out of time" lands "time" on D4 on the downbeat of bar 20; "You will sing me again" leans on E4; and "'Cause the singer doesn't stop" comes home to G3. Bass and brushes come in here.

## Verse 2 (bars 25–32)

| Bars | Chords | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 25–26 | G | Lazarus walked out of the dark and back into the same old town |
| 27–28 | Em C | Just a few more years of breathing till they laid him in the ground |
| 29–30 | G D | But You walked out into a morning where the walls can't keep You in |
| 31–32 | C D | With the nail holes in Your hands so Thomas knew You by Your scars |

The verse tune again, with the words fitted to it: Lazarus, then the empty tomb, then Thomas. Let the weight grow line by line. "Nail holes" sits on E4, and "scars" lands on D4 on beat 3 of bar 32. The low electric guitar swells in under it; the brushes stay, without the kick.

## Chorus 2 (bars 33–44)

As chorus 1, with the electric guitar now in.

## Bridge (bars 45–56)

| Bars | Chords | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 45–46 | Em C | Not a copy, not a cover |
| 47–48 | G D | Not a stranger with my face |
| 49–50 | Em C | Every scar and every stumble |
| 51–52 | Am D | Kept and carried into grace |
| 53–54 | Em C | No part of me remembers all of me |
| 55–56 | D | But You do, oh You do |

The band drops out: guitar and voice. The bridge sits low, between G3 and E4. "Not a copy, not a cover" and "Not a stranger with my face" share a shape, as do the next two lines: start on G3 and lift to the line's last stress ("cover," "face," "stumble," "grace"). "But You do, oh You do" turns around D4.

## Bridge, again and higher (bars 57–72)

| Bars | Chords | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 57–58 | F♯m D | Not a copy, not a cover |
| 59–60 | A E | Not a stranger with my face |
| 61–62 | F♯m D | Every scar and every stumble |
| 63–64 | Bm E | Kept and carried into grace |
| 65–66 | F♯m D | No part of me remembers all of me |
| 67–68 | E | But You do |
| 69–70 | A | Yes You do |
| 71–72 | C D | You remember all of me |

The whole step up, to A major, for the first fourteen bars: the first five lines are the first bridge note for note, a step higher. Brushes and bass come back at bar 61, the kick at bar 65, and the electric grows. Then "But You do" rises by step, E4, F♯4, G♯4, and "Yes You do" a step above it, F♯4, G♯4, A4, with "do" held (bar 69). That A4 is the top of the song. "You remember all of me" falls back into G over C and D, ending on A3, and the last chorus comes in.

## Last chorus (bars 73–84)

| Bars | Chords | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 73–74 | C G | The song can stop |
| 75–76 | Am D | But the singer doesn't stop |

The chorus melody, full but plain: no gang vocals, no pads. Line 4 becomes "Even the high part I sang flat," its "flat" on D4. The last line is new: "And it will still be me," coming home to G3 on the downbeat of bar 84.

The guitar alone, the hook once more, and "'Cause the singer doesn't stop" home to G3. Bar 88 is one G chord, let ring.

# For each player

**Voice.** A tenor, D3 to A4. The verses are spoken-sung, quick and close; the words matter more than the notes. In the chorus, honor the silence after "The song can stop" and keep "stop" going into "You remember." The bridge is quiet the first time; the second time, the step up and the climb to A4 on "Yes You do" is the one big moment. Even there, sing it, don't belt it.

**Acoustic guitar.** A steel-string, E2–G4 in the demo: a soft sixteenth-note strum (down on each beat and its eighth, light up-strokes between), from the chord symbols. Alone in the intro, verse 1, the first bridge and the tag. In bars 57–70 the chords move up a whole step (capo 2 and the G shapes work).

**Drums.**

- *Intro, verse 1:* tacet.
- *Chorus 1:* brushes: kick on 1 and 3 (and a soft one on the "and" of 2), brush on 2 and 4, brushed sixteenths.
- *Verse 2:* brushes without the kick.
- *Chorus 2:* as chorus 1, with a cymbal swell into its first bar.
- *Bridge:* out.
- *Bridge, again:* brushes from bar 61, the kick from bar 65.
- *Last chorus:* as chorus 2, a little fuller, still plain.
- *Tag:* out.

**Bass.** A fingered bass, E1–E3, from chorus 1: the root on every chord change, a two-bar figure and its variants, steps into the next bar. Out for verse 1's guitar-and-voice and the first bridge; back at bar 61. Out for the tag.

**Electric guitar.** A low, clean electric, G3–F♯4: volume swells on sustained chord tones, two a bar, from verse 2 on (out for the first bridge). Underneath, never a lead line.

# Arrangement

Small on purpose. The song is one person talking to God, so it starts with one guitar and adds only what the words call for: bass and brushes when the chorus opens, an electric guitar's swell when Lazarus and Thomas arrive, nothing at all in the first bridge, and a slow build through the second, carried by the key change and the climb rather than by volume. The last chorus is the fullest moment and still plain. The tag hands the song back to the guitar.

## Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** Intimate and unafraid. A man facing his own death with a tune in his head and the conviction that the one who wrote it remembers every note. The specifics carry it: the doctor, Mama, the third pew, Thomas's hands. Nothing should sound like a stadium.

**The three references.**

- **Rich Mullins, "Hold Me Jesus"** (1993), the lead: an acoustic, unguarded confession from a man to God. Take the closeness and the plain voice.
- **Andrew Peterson, "Is He Worthy?"** (2018, on *Resurrection Letters, Vol. 1*): a medium-slow song about resurrection hope. Take the wordy verses that still sing, and the build that never turns bombastic.
- **Matt Maher, "Lord, I Need You"** (2013): about 75 BPM, guitar-led, sung close. Take the small-band pulse and the chorus that opens without getting loud.

**The dynamic arc.**

| Section | Bars | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro, verse 1 | 1–12 | p | guitar alone; the voice close |
| Chorus 1 | 13–24 | mp | bass and brushes |
| Verse 2 | 25–32 | mp ↗ | the electric's swells; the weight grows |
| Chorus 2 | 33–44 | mf | the band, still small |
| Bridge | 45–56 | p | guitar and voice |
| Bridge, again | 57–72 | mp ↗ f | a step up; the band grows; the climb to A4 |
| Last chorus | 73–84 | f | full but plain |
| Tag | 85–88 | p | guitar alone; one chord |

**The non-negotiables.** The beat of silence after "The song can stop," and "stop" held straight into "You remember." The first bridge with guitar and voice only. The whole-step lift and the climb to A4 on "Yes You do." The last line, "And it will still be me." A small band: no pads, no gang vocals, no big drums.

**The mix priorities.** The voice on top, dry and close, every word clear (the verses are dense). The guitar's strum is the bed; the bass and brushes sit under it. The electric is felt more than heard.

## Production brief

*The song.* An intimate, acoustic Christian song about death and resurrection: a life as a song that can stop, sung by a believer to the God who doesn't, and will sing him again, scars and wrong notes and all.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Rich Mullins, "Hold Me Jesus."** Words and music by Rich Mullins (1993); produced by Reed Arvin.
- **Andrew Peterson, "Is He Worthy?"** From *Resurrection Letters, Vol. 1*, written with Ben Shive (2018); listed as 4/4, medium slow, about 73 BPM.
- **Matt Maher, "Lord, I Need You."** From *All The People Said Amen* (2013); listed at 75 BPM in B♭.

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.

## Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** ♩ = 84, 4/4, a sixteenth-note feel. Click on.
- **Key.** G major; bars 57–70 in A major (the second bridge), then back to G.
- **Chords per bar.** As in the form table and the chart; where a bar has two chords, two beats each.
- **Range.** Voice D3–A4. Bass E1–E3. Electric G3–F♯4.
- **Tracking.** Guitar first (to a click), then bass and brushes, then the electric, then the voice. The verses may want the voice comped from several passes for the diction.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice up the middle, dry and close. Guitar slightly left; the electric right and back. Bass centred; brushes a natural stereo spread.
