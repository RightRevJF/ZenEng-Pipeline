## Findings against v64

1. **A pronoun-object tail is killed as a weak ending.** "I stand because of it" ends on the stressed CAUSE and an unstressed tail ("of it"), which sings like a feminine ending. The room killed it, and the writer kept it. A line-final object pronoun after a preposition ("because of it," "without it," "all of it") could be allowed when the stress before it falls on a full vowel; print the rule when it fires.
2. **27.21 has no riff (ostinato) kind, and v65's proposed motion row would fail one.** A riff repeats its phrase and chugs its root. The plain sequence (repeat, tonal, inverted, varied) varies by design, and the repeated-note ceiling (≤ 0.40) proposed for v65 fix 6 fails this riff at 0.55–0.59. Add an ostinato kind (one phrase, repeated; the motion row read on span alone).
3. **The push and the release aren't in the engine's vocabulary.** The intake's "the riff lands just ahead of the beat" has no field. The run wrote anticipated changes into the band rules by hand. 9.12 reads energy by event count, so a released, ringing chorus scores below a chugged verse. 9.12 could weigh velocity, register and sustain alongside events.
4. **The fill doesn't look ahead to an aimed note.** An aimed note (a title peak, a final allowance) is fixed after the walk, so the note before it can leap more than a fifth into it. This was half the first trial's deaths. The run's port skips any candidate more than the leap limit (or a tritone) from the next aimed note. Module 27 could state it.
5. **The form's length was misjudged at intake.** The form option said "about 4 minutes"; at ♩ = 104 with two bars a line, the form runs 3:00 even with a stripped fourth chorus. The run made the estimate, not v64, but the intake's form row could estimate length from tempo, lines and sections before it offers the option.
