## What the song is about

Things stand up because they are pushed from both sides. An arch over a river: every stone is leaning toward the ground, and trucks roll over it all day. A man on a wire between two towers, with wind on the left and wind on the right, walking it like a kitchen floor. Two beams locked at the ridge of a roof. Take one away and the other falls.

The song never explains this. It shows it, then the bridge turns it on the singer. Every bone in him is pulled two ways, and one muscle bends what the other straightens. He isn't standing in spite of the strain. He stands because of it. The last line pulls the hook and the title together: "push me, and I stand: that's what holds."

**Playing it:**

- **The tension is the sound.** The riff and the band always arrive a little early, pulling against the beat. Keep that push in every section.
- **The verses are wound tight; the choruses release.** Muted and locked in the verses; open, ringing and loud in the choruses.
- **The bridge builds the pressure, and chorus 3 drops it out.** Then chorus 4 hits with everything.

*Em Em · C D · Em Em · C D.* The riff guitar alone for four bars, then the band comes in on bar 5.

- **The push:** every bar's first note is played an eighth early, on the "and" of 4, and tied over (C3 into bar 3, D3 into bar 4). A D3 pickup leads back to the top.

## For the singer

**Rock anthem (male): E3–G4, A4 once.**

- **The verses sit low (E3–C4), in a speaking range.** Hold back. The chorus should feel like a door opening.
- **The chorus lifts to G4 twice: on "holds" at the start, and on "stand" at the end.** Keep everything else under it.
- **The bridge climbs to F♯4 on "stand."** Let the build carry you, but don't peak yet.
- **Chorus 3 is exposed,** with almost no band. Sing it close, almost to yourself.
- **The A4 on the last "stand" (bar 68) is the top of the song.** It's an open "a" vowel, held over the D chord. Then fall back to G4 for "holds" and hold it through bar 70.
- **Breathe** at each line end. Every line has at least a beat of air after it.

## Arrangement and production notes

- **The push is the identity.** The bass and guitar arrive on each new chord an eighth early. If they land on the beat, the song goes slack.
- **Contrast is the release.** Tight and muted in the verses; open and wide in the choruses. Don't let the verses get loud.
- **The bridge and chorus 3 are a pair.** Build the pressure, then take everything away. Chorus 4 should hit hard because of the gap before it.
- **The voice stays in front,** dry enough that every word is clear.
- **The last chord is E minor, not G.** The song ends on the riff's own key, still under tension.
