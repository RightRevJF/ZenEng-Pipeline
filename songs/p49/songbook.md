# At a glance

**What it's about.** A brother and a sister who grew up in the same house and drifted onto different roads. They kept it civil, kept it short, and he was saving the real conversation for some kitchen table when their parents got old. Then the hospital room. He knows that room: his work has walked him into it for a hundred other families, but never as the brother. The chorus is what he says to her: hold that thought, I'm still coming. She is only in the next room. Someone left the hall light on and the door is cracked, and this time no one calls lights out before the conversation is finished. The bridge is a child the night before his birthday: everything is already waiting in the next room, and the morning doesn't need him to believe in it to come. The last chorus is the morning. The tag is the conversation picking up where it stopped: "Go on. You were saying before."

**How it goes.** A fingerpicked ballad, ♩ = 72, for one voice and one steel-string guitar, with a cello from the second verse. G major, and the last chorus lifts a whole step to A. The guitar is in standard tuning with no capo, on open-string shapes that keep the top two strings ringing D and G through most of the G-major part of the song. Nothing in the guitar part is simplified: the bass walks, the sus chords resolve under held notes, and the intro and the tag both quote the chorus's last four notes on the top strings.

# Section by section

Pitches are where the voice sounds. Bar numbers count from the first bar of the intro.

## Intro (bars 1–4)

The guitar alone, fingerpicked, every string left to ring: Em7, Cadd9, G, then D. In bar 4 the top strings play D, C, B, A in quarter notes over the D chord. Those are the four notes the chorus will end on ("before it's done"), so the song has already said its last line once before anyone sings.

## Verse 1 (bars 5–12)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| Em7 Cadd9 | We kept it civil, kept it short, kept it far |
| G D | Two kids from the same house on different roads |
| Em7 Cadd9 | I was saving it for one kitchen when the folks got old |
| Am7 D | Your number's still in my phone, and I don't know where it goes |

Low and close, D3 to A3, in quick eighths and sixteenths, the way someone talks when he is keeping his voice down. The first line opens on E3 on the downbeat of bar 5 and ends on G3; the second starts on A3 in bar 7 and ends on F♯3. The last line ends on D3 on the "and" of beat 2 in bar 12, over the D chord: the verse stops on an open question, not at home.

## Chorus 1 (bars 13–20)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| G D/F♯ | Hold that thought, I'm still coming |
| Em7 Cadd9 | You're only in the next room |
| G D/F♯ | Somebody left the hall light on and the door cracked open |
| Cadd9 D | And this time no one calls lights out before it's done |

"Hold that thought" comes on the beat: "Hold" B3 on 1, "that" A3 on 2, and "thought" on G3 on beat 3 of bar 13, held two and a half beats, across the bar line. Under it the guitar's G turns to Gsus4 (a C on the second string) and back to G on beat 4, and the D in bar 14 starts as Dsus4, so the G the singer is holding stays a chord tone until he moves. "You're only in the next room" starts high on C4 in bar 15 and tumbles down quickly to G3, then leaves a long breath. The last line starts on D4 in bar 19 and ends by stepping down D4, C4, B3, A3 on "before it's done," landing on A3 on beat 3 of bar 20, over D. That is the 2nd of the key: the chorus ends unfinished, on purpose, every time until the last.

As chorus 1. The cello now plays a high line above the voice, holding under "Hold that thought" and answering in the gaps: it rises E4, G4 to A4 in bars 31–32 after "You're only in the next room."

| Am7 Em7 | The morning doesn't need him to believe it |

Back down to the verse's range, D3 to A3, and lighter: the guitar slows to quarter notes, let ring, and the cello holds low notes. ## Tag (bars 53–56, in A)

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| F♯m7 Dadd9 | Go on |
| A Dadd9 | You were saying before |

"Go on" on C♯4, quietly, in bar 53. "You were saying before" lifts to E4 on "saying" in bar 55 and comes to rest on B3 on the downbeat of bar 56, held the whole bar. In that last bar the guitar plays E, D, C♯, B on the top strings over Dadd9, arriving on the singer's B. The song ends on the 2nd of A over the IV chord: the conversation has started again, and it isn't over.

# For each player

**Voice.** D3 to E4. Sing the verses as if the room is quiet and someone is asleep nearby. Let the chorus open, but keep it tender rather than loud. Hold "thought" its full length and let the guitar move under it; hold "morning" the same way in the last chorus. Don't resolve the end of the chorus with your voice: A3 on "done" is meant to hang. Only the last chorus comes home. The tag is almost spoken; the last B3 can fade.

**Guitar.** Steel-string, standard tuning, no capo, fingerpicked throughout. The voice-and-guitar score has the whole song in notation and TAB, so one guitarist and one singer can play it complete. Shapes (low E string first; x = not played):

- G 320033 · Gsus4 3x0013 · G (resolving) 3x0003
- D/F♯ 200232 · Dsus4 x00233 · D x00232
- Em7 022033 · Cadd9 x32033 · Am7 x02013
- Esus4 022200 · E 022100
- In A: A x02220 · Asus4 x02230 · E/G♯ 4x2100 · F♯m7 2x2200 · Dadd9 x54230

The top two strings stay on D and G (third fret) through G, Em7, Cadd9 and Dsus4, so they ring across those changes. Hold each shape for its half bar and lift only to change.

- *Verses:* thumb on beats 1 and 3 (the chord's bass, then a second bass string), fingers on strings 3, 2, 1, then 3, 2, 3 between. Quiet.
- *Choruses:* pinch the top string with the bass on 1 and the second string with the bass on 3. The bass walks down G, F♯, E to C across the first four bars (where the D starts as Dsus4, its F♯ arrives at the half bar). In bar 1 of each chorus the C on the second string (Gsus4) comes on beat 3 and resolves to the open B on beat 4, under "thought."
- *Where the voice holds the fourth over D* (bars 14, 18, 30 and 34) play Dsus4 for that half bar and resolve to D when the singer moves; in the verses play the plain D, because the voice sings its F♯.
- *Bridge:* the bass on 1, then strings 3, 2, 1 in quarter notes, let ring. Bar 43: strum Cadd9 in quarters. Bar 44: strum Esus4 in eighths, building, and change to E on beat 4.
- *Last chorus (A):* pinch on 1 and 3, fullest. The bass walks down A, G♯, F♯ to D (in bar 46 the G♯ arrives at the half bar, after the Esus4). Asus4 to A under "morning" in bar 45; Esus4 in the first half of bar 46 and the second half of bar 50, where the voice holds A.
- *Intro bar 4 and tag bar 56:* the top-string figure, D C B A and then E D C♯ B, in quarter notes over the bass.

**Cello.** Tacet until verse 2, then:

- *Verse 2 (bars 21–28):* whole notes, a falling line under the voice: E3, C3, B2, A2, G2, C3, A2, D3.
- *Chorus 2 (bars 29–36):* above the voice, in the tenor clef: D4, then G4 to F♯4 (taking the suspension under "thought"), rising E4, G4 to A4 in the gap after "the next room," and ending on F♯4 against the voice's A3.
- *Bridge (bars 37–44):* low and held: A2, B2, C3, B2, A2, G2, E2, and E2 again with a swell into the key change.
- *Last chorus (bars 45–52):* half notes, singing. E4 then A4 held over the bar into bar 46, falling to G♯4 with the guitar's resolution; C♯5, B4, A4 in bar 48 (the top of the song, in the gap after "the next room"); ending E4 to C♯4 under the voice's last A3.
- *Tag (bars 53–56):* A3, F♯3, E3, D3, the last D left to ring with the guitar.

# Arrangement

The song is built so a singer and a single guitar can play it whole and lose nothing essential. The guitar carries the harmony, the bass line and the song's two quotations: the chorus ending, played before anyone sings it and again at the very end, arriving on the singer's last note. It also listens: wherever the voice holds the fourth over the D (or, in A, over the E), the guitar plays the sus chord and resolves only when the voice moves on. The cello is the second voice that arrives in the second verse, as the hospital room does. It sings above the chorus, drops low for the bridge, swells into the key change and takes the suspension over "morning." Every chorus ends open on the 2nd until the last one, which lands home; the tag reopens the door.

## Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** A brother at his sister's bedside, everything he meant to say still unsaid, and the conviction that the conversation is interrupted, not ended. Hushed and unguarded in the verses, aching in the chorus, open and warm by the last chorus. It should make people cry and leave them hopeful.

**The three references.**

- **Eric Clapton, "Tears in Heaven"** (1991): a father singing to the son he lost, on fingerpicked acoustic guitar, with inverted chords so the bass moves smoothly. Take the intimacy, the guitar carrying the whole song, and the walking slash-chord bass.
- **Jason Isbell, "If We Were Vampires"** (2017): a quiet acoustic song about mortality and the limited time two people have, with expressive guitar picking and a close harmony voice. Take the plain, conversational delivery and the restraint.
- **Sufjan Stevens, "Fourth of July"** (2015): a hushed, spare conversation at a dying mother's hospital bed. Take the bedside honesty and the quiet; not the instrumentation.

**The dynamic arc.**

| Section | Bars | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–4 | p | the guitar alone; the chorus's last four notes on top |
| Verse 1 | 5–12 | p | low and close |
| Chorus 1 | 13–20 | mp | pinched, the bass walking |
| Verse 2 | 21–28 | mp | the cello enters, low |
| Chorus 2 | 29–36 | mf | the cello high above the voice |
| Bridge | 37–44 | p ↗ | quarters, let ring; the strummed build into the key change |
| Last chorus | 45–52 | f | up a step, fullest; the cello at its top |
| Tag | 53–56 | p | the door reopened; ends on the 2nd, over the IV |

**The non-negotiables.**

- The guitar plays the arrangement as written: open strings ringing, the walking bass, the sus chords resolving under the held notes. Don't simplify it.
- "Thought" (and "morning" in the last chorus) held the full two and a half beats.
- Every chorus but the last ends unresolved on A3 over D. Only the last chorus lands home.
- The key change comes out of the bridge's E chord, with no gap.
- The tag ends open: the voice on the 2nd, over Dadd9. Don't add a final A chord.
- The words stay as written.

**The mix priorities.** The voice close and dry enough to hear breath. The guitar wide and present, so the open strings ring into each other. The cello behind the voice in the verses, then forward in the high lines.

## Production brief

*The song.* A fingerpicked ballad: a brother sings to his estranged sister, who has died, and holds the conversation open. It is a tearjerker with a hopeful ending.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Eric Clapton, "Tears in Heaven."** Written with Will Jennings about the death of Clapton's four-year-old son, Conor; released on the soundtrack of the 1991 film *Rush*. Fingerpicked acoustic guitar; a guitar transcription notes the chords are inverted so the bass moves smoothly. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tears_in_Heaven))
- **Jason Isbell and the 400 Unit, "If We Were Vampires."** From *The Nashville Sound* (2017). A soft acoustic love song about mortality, with expressive acoustic picking and Amanda Shires's harmonies; it won the 2018 Grammy for American Roots Song. ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/1941868/jason-isbell-the-400-unit-if-we-were-vampires/music), [Nashville Scene](https://www.nashvillescene.com/music/features/considering-jason-isbell-s-best-songs/article_e9999985-9324-5388-b0bd-d116ff732c49.html))
- **Sufjan Stevens, "Fourth of July."** From *Carrie & Lowell* (2015), the album written after his mother's death in 2012. The song is built as a conversation with his dying mother in hospital; spare and hushed, with minimal keyboard. ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/sufjan-stevens/fourth-of-july))

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.

## Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** ♩ = 72, 4/4. A click is fine for tracking, but the guitar should breathe; a slight push into each chorus is welcome.
- **Key.** G major (one sharp) for bars 1–44; A major (three sharps) for the last chorus and the tag, bars 45–56. The bridge's E chord (bar 44) brings the G♯.
- **Chords per bar.** As in the form table, the chart and the score. Where a sus chord resolves inside a bar, the score shows both halves.
- **Range.** Voice D3–E4. Guitar E2–G4. Cello E2–C♯5 (the high lines in the tenor clef).
- **Tracking.** Guitar and voice together if possible, so the held notes and the sus resolutions breathe together. Then the cello, to the guitar.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice centre. Guitar slightly left and wide. Cello slightly right.
