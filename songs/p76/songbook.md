# They Did the Right Thing (first edition): songbook and production notes

Words and music: Jason Bryant

## At a glance

**What it is about.** Two people who work in the same building, married to others, meet in a parking garage. Then one of the spouses walks in, reads the messages, and asks for it to end. They end it. They go home, keep their vows and stay, as decent people do. The song does not argue with that. It only says that doing the right thing and being gone can be true at once.

**How to play it.** A minor, ♩ = 72, 4/4, dark indie folk-rock. 121 bars, about 6:43. One tenor (G3 to A4) with a harmony voice in the choruses; strummed guitar, bass and drums. Each chorus ends on a long held E4 while the bass walks down by half steps. The bridge drops to voice, guitar and bass, stops dead, and the final chorus comes in all together.

## For each player

**Voice.** A tenor, G3 to A4. Tell the verses plainly, like a report; the pre-choruses lean in. The song's top note, A4, comes on “again” (verse 1, bar 12); “quiet” (verse 2, bar 54); “part” (verse 2, bar 54). Every chorus ends “And still be gone” with “gone” held on E4: 2 bars in the first two choruses, 3 in the last. Hold it steady and level while the chords move under you; do not swell. The bridge is close and quiet.

**Harmony voice.** A lower voice (a baritone suits it), with the lead in the pre-choruses and choruses, in its rhythm and on its words; 61 in 100 of its notes sit a third to a fifth under the lead. It drops out on the held E4s, in the bridge and in the tag. Range A2 to F4.

**Guitar.** Steady eighth-note strums from the chord names, the whole song. Under each held E4 the chords walk down from E to E/D♯; after the first chorus on to E7/D and Am/C, after the second back to E for the bridge, and after the last on to E7/D, A/C♯ and C. Softer in the bridge; stop dead on beat 3 of bar 92; come back on the downbeat of bar 93. The tag is you and the voice, fading; the outro you alone, ending on A minor.

**Bass.** Quarter notes on the roots, as written. Under each held E4 walk down by half steps from E. Softly in the bridge; stop on beat 3 of bar 92; out for the tag and outro. Range E1 to G2.

**Drums.** Kick, snare and eighth-note hi-hat. Out for the whole bridge (bars 85–92): no fill into the final chorus, which you start with a crash on its downbeat (bar 93). Out for the tag and outro.

## For the singer

Breathe between lines: the phrases run 5.0 seconds at the median, the longest stretch without half a beat's rest 12.1 seconds (the held E4s). Take a full breath before each “And still be gone”: the hold is long, and it should end because you let it go, not because you ran out. Take no side with or against the people in the song. The verses are what happened; the chorus is what decent people do; the last line is the cost.

## The arrangement

The band plays it straight and steady, like the people in it. The choruses are full, but each ends on one held note while the bass slides down under it, so the chorus keeps sinking after the words stop. The bridge strips to voice, guitar and bass, the quietest part of the song, and stops dead for two beats in bar 92. The final chorus comes back all at once, nothing in the bar before, and its held E4 lasts three bars over the whole descent: E, D♯, D, C♯, C. The tag is the voice and the guitar alone, fading, and the guitar ends on A minor without resolving.

### Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** Two people who did what they should, told without blame and without comfort. The listener should feel the decency and the loss at the same time, and leave unresolved, the way the song ends.

**References.**

- Jason Isbell, “Elephant” (Southeastern, 2013): take how a heavy story is told as a plain character sketch, with no sermon.
- Bon Iver, “Skinny Love” (For Emma, Forever Ago, 2007): take the close, dry acoustic guitar and voice, and a hurt that stays steady in a minor key.
- Damien Rice, “The Blower's Daughter” (O, 2002): take the slow, minimalist pace and the long held lines that let the room fill in.

**Dynamic arc.**

| Sections | Bars | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro, verses | 1–18, 45–60 | light | the story, plain |
| Pre-choruses | 19–26, 61–68 | strong | leans in |
| Choruses 1, 2 and turns | 27–44, 69–84 | full | E4 held, the bass walks down |
| Bridge | 85–92 | quietest | voice, guitar, bass; silent from beat 3 of bar 92 |
| Final chorus | 93–111 | full, the top | all in on the downbeat; E4 held three bars over E, D♯, D, C♯, C |
| Tag, outro | 112–121 | soft, fading | voice and guitar; the guitar alone to A minor |

**Non-negotiables.**

- ♩ = 72, steady; no push.
- Every chorus's “gone” held on E4 (bars 40, 82, 106; 2, 2 and 3 bars), the bass walking down by half steps under it; the tag's last word held on E4 too (bar 116).
- The bridge: voice, guitar and bass only, the quietest part of the song.
- All stop on beats 3 and 4 of bar 92, no fill; the final chorus all in on its downbeat.
- The tag: voice and guitar only, fading. End on A minor; no cadence.

**Mix priorities.** The voice in front and close, the words always clear. The guitar just left of centre; bass and kick together, felt under the holds. The harmony voice right and behind the lead. Nothing may cover a held E4; the bass's half steps under it must be heard. The stop in the bridge must be silent: no reverb tails left hanging.

### Production brief

**The song.** “They Did the Right Thing”, words and music by Jason Bryant: dark indie folk-rock in A minor at ♩ = 72, 6:43, for a tenor with a harmony voice, strummed guitar, bass and drums.

**References** (checked): Jason Isbell, “Elephant” (2013); Bon Iver, “Skinny Love” (2007); Damien Rice, “The Blower's Daughter” (2002). What to take from each is on the one-sheet.

### Session notes

- Tempo and click: ♩ = 72 throughout, 4/4. The voice enters in bar 2.
- Key: A minor. Chords bar by bar are on the number chart and above the lead sheet.
- Range: voice G3 to A4; harmony A2 to F4.
- Tracking: guitar, bass and drums together for the feel; then the voice; the harmony voice last.
- The stop (bar 92, beats 3 and 4): track it with the band, not as an edit, so the final chorus's entry is played together.
- Blend and panning: voice centre; guitar slightly left; harmony slightly right; bass and kick centre; hi-hat a little right.
- Every part can be played by one player at once.
