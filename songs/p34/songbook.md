# Swallowed Up

*Words and music: Jason Bryant*

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Style | Death metal: two rhythm guitars (the same riff, left and right), a lead guitar, five-string bass, drums, and a harsh voice |
| Voice | Harsh from start to finish, with no sung pitch. The verses are growled low. The choruses and the taunt are shouted in the middle of the voice so the words come through. The last "Swallowed up!" is a high scream. The singer is a minister burying his own father |
| Tempo | ♩ = 200 in 4/4. The slowdown (bars 113–128) is a true drop to ♩ = 100 |
| Length | 152 bars, about 3:22 |
| Key | B minor. Guitars tuned B E A D F♯ B (low string B); bass with a low B string |
| Feel | A palm-muted chug in the verses, blast beats in the choruses, a gallop under the solo, then the slowdown and the breakdown before the last chorus |

## What the song is about

A November burial in the rain. The speaker is the minister at the grave, and the man in the casket is his own father. He holds his father's Bible, margins worn to cloth, the psalms gouged with pencil. His father drilled these words into him, and now he has to read them into the hole.

In the second verse he throws the dirt. "Earth to earth. Ashes. Dust." The lid booms back like a fist on the door at three in the morning. His mother turns her face into his coat, and the backhoe waits by the hedge.

In the third verse he steps to the edge and looks the grave in the throat: clay walls, roots like nerves, rain on the vault, fake grass over the spoil heap. Take him, he tells it. Lock your jaw. Roll on the stone. But he has read the end, and the stone gets moved. You don't get to keep this one.

The chorus is the grave: "Clay jaw. Stone throat. Never says 'Enough!'" In the slowdown he turns Paul's taunt on it, "O death, where is your sting?", and in the last chorus the words turn around. "Death is swallowed up," and the grave that never said enough has finally had enough.

**Playing it:**

- **Everything starts from low B.** Riffs A and B are a palm-muted pedal on the low open string with open power-chord accents. The accents land on the notes that grind against B: C (a half step up) and F (the tritone).
- **Every riff changes on its fourth time through.** The change carries the most tension, and it resolves on the next downbeat.
- **The verses are tight; the choruses explode.** The verses are a sixteenth-note chug with the kick doubling the guitar. The choruses are tremolo-picked over a blast beat.
- **The slowdown is real.** The tempo halves to ♩ = 100. Bar 120 ends with a beat of silence, then the breakdown hits.
- **The voice has no pitch.** It's a rhythm instrument locked to the guitar's accents. Only the shouted lines need to be understood.

## The song, section by section

The voice line gives rhythm and register, not pitch. "Low," "mid" and "high" are the three heights of the harsh voice. Bar and beat numbers come from the score.

### Intro · bars 1–16

*Riff A, on B.* Bars 1–8: guitar 1 alone. Bar 1: palm-muted low B on beat 1 and the "and" of 1, open F5 on beat 2, two muted B sixteenths, open E5 on beat 3, muted B, then C5 held on beat 4. Bar 2: the same start, then G5 and F♯5. On its fourth time through (bar 8), bar 2 becomes G5, F♯5, F5, C5, the half step up resolving to B on the next downbeat. Bar 9: guitar 2 and bass in; floor tom and china on the open accents. Bar 13: the full kit in normal time. Snare fill in bar 16.

### Riff · bars 17–24

*Riff B, on B.* A one-bar sixteenth chug: B5 on beat 1, two muted B sixteenths, D5 on beat 2, three muted Bs, C5 on the "and" of 3, two muted Bs, F5 on the "and" of 4. Every fourth bar (20, 24) changes: B5 on beat 1, six muted sixteenths, G5 held on beat 3 falling to F♯5 on beat 4. Double time, with the kick doubling every guitar stroke and the snare on the "and" of each beat.

### Solo · bars 97–112

*Riff D: B5 B5 A5 A5 G5 G5 F♯5 F♯5, twice.* A gallop chug: an open chord on each beat with two muted sixteenths between. The solo starts on D5 and works in two-bar phrases. Its opening phrase comes back moved to fit the A and F♯ chords, and the line climbs and gets faster through bars 105–110. Its one top note, B5, falls in bar 110. It ends on A♯4 over F♯5 in bar 112, the leading tone into the slowdown's B. The solo sticks to B minor, with C and F as passing colour and A♯ over the F♯ chord.

### Slowdown · bars 113–120

| Riff | Line |
|---|---|
| E: B5 · C5 F5 (113–114) | O death, where is your sting? |
| E: B5 · C5 F5 (117–118) | O grave, where is your victory? |

- **Tempo:** ♩ = 100. A true drop, not a feel change.
- **Band:** long chords: B5 for a bar, then C5 and F5 (the half step and the tritone), B5, then G5 to F♯5, and the same again. Half time: snare on beat 3, china on the beat, the kick under every chord. Bar 120: F5, then C5 on beat 3, then **beat 4 silent**.
- **Voice:** shouted, mid. In bar 113, "death" falls on beat 2 and "sting" on the downbeat of bar 114. In bar 117, "grave" falls on beat 2, with "vic-to-ry" across bar 118.

### Breakdown · bars 121–128

| Riff | Line |
|---|---|
| F (121–122) | You'll split like a seed in the ground |
| F (125–126) | and give him up at dawn. |

- **Band:** guitars, bass and kick strike low B together on beat 1, the "a" of 1, the "and" of 2 and the "and" of 3, with silence between the strokes. Bars 124 and 128 add strokes and end on a C. - **Voice:** shouted on the strokes. "Split," "seed" and "ground" land on the unison hits, and so do "give," "up" and "dawn."

## For the players

- **Rhythm guitars 1 and 2:** the same part, left and right. Guitar 1 alone for bars 1–8. Every palm-muted note is the low open B unless the riff moves its centre (bars 73–80, on C). The open accents are power chords with the octave (root, fifth, octave). The chorus tremolo is in two-note power chords. Keep the muted notes short and the accents ringing for their full value. In the breakdown, the strokes are short with dead silence between them.
- **Lead guitar:** the solo (bars 97–112), as written, ending on A♯ into bar 113. In the last chorus (bars 137–144), double the tremolo two octaves up.
- **Bass:** five-string with a low B. Play the guitar's lowest note an octave down, in the same rhythm, mutes included. Tacet for bars 1–8.
- **Drums:**
  - **Intro:** tacet for bars 1–8. Bars 9–12: floor tom and china on the guitar's open accents. Bars 13–16: normal time (kick 1 and 3, snare 2 and 4, ride eighths), with a fill in bar 16.
  - **Riff, verses, solo:** double time, with the kick on every guitar stroke and the beats, snare on the "and" of each beat, ride on the beat.
  - **Choruses:** the blast. Kick on the even sixteenths, snare on the odd ones, ride eighths, crash every two bars.
  - **Slowdown:** half time, china on the beat.
  - **Breakdown:** kick on the strokes, snare once every two bars, the cymbal thinning to nothing.
  - **Riff 2:** normal time, then double time.
  - **Outro:** the strokes with crash and china.
  - **Level:** keep it even. The heavy sections get their contrast from the texture, not from playing louder.

## For the singer

**Harsh voice, no pitch, three heights.**

- **Verses: the low growl.** Darken the stressed vowels toward the back of the mouth, so "clay" moves toward "cloh" and "rain" toward "rohn." Open up on one word per line, the one on the strongest hit: for example "slack," "drumming," "father," "pencil," "fist," "edge." Close the vowel down again at the end of the line. Let each line end before its second bar is out; the breath belongs to the riff's restart.
- **Choruses and the taunt: the mid shout.** These lines have to be understood, so keep the words' own vowels. One hit per beat, as written.
- **The breakdown lines** sit on the band's unison strokes. Hit with them, not between them.
- **The last "Swallowed up!" (bar 143)** is the one high scream. Brighten it: "swallowed" toward "swaa-lowed," "up" toward "ap."
- **The words to land** are the title, "Clay jaw. Stone throat.," "O death, where is your sting?" and "Death is swallowed up!" Most listeners won't catch the verses, so the lyric sheet carries them.

## Producer one-sheet

**Emotional target.** Grief that turns into defiance. The verses should feel cold, wet and close, the choruses like the ground opening, and the slowdown like a man standing over the grave and taunting it. The listener should come out of the last chorus with the fear turned into strength.

**Where it sits.** Between a naturalistic and a hyperreal production, nearer the naturalistic end: a real kit and real amps, early-1990s in spirit. Allow just enough reinforcement that the kick and the palm mutes stay distinct at ♩ = 200. The slowdown and the breakdown should breathe and should not be edited to the grid.

**References**

- **Death, "Pull the Plug" (1988):** a death metal chorus a crowd can shout back. Take the clear, chantable title line over a harsh voice.
- **Suffocation, "Infecting the Crypts" (1991):** blasts and heavy breakdowns in one song. Take the drop from speed into the breakdown.
- **Mortification, "Scrolls of the Megilloth" (1992):** Christian death metal, with the genre's dark themes written from a Christian point of view. Take its stance: the lexicon stays dark, and the enemy is death.

**The four targets**

| Target | For this song |
|---|---|
| Impact | The breakdown strokes (bars 121–128) and the downbeat of each chorus. Guitar, bass and kick must land as one hit |
| Energy | Blast choruses over a chugging double-time verse. The arc rises to the last chorus; the breakdown is the peak of weight, not of speed |
| Precision | Two guitars tight to each other and to the kick in the verses, where the kick doubles the guitar. Tremolo even across the bar |
| Extremity | The tritone and the half step in every riff, a harsh voice throughout, blasts at ♩ = 200. Don't soften any of these; the clarity budget is spent on the shouted lines, not on smoothing the sound |

**Dynamic arc**

| Section (bars) | Weight | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Intro (1–16) | building | One guitar, then layers |
| Verses (25–40, 49–64, 73–88) | heavy, tight | Palm-muted chug, double time |
| Choruses (41–48, 65–72, 89–96) | full | Tremolo and blast |
| Solo (97–112) | driving | Gallop, the lead on top |
| Slowdown (113–120) | wide | Half the tempo, long chords |
| Breakdown (121–128) | the heaviest | Unison strokes, silence between, the cymbal thinning |
| Last chorus (137–144) | the fullest | The blast, the lead doubling, the scream |
| Outro (145–152) | ending | Strokes, then one ringing chord |

**Non-negotiables**

1. The slowdown is a real tempo change to ♩ = 100, and bar 120's last beat is silent.
2. In the breakdown, there is silence between the strokes: no tails, no ghost notes.
3. The title, the chorus and the taunt are understood. Everything else may be growled.
4. Two rhythm guitars play the same part, hard left and hard right.
5. Every riff keeps its fourth-bar change.
6. The song ends in hope, on the last chorus's "Death is swallowed up!"

**Mix priorities**

1. **The shouted words:** "Swallowed up!" and the taunt sit on top.
2. **Kick and palm mutes:** one percussive sound in the verses.
3. **The guitars:** wide, with the riff's open accents clear against the mutes.
4. **The snare in the blast:** present but not machine-gun flat. The velocities vary.
5. **Never cover:** bar 120's silent beat, the breakdown's gaps, the final ringing B5.

## Production brief

**The song.** A death metal burial song: a minister buries his father in the November rain, throws the dirt, stares down the grave, and in the end taunts death with Paul's own words. It is a harsh voice over two low-tuned guitars, bass and drums. It has eight riffs: palm-muted verses, blast choruses, a solo over a gallop, a true slowdown to half tempo, a unison breakdown, and a last chorus where the words turn around.

**References**

- **Death, "Pull the Plug," from *Leprosy* (1988).** Its chorus is often described as the most crowd-shouted in Death's catalogue, a catchy title line over brutal music. This song's chorus works the same way: the title shouted over the blast.
- **Suffocation, "Infecting the Crypts," from *Effigy of the Forgotten* (1991).** Suffocation are known for sharp riffs, tempo changes from heavy breakdowns to fast blasting parts, and breakdowns that later gave rise to deathcore. That is the model for bars 113–128.
- **Mortification, "Scrolls of the Megilloth" (1992).** A landmark of Christian death metal, whose lyrics take the genre's themes from a Christian point of view. That is the stance of this song: the dark imagery is kept, and death is the enemy.

**Non-negotiables**

1. **The slowdown:** bar 113 at ♩ = 100; bar 120, beat 4 silent; the breakdown in bars 121–128.
2. **The words that must be understood:** the chorus, the taunt, "Death is swallowed up!"
3. **The riffs:** each riff's fourth-bar change; the half step (C) and the tritone (F) against B.
4. **The ending:** the last chorus turns the words; one B5 rings out at bar 152.

**Range**

- **Voice:** no pitch. Low growl (verses), mid shout (choruses, slowdown, last chorus), one high scream (bar 143).
- **Lead guitar:** the solo D4 to B5, with the top B5 once, in bar 110. The last-chorus doubling runs B3 to F4.
- **Rhythm guitars:** low B1 up to the octave above the accented roots.

**Tracking**

1. **Drums and bass** to the click, with the tempo change included.
2. **Rhythm guitars:** two separate performances of the same part, panned hard left and right. Don't copy one to the other.
3. **Lead:** the solo and the last-chorus doubling as their own pass.
4. **Voice:** the growled verses, the shouted lines and the scream as separate passes. The shouted lines can be doubled for clarity.

**Blend and panning**

- **Rhythm guitars** hard left and right; **bass** centre, locked to the kick; **lead** right of centre; **voice** centre, in front.
- **The blast:** little reverb on the drums and guitars.
- **The breakdown:** dry and close, with the gaps truly silent.
- **The end:** let the last B5 ring out naturally.

**About the demo.** The demo is a reference for rhythm, form and the lead's pitches, not for tone. Its guitars and drums are General MIDI sounds, and its harsh voice is a stand-in: shaped noise that follows the words' rhythm, height and vowel. It is not a performance.
