> **The turn.** The refrain is sung to the same notes in all four verses. In verse 4 one word changes: "son" becomes "song." Don't change the delivery to mark it; the word does the work. The peak of the song comes after the turn, on "wake" in bar 97.

## Production brief

**The song.** A grown son visits a parent who no longer knows him. Every visit ends with him saying who he is. The parent's old lullaby is the chorus, and in the end the parent sings it with him: they've lost him, but not the song. It's one tenor and one fingerpicked guitar, recorded close and quiet. It should sound like a room, not a stage, and it should never reach for tears.

**References**

- **Glen Campbell, "I'm Not Gonna Miss You" (2014):** the subject, sung plainly. His last recording, made after his Alzheimer's diagnosis. Take its restraint: no pleading in the voice.
- **Jason Isbell, "If We Were Vampires" (2017):** an acoustic love song about one of a couple being left alone. Take the close, dry guitar and vocal sound, and the way it states hard facts gently.
- **Eric Clapton, "Tears in Heaven" (*Unplugged*, 1992):** a fingerpicked acoustic ballad about loss, one voice over one guitar. Take the picking that stays steady underneath while the voice carries the grief.

**Non-negotiables**

1. ♩ = 76 in 3/4 throughout. A slight easing in the last two bars is fine; no other tempo changes.
2. **Two instruments only:** voice and guitar. No pads, strings or piano.
3. **The intro plays the lullaby's tune first,** so the listener knows it before it's sung.
4. **The refrain is sung the same way every time,** including "I'm your song" in bar 85.
5. **Verse 4 drops to one ringing chord a bar.** It's the quietest place in the song.
6. **The B♭4 on "wake" (bar 97) is the only note above A4.** It can crack a little; don't fix it in editing.
7. **The last F major chord rings out.** Don't fade it.

## Session notes

**Tempo and metre.** 112 bars, about 4:25. 3/4 at ♩ = 76. Use a quarter-note click, or a dotted-half click if the waltz feels stiff.

**Key and capo.** F major throughout. The guitar is capoed at fret 3 and plays D shapes:

| Concert | F | B♭ | C | Dm | Gm | Am |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shape | D | G | A | Bm | Em | F♯m |

**Range**

- **Voice:** tenor, E3–A4, with one B♭4 (the last "wake," bar 97).
- **Verses:** E3–C4, close and low.
- **Choruses:** C4–A4. "Dark" sits on A4 at bars 43 and 91.
- **The refrain every time:** "me" on C4, held; then "son" on F3.

**Tracking**

1. Record voice and guitar together in the same room if the players can. The song breathes better live.
2. If tracking separately, record the guitar first to the click, then the voice with the guitar in the headphones.
3. Keep every vocal take's verse 4 quiet. Don't comp it from louder takes.
4. For the last chorus, the singer can double lightly or not at all. It's "sung by both" in the story, not with a second voice.

**Blend and panning**

- Voice centre and in front, dry, with a short room.
- Guitar slightly off-centre. A stereo pair works: one mic at the 12th fret and one at the body.
- Use a little natural room reverb on both and nothing on the voice that you can hear as an effect.
- The verses should sound close enough to hear the breath.
