## Findings against v64

1. **11.2 has no tenor row.** The writer asked for a male tenor. The rows run from bass-baritone (B2–C4) through male (E3–F♯4) to rock anthem (E3–G4). The run set D3–A4, with B♭4 once. A tenor row, and a mezzo row to match, would complete the table.
2. **The title-cell rule assumes the title is sung.** "The Song You Sang Me" names the lullaby; no line carries it. The run designed the refrain and the chorus's cells first instead. 27.20 could fall back, in order: the title line, the refrain line, then the chorus's first line, printed when it fires.
3. **The writing room's placement in 3/4 finds one strong beat a bar.** "you're folding the same napkin by the window" scored 0.00 on strong-beat agreement and died, yet the melody walk set it with stresses on downbeats and beats. The room's walk moves a stress to the next beat (every quarter) but credits only the bar's downbeat. In 3/4 it could credit beats 2 and 3 at half weight, as 4/4 credits beat 3.
4. **Long lines and designed cells.** In a 24-eighth line, the walk rarely reaches two required downbeats (SLEEP on bar 1, DARK on bar 3). The minimum-length rule (the last syllable at n − 9 or later) also kills short lines outright: "your hand came up and kept the time," 8 syllables. The run hand-wrote the designed rhythms and exempted short lines. 27 could let a designed cell fix its anchor syllables and walk only the syllables between them, and scale the minimum to the syllable count.
5. **Unstressed line endings are clipped.** The duration rule caps every unstressed syllable at a quarter, including a line's last ("where you ARE," "you know you're HERE"). The lullaby's lines ended short. A line-final syllable on a downbeat should hold, stressed or not.
6. **Contrastive pronouns.** "it's ME" is stressed in the singing, but the lexicon reads "me" as a function word. The refrain's peak then failed the reduced-peak rule. This is the same family as song 22's negation focus (v65 fix 7). The stress rules could add a contrastive pronoun after "it's" or "it was," and before a full stop.
7. **No capo field.** The guitar's capo and shapes are a performance fact, but the engine has nowhere to put them. The run wrote concert pitch with a second line of capo chord names. The intake's instrument row could take a capo, and the lead sheet, chart and parts could print the shapes.
8. **27.21 has no quote kind.** A folk intro often plays the chorus tune before it's sung; here it's the point of the song. The run quoted the lullaby's lines directly. This sits beside v65 fix 10's riff kind. A quote kind would copy a sung section's tune, its floor read on chord tones only (the tune's own span stands).
