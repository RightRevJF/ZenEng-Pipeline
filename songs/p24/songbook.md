# The Song You Sang Me

*Words and music: Jason Bryant*

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Style | Folk ballad; one tenor and one guitar |
| Tempo | ♩ = 76 throughout |
| Metre | 3/4, a slow waltz |
| Length | 112 bars, about 4:25 |
| Key | F major. Guitar: capo 3, play D shapes |
| Voice | Tenor: E3–A4, B♭4 once (the last "wake") |
| Feel | A lullaby rock. Fingerpicked, close, unhurried; each line held across four bars, the way a lullaby breathes |

## What the song is about

A grown son visits a parent who is losing their memory. Each verse is a visit:

- **The first visit:** the parent folds the same napkin and asks if he has come to fix the heat.
- **The photographs:** the parent touches a face, says "she used to swim," and holds his hand the way they'd hold anyone's.
- **The wrong name:** the parent calls him by their brother's name. He writes his own name under the date on the board.

Every visit ends the same way: "It's me. I'm your son."

The chorus is the lullaby the parent used to sing to him: "Go to sleep, I've got the dark." In the last verse he sings it to them. Somewhere in the second line their hand comes up and keeps the time. They don't know his face, but they know the song. The refrain turns: "It's me. I'm your song." The last chorus is sung by both of them, and now the lullaby's promise is his.

The parent is never named as mother or father. Every listener puts their own there.

**Playing it:**

- **Don't push it.** This song works by holding back. Let the long notes hang.
- **The refrain is the same every time,** so the listener feels the weight build. Sing it plainly each time. Don't add emotion; it's already there.
- **Verse 4 is the quietest place in the song.** Then the last chorus opens up.

## The song, section by section

Each lyric line takes four bars of 3/4, and the chords of all four bars are shown beside it. Capo shapes are in brackets.

### Intro · bars 1–8

*B♭ B♭ F F · Gm Gm C C* (G G D D · Em Em A A). The guitar plays the tune of the chorus's first two lines on the top strings, with the bass on 1 and the chord on 2 and 3. The listener hears the lullaby before anyone sings it.

### Verse 1 · bars 9–24

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| F · F · B♭ · F | you're folding the same napkin by the window |
| Dm · Dm · B♭ · C | you ask me if I've come to fix the heat |
| F · Am · B♭ · B♭ | I pull my chair in close like I've always done |
| C · C · F · F | it's me. I'm your son. |

- **Melody:** low and close, F3–C4, nearly spoken. "Window" holds on B♭3, "heat" on G3 and "done" on G3.
- **The refrain:** "it's" is a pickup on A3, and "ME" lands on C4 at the start of bar 21 and holds. Breathe, then "I'm your" (A3, G3) leads down to "SON" on F3 in bar 23, held to the end of the line.
- **Guitar:** the waltz pattern in eighths:
  - the bass on 1, the root one bar and the fifth the next;
  - then the chord's three upper notes, up and back.

### Verse 2 · bars 25–40

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| F · F · B♭ · F | I brought the pictures from the lake in August |
| Dm · Dm · B♭ · C | you touch a face and say, "she used to swim" |
| F · Am · B♭ · B♭ | you hold my hand the way you'd hold anyone |
| C · C · F · F | it's me. I'm your son. |

- **Melody:** E3–C4. "Swim" falls to E3 and holds, the lowest note in the song.
- **Guitar:** as verse 1.

### Chorus 1 · bars 41–56 · the lullaby

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| B♭ · B♭ · F · F | go to sleep, I've got the dark |
| Gm · Gm · C · C | I'll keep it folded, keep it near |
| B♭ · B♭ · F · Dm | and if you wake and don't know where you are |
| Gm · C · F · F | I'll say your name till you know you're here |

- **Melody:** higher, from C4 to A4.
  - "Go to" is a pickup, and "sleep" falls on D4 at bar 41.
  - "Dark" lands on A4 at the start of bar 43, over F, and holds for most of two bars.
  - "Near" holds on E4.
  - "And if you" is a three-note pickup into "WAKE" on F4 at bar 49.
  - "Here" comes home on F4 at bar 55.
- **Guitar:** the same pattern, with the top note pinched together with the bass on beat 1.

### Verse 3 · bars 57–72

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| F · F · B♭ · F | you called me by your brother's name on Tuesday |
| Dm · Dm · B♭ · C | now you watch the birds come to the feeder |
| F · Am · B♭ · B♭ | I write my name in marker underneath the date |
| C · C · F · F | it's me. I'm your son. |

- **Melody:** E3–C4. "Date" holds on B♭3.
- **Delivery:** this is the hardest verse, so sing it the most plainly.

### Verse 4 · bars 73–88

| Chords | Line |
|---|---|
| F · F · B♭ · F | I sang it soft, and somewhere in the second line |
| Dm · Dm · B♭ · C | your hand came up and kept the time |
| F · Am · B♭ · B♭ | you didn't know my face, but you sang along |
| C · C · F · F | it's me. I'm your song. |

- **Melody:** F3–C4. "Time" holds on B♭3 for nearly two bars. "Along" holds on C4.
- **The turn:** the refrain has the same notes as every other verse, with one word changed: "son" becomes "song."
- **Guitar:** thin it out. One chord a bar, struck on 1 and left to ring.

### Chorus 2 · bars 89–104 · the lullaby, sung by both

The same words as chorus 1.

- **The top of the song:** "WAKE" in bar 97 goes up to B♭4 over B♭. The line climbs to it from E4 and F4, then comes down through F4 and G4 to "are" on E4.
- **Guitar:** the chorus pattern, a little fuller than the first time: pinch on beats 1 and 3.

### Outro · bars 105–112

*B♭ B♭ F Dm · Gm C F F* (G G D Bm · Em A D D). The guitar alone plays the tune of the chorus's last two lines, ending on F. The last chord rings.

## For the guitarist

- **Capo 3, D shapes:**

  | Written | F | B♭ | C | Dm | Gm | Am |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Play | D | G | A | Bm | Em | F♯m |

  The part on the lead sheet is written out in full at concert pitch.
- **The waltz pattern (verses 1–3):** six eighths a bar. The bass is on 1, the root one bar and the fifth the next. Then the chord's three upper strings go up and back: 1 2 3 2 1. Let everything ring.
- **The choruses:** the same pattern, with the highest note pinched together with the bass on beat 1. In the last chorus, pinch beat 3 as well.
- **Verse 4:** one chord a bar, struck on 1 and left to ring. Give the voice all the room.
- **Intro and outro:** play the lullaby's tune on the top strings, with the bass on 1 and a two-note chord on beats 2 and 3.
- **The last bar:** an F chord, let ring.

## For the singer

**Tenor: E3–A4, B♭4 once.**

- **The verses sit low (E3–C4).** Sing them close to the microphone, almost speaking. They're a son talking to his parent.
- **The refrain should come out the same every time.** "Me" on C4, held; a breath; then down to "son" on F3. The repetition is the point.
- **The chorus is a lullaby.** Even when it climbs to A4 on "dark," keep it soft, the voice you'd use beside a sleeping child.
- **"It's me. I'm your song" (bar 85)** uses the same notes as every refrain. Don't change the delivery: the word does the work.
- **The B♭4 on the last "wake" (bar 97)** is the only time the voice reaches past A4. It can almost break; let it.
- **Breathe** at each line end. Every line has room.

## Arrangement and production notes

- **Two instruments, nothing else.** Voice and guitar, recorded close.
- **The intro teaches the tune.** When the chorus arrives, the listener already knows it. When the parent sings along in verse 4, the listener does too.
- **Verse 4 is the bottom of the song:** one chord a bar. The last chorus then opens up, but never loud.
- **Let the last F ring out.** Don't fade it.
