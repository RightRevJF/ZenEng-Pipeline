# The Small Things: songbook and production notes

Words and music: Jason Bryant

## At a glance

**What it's about.** A late afternoon on the couch. The light lies across the floor, Sammie takes his post by the sliding glass door, a bumblebee bounces past, and Maggie hits the glass with both paws. There's no moral to it, the song says, and then it finds one anyway: somebody else is watching this too, and laughing, and God likes the small things. The bridge says it plainly: nobody sent me here; I just sat down on a Tuesday, and the holy showed up clear.

**How it goes.** Gentle acoustic folk, ♩ = 88 with a slow swing, D major, for a baritone. A fingerpicked acoustic guitar carries the whole song; a soft piano, an acoustic bass and light brushes come and go around it. The hook, "I think God likes the small things," climbs to the song's top note on "small" and holds it; "I think He always will" steps back down and lands home. The bridge drops to the guitar alone, stops for one beat, and rises into "the holy showed up clear." The last line of the song slows to a stop.

**Form (96 bars, about 4:22).** Every sung line takes two bars. Swing the eighths: long-short, gently.

**The singer.** A baritone, A2 to D4. The verses sit low (around D3); the choruses lift about 4 semitones (around F♯3); the top, D4, is the hook's held "small."

## Section by section

Pitches are where the voice sounds. Bar numbers count from the first bar of the intro.

## Intro (bars 1–4)

The acoustic guitar alone, fingerpicked and swung: the thumb on the root and fifth, the fingers between. In bars 3–4 the piano plays the hook's tune an octave up, softly, before anyone has sung it.

Plain and unhurried, B2 to B3. "Late afternoon" begins on D3 on the downbeat. The verse stays close to home, often repeating a note the way speech does ("Late afternoon comes rolling in" holds D3 for 3 syllables), so the story sounds told rather than sung. The second half of the verse (from bar 13) takes the first half's tunes again over the same chords; the acoustic bass enters there. "and doesn't even slow down" ends on E3, not home: the chorus answers it.

## Outro (bars 93–96)

The guitar picking, softer each bar; in bars 94–95 the piano echoes "I think He always will" an octave up; the last bar a D chord, let ring.

## For each player

**Voice.** A baritone, A2 to D4. Sing the verses like you're telling the person next to you on the couch what the cats just did: no push, a smile in it. Open up a little at the chorus, and lean on the held "small"; then let "I think He always will" come home easily. In the bridge come right down, nearly speaking, take the one beat of silence, and let "the holy showed up clear" rise on its own. Take your time on the last line.

**Harmony vocals.** Two parts, one above and one below, on the hook in every chorus and on every line of the last chorus (high E3–G4, low A2–B3). Blend under the lead.

**Acoustic guitar** (E2–F♯4). Steel-string, fingerpicked all the way through (thumb on the root and fifth on the beats, fingers on the upper chord notes between), with the eighths swung. A little fuller in the choruses. Alone in the bridge; stop for the one beat of silence at the start of bar 75.

**Piano** (D3–F♯5). The hook's tune in the intro; soft answers at the end of every other bar in verse 2; half-note chords in the choruses; out in the bridge; the hook's last line in the outro.

**Acoustic bass** (A1–A3). In halfway through verse 1; root on every chord change, walking lightly between; out in the bridge; back for the last chorus; a last low D in the outro.

**Drums.** Brushes only.

- *Intro, verse 1, verse 2, bridge, outro:* out.
- *Chorus 1:* brushes: kick soft on 1 and 3, rim on 2 and 4, ride in quarters.
- *Chorus 2:* brushes, snare on 2 and 4, ride in eighths (swung).
- *Last chorus:* as chorus 2, a touch more; follow the singer through the slowing last line.

## Arrangement

The song is an afternoon you don't want to end. The guitar never stops picking, so the whole song feels like one long, easy moment. Each chorus adds a little: piano, bass, brushes, and harmony on the hook. The bridge takes everything away except the guitar, then takes that away too for one beat, so "the holy showed up clear" arrives in real quiet. The last chorus is the fullest, and its last line slows down like the light going.

## Producer one-sheet

**The emotional target.** Contentment and quiet wonder. Nothing happens, and that is the point: a man notices an ordinary afternoon and suspects God is enjoying it too. Warm, a little funny, never preachy.

**The three references.**

- **Cat Stevens, "Morning Has Broken"** (*Teaser and the Firecat*, 1971): Eleanor Farjeon's hymn of praise for an ordinary morning, with Rick Wakeman's piano. Take the wonder at plain things and the piano's gentleness.
- **Simon & Garfunkel, "The 59th Street Bridge Song (Feelin' Groovy)"** (*Parsley, Sage, Rosemary and Thyme*, 1966): "Slow down, you move too fast." Take the unhurried delight and the light touch.
- **Iron & Wine, "Naked as We Came"** (*Our Endless Numbered Days*, 2004): a short, intimate acoustic song in close harmony. Take the closeness of the voice and the harmony on the hook.

**The dynamic arc.**

| Section | Bars | Level | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 1–4 | p | guitar alone; the hook's tune on piano |
| Verse 1 | 5–20 | p → mp | guitar and voice; bass in halfway |
| Chorus 1 | 21–36 | mp+ | piano, bass, brushes on the rim; harmony on the hook |
| Verse 2 | 37–52 | mp | guitar, bass, piano answers |
| Chorus 2 | 53–68 | mf | brushes on the snare |
| Bridge | 69–76 | pp → silence → p | guitar alone; one beat of silence |
| Last chorus | 77–92 | mf | the fullest; harmony on every line; the last line slows |
| Outro | 93–96 | p | guitar and the piano's echo, fading |

**The non-negotiables.** The held D4 on "small." "I think He always will" landing home on D3. The one beat of silence before "and the holy showed up clear," held open on A3. The swing, gentle throughout. The last line slowing to a stop. Nothing louder than mf.

**The mix priorities.** The voice close and warm, every word clear. The guitar's picking at the centre of everything, slightly left; the piano right and soft. Brushes as texture, never a beat you notice. In the bridge's silent beat, let the room be silent.


## Production brief

*The song.* A gentle folk song about watching two cats and a bumblebee on a lazy afternoon, and suspecting that God likes the small things too.

*References* (checked by search):

- **Cat Stevens, "Morning Has Broken."** From *Teaser and the Firecat* (1971); words by Eleanor Farjeon (a hymn first published in 1931) set to the traditional tune "Bunessan"; piano by Rick Wakeman; recorded March 1971 at Morgan Studios, London; 3:20.
- **Simon & Garfunkel, "The 59th Street Bridge Song (Feelin' Groovy)."** From *Parsley, Sage, Rosemary and Thyme* (Columbia, 1966); recorded August 16, 1966; 1:43; Joe Morello (drums) and Eugene Wright (double bass) of the Dave Brubeck Quartet play on it.
- **Iron & Wine, "Naked as We Came."** From *Our Endless Numbered Days* (Sub Pop, March 23, 2004), produced by Brian Deck; 2:32.

*Non-negotiables.* As on the one-sheet.

## Session notes

- **Tempo and click.** ♩ = 88 throughout, 4/4, swung eighths (a triplet feel); the click in quarters. The last line of the song slows (ritardando): follow the singer, no click.
- **Key.** D major. No note outside the scale in the voice.
- **Chords per bar.** As in the section tables; most bars hold one chord, some two (two beats each).
- **Range.** Voice A2–D4; harmony as above.
- **Tracking.** Guitar and a guide vocal to the click first; then bass and piano; then brushes; the lead vocal; the harmonies with the finished lead in the headphones.
- **Blend and panning.** Voice centre; guitar slightly left, piano right; bass and kick centre; harmonies slightly left and right.
- **The silent beat.** The first beat of bar 75: every track silent; cut reverb tails before it.
