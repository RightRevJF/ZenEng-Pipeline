## Summary

- **v7 has one tempo figure (104 bpm, all eras), channel design specs, a fixed 2:1 shuffle and no length band.** Songs 52 to 56 chose tempo and length by hand.

- **Tempo is an era setting.** Top-five hits ran 116 bpm in the 1960s, 91 in the 1990s (a third under 80) and 108 since 2010; both published trends sit on this curve. R&B is the slow genre; minor-key hits aren't slower. A fifth of hits sit in the 80–95 zone v7 says to avoid.

- **Feel changed:** 12/8 was a third of 1950s hits and has vanished; hits are now almost all straight 4/4, and tempo has grown steadier since click tracks (1979 on). Swing ratio depends on tempo: about 3.5:1 slow, 2:1 medium, near straight fast.

- **Length:** median 2:31 in the early 1960s, 4:12 by 1985–91, a 1992 peak, and about 3:15 now.

- **What v8 should do:** tempo, feel and length bands read from the chosen era and style; swing rendered by tempo; tempo steady unless designed otherwise; the form's length checked against the era.

## 2. The academic record

### Tempo

- **A preferred tempo near 120.** Moelants (2002) combined DJ bpm lists with tapping experiments and found tempi concentrated between about 115 and 127 bpm, peaking at 125; later work places preferred tempo in popular music nearer 120 than the 100 once claimed (de Clercq 2023 reviews this). Groove experiments find an optimum for drum breaks around 100 to 120 bpm.

- **Hit tempo moved, both ways.** Schellenberg and von Scheve (2012; over 1,000 Billboard top-40 hits, 1965–2009) found average tempo fell from about 116 bpm in the 1960s to about 100 in the 2000s. Léveillé Gauvin (2018; top-10 hits 1986–2015) found tempo rising over his later window. The two overlap only from 1986, and the rise came after the 1990s low.

- **Tempo got steadier.** Carter and von Appen (Integral, 2025; Billboard hits 1966–1995) found a steep fall in tempo variability from 1979, which they attribute to click tracks and sequencing; Condit-Schultz and Clark (2024; 45,000 Spotify tracks) found tempo stability rising in rock, country and blues across the 20th century.

### Feel

- **Swing depends on tempo.** Friberg and Sundström (Music Perception, 2002) measured jazz drummers' ride cymbal: the long-short swing ratio reached 3.5:1 at slow tempos and fell to 1:1 at fast ones; the textbook 2:1 holds only at medium tempos. v7's "about 2:1" shuffle is right only there.

- **Groove peaks at moderate syncopation** (Witek and colleagues; already in v7).

### Song length

- **Up, then down.** A 2025 count of all 6,618 Billboard year-end hits (1959–2025, Spotify durations) gives median lengths of 2:44 (1960s), 3:35 (1970s), 4:06 (1980s), 4:21 (1990s), 3:58 (2000s), 3:40 (2010s) and 3:15 (2020s). The mean peaked at 4:39 in 1992 and bottomed at 3:13 in 2019. Two thirds of 2019's hits ran 2:30 to 3:30. Weighting by Spotify plays lengthens the average slightly: the biggest hits aren't the shortest.

- **Earlier counts agree.** Schellenberg and von Scheve found hits lengthening from under three minutes in the 1960s to about four in the 2000s; MIDiA Research found top-10 hits shortening by 16% from 2000 to 2020, to 3:42, with intros falling from 13.1 to 7.4 seconds.

### What the numbers say

1. **Tempo is an era setting, with a 1990s dip.** Hits ran fastest in the 1960s (median 116), slowest in the 1990s (91, a third of them under 80), and have climbed back to 108 since 2010. Both published trends are in this curve: Schellenberg and von Scheve's fall to the 1990s, Léveillé Gauvin's rise after it.

2. **v7's 104 bpm is the all-era median.** It fits the 1970s, 1980s and 2000s and misses the 1960s and 1990s by more than 10 bpm.

3. **v7's two channels are real but leaky.** Half of hits sit in its motor band (95–130) and 15% under 80, but a fifth sit in the 80–95 zone it calls a place to avoid.

4. **R&B is the slow genre** (median 93, a quarter under 80); rock the fastest and widest (28% over 130). Minor-key hits are not slower than major ones (108 against 101), the opposite of the sad-is-slow expectation.

5. **The swung ballad metre vanished.** 12/8 (the doo-wop and soul ballad shuffle) was a third of 1950s hits and is absent after 2010; hits now are almost all straight 4/4. Compound and triple metres are a choice of style or era, not a default.

6. **Length follows the format.** From 2:31 in the early 1960s to 4:12 by 1985–91 and back to about 3:15 now.

## 4. What v8 should do

The same principle as Part 25's intros: tempo, feel and length are era and style settings. Give the intake an era (or "now") and a style, and read the defaults and bands from the tables.

### T1. Tempo bands by era and style

- Default tempo: the era's median, adjusted by genre (R&B about 10 bpm slower, rock and funk a little faster); the band is the era's middle half. Now: 108 (91–128). A 1990s sound: 91 (70–102). A 1960s sound: 116 (95–128).

- Keep v7's channels as a description (contemplative under 80, motor 95–130), but drop the instruction to avoid 80–95: a fifth of hits live there.

- Don't slow a song because it's minor; mode doesn't set tempo in hits.

- Tempo is steady by default (click-track era); a free or drifting tempo (a rubato intro, a slowing outro) is a designed moment.

### T2. Feel

- Feel is an intake choice: straight, shuffle (swung eighths), 12/8 or 6/8, triple, half-time. Straight 4/4 is the default for anything after 1980; 12/8 is a 1950s and early-1960s ballad or soul sound, and blues and country shuffles are style choices.

- **Swing ratio by tempo** , from Friberg and Sundström: about 3:1 at slow tempos, 2:1 around medium, approaching straight at fast tempos. The demo renders the ratio for the tempo rather than a fixed 2:1.

### T3. Length

- Bands by era, checked by the commercial profile (fix 71): 1960s about 2:20 to 3:15; 1970s 2:55 to 4:10; 1980s 3:25 to 4:35; 1990s around 4:20; now about 2:30 to 3:30 (two thirds of 2019's hits), median about 3:15.

- The form options at intake print their length against the band, so the writer sees when a form runs long for the era.

### T4. Tests

- The test report prints tempo, feel and length against the chosen era's bands, and the swing ratio the demo used.

- Songs 52 to 56 as targets: 54 and 55 at 120 sit in the modern band; 56 at 72 is in the slow tail, right for a soul ballad; the 4- to 5-minute forms offered at intake are long for now and right for the 1980s and 1990s.
