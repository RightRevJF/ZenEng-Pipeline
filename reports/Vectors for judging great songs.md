# Stop Rewarding the Median Song

No published method, on arXiv or in the journals up to November 2026, picks great songs out of competent ones from the notes alone, and the judge's zero correlation with weeks on chart and peak position is what the literature predicts for a judge of its design. Three facts produce that zero. Chart outcome among songs that all charted is carried mostly by artist, era and exposure, with note-level effects of about a third of a standard deviation at best; 159 songs cannot detect effects that small; and a judge that charges each reading for leaving the middle half and then prefers the medians is aiming at the centre of the corpus, a place real songs almost never occupy and which the graded-outcome studies say is not where the longest-running hits sit. The readings with real evidence are few: melodic surprise charged one-sidedly (too much is bad, a little too little is harmless), phrase endings more predictable than their phrases, a verse harmonically rarer than its chorus, balance of the melodic shape, a ceiling on syncopation, and enough repetition to be parseable. Learned "aesthetic" predictors all need audio and reward production, hit prediction from content is close to nil once artist data is removed, and the famous uncertainty-by-surprise interaction now points in three different directions. The judge the evidence supports has four layers: pass-or-fail craft gates, a joint typicality test calibrated on held-out real songs, a short unit-weighted sum of readings whose direction is known in advance, and a final choice made by several differently built critics that returns a small varied set instead of one winner. The practical new resources are graded labels already attached to songs with notes: chart peak and weeks inside every CoCoPops Billboard file, Rolling Stone list rank inside 200 more, and about 2,200 Hooktheory songs that join to the Hot 100. Even built this way the judge earns the claim "not broken, not over-fitted, weakly aligned with independent outcomes", and the last step from that to "great" still needs a listener.

## A correlation of zero is the expected result, for three separate reasons

### The outcome itself is mostly not in the notes

Where studies remove one kind of input at a time, the picture is consistent. On 74,206 Spotify tracks, dropping artist and platform metadata raised prediction error by 40%, dropping lyric embeddings raised it by 10%, and every content-only model sat at about the same error; no prediction exceeded 0.8 on the 0-to-1 popularity scale although 1.2% of real songs do ([Choudhary et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05508v1)). On about 30,000 tracks, the best model of audio descriptors beat "predict the average" by **7%**, with genre and year doing most of that ([Jung & Mayer](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12079)). Eurovision entries from 2008 to 2024 gave the same answer: intrinsic audio and lyric features "did not accurately predict rankings" ([Adamska & Reiss 2025](https://doaj.org/article/d791cfe05c8240dcb6b0172419c41102)). The one chord-symbol test found that chord progressions were never chosen as a split once artist popularity was in the model, and that I–V–vi–IV appears in every popularity decile ([Campagna thesis](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/fbf0c0f5-7fee-4112-817e-b8fed5ecf53d/prod_425777-doc_153760.pdf)). The only MIDI-based hit classifier reached about 60% precision on a balanced task whose negative class was defined by artist, with coefficients showing "no clear pattern" ([Wang 2014](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2014/Kedao%20Wang,%20Predicting%20Hit%20Songs%20with%20MIDI%20Musical%20Features.pdf)), and about 150 melody features separated hits from non-hits at 52% under honest cross-validation ([Müllensiefen et al.](http://www.doc.gold.ac.uk/~mas03dm/papers/HitSongScience_Weimar.pdf)). The headline "97% accuracy" hit paper was audited and found to have copied 24 songs into 10,000 synthetic samples before splitting them; with the split fixed, accuracy was "little better than random" ([Kapoor & Narayanan 2023](https://reproducible.cs.princeton.edu/predicting-hits.html)).

Within songs that all charted, which is the builders' situation, the ceiling is known from audio. Full audio feature sets reach a rank correlation of 0.47 with peak and 0.42 with weeks on a set that mixes charting and non-charting songs, so the purely within-chart figure is lower ([Vötter et al. 2021](https://dbis-web1.uibk.ac.at/sites/default/files/2022-06/ISM_2021__Hit_Song_Prediction.pdf)). On a median split, peak position is predictable at about 60% and **weeks on chart at 51 to 53%, near chance** ([Lee & Lee 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.00551)). Symbolic features carry less than audio. The only symbolic study with a graded chart outcome found top-quartile Billboard songs differed from bottom-quartile ones by d = 0.29 in mean chord rarity ([Miles et al. 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00263/full)).

### 159 songs cannot see effects of that size

The research team downloaded the 214 CoCoPops Billboard melody files, from which the kept corpus appears to be drawn, and counted the labels: among the 170 songs sampled once, peak position has a median of 8 (quartiles 2, 8 and 26), weeks on chart a median of 14 (quartiles 11, 14, 18), and the two correlate at −0.65 ([CoCoPops sample table](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Computational-Cognitive-Musicology-Lab/CoCoPops/HEAD/CoCoPops_Sample.tsv)). The set leans toward big hits, so the range of outcomes is compressed. By the team's estimate, a top-against-bottom quartile contrast at this size detects effects of about d = 0.55 and larger, not the 0.3 typical of the one positive symbolic study. A Spearman of zero on 159 songs is therefore weak evidence about any judge, good or bad. It would be the expected reading even for a judge that had captured everything the literature has found.

Peak and weeks are also different targets and should never be pooled. Peak is the more predictable from content; weeks behaves like a typicality optimum; and artist history pushes them opposite ways, with ten or more prior hits raising peak and shortening the run ([Askin & Mauskapf 2017](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/culture/2017-askin.pdf)).

### The judge aims at the centre, and the centre is the wrong place

This is the reason that can be fixed. Work on generative models established that the most probable point of a distribution is not a typical member of it: models assign high likelihood to inputs unlike their training data because the "typical set" and the high-density region are different places, and the remedy is a two-sided test of whether an item's likelihood falls where real items' likelihoods fall ([Nalisnick et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02994v2)). In text the same thing shows up with human ratings: quality rises with model likelihood to a point and then falls, the highest-likelihood sentences being repetitive ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.10450)), and near-mode translations agree least with the statistics of real data ([Eikema & Aziz 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.10283)).

Put in the judge's own units, the argument is arithmetic done by the research team, not a published result. If the readings were independent, a real corpus song would have all 20 of 20 readings inside the middle half about once in a million songs, and the average real song would be charged on half of them. Correlation among readings softens this but does not rescue it. A candidate with zero charges is unlike essentially every kept song, and the tie-break toward the medians pushes further the same way. That explains both symptoms on the builders' bench: strong response to damage (damage moves readings a long way out) and no ordering among kept songs (every one of them is "charged" for being a real song).

The graded-outcome studies add that the centre is not where success sits either. On about 25,100 Hot 100 songs, peak position and weeks on chart were both an inverted U in typicality relative to the previous year's chart; the average number one was less typical than other charting songs, and a song one standard deviation below mean typicality stayed about half a week longer ([Askin & Mauskapf 2017](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/culture/2017-askin.pdf)). On 14,248 songs measured against their own genre and year, the optimum for a long run was 0.27 to 0.35 standard deviations on the more typical side for audio and 0.14 to 0.18 for lyrics, with the two penalties independent and additive ([O'Toole & Horvát](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07754)). The two studies disagree about which side of the mean the optimum lies on, and both used audio descriptors, so transfer to melody readings is an assumption. They agree on what matters here: the best position is near the norm and off it by a fraction of a standard deviation, the effect is worth about half a chart week, and no study supports "nearest the median wins" or "more novel is better".

## Six note-level readings have evidence behind a direction; most famous ones do not

The table grades each candidate reading by what the evidence allows a judge to do with it. "One-sided" means charge in one direction only; "shape" means a comparison between parts of the song.

| Reading (computable from melody, chords, lyrics) | Best evidence | Verdict for the judge |
|---|---|---|
| Mean melodic surprise (information content), duration-weighted | Liking falls as it rises in three experiments with 380 listeners; no inverted U there | Keep. Charge steeply above the kept band, lightly or not at all below |
| Entropy (uncertainty before each note) as its own reading | Linear β = −0.31, R² = 34.9% in one repeated-listening study | Add; cheapest new reading; sign provisional |
| Phrase-end surprise below the phrase mean | Expected endings liked more in seven studies; closure expectation peaks at the cadence | Add as a shape check |
| Verse harmonically rarer than chorus (zeroth-order chord rarity) | d = 0.42 overall, d = 0.96 where verse exceeds chorus, among charting songs | Add as a shape term; test on kept corpus |
| Balance: events evenly spread, climax near the middle | d = −0.25 on imbalance, r² = 0.06, four-second melodies | Add, low weight |
| Syncopation | Pleasantness flat from low to medium, falls by high | Ceiling only, not a target |
| Repetition | Helps hard-to-parse material; near-independent of preference in tonal music | Gate with diminishing credit |
| Uncertainty × surprise interaction | Three studies, three patterns | Leave out, or fit sign on own data |
| Tension curve shape; chill triggers; resolution timing | No liking test exists | Do not score |
| Exact retrograde symmetry | Mildly disliked (d = 0.20 for asymmetry) | Do not reward |
| Chord-progression identity; pitch n-grams as hit markers | Null once artist is controlled; no pattern | Dead end for ranking |

### Surprise is a decline, so the two-sided charge is wrong on one side

The largest test since the original studies used three experiments with 182, 93 and 105 listeners and found liking declining with information content every time (coefficients −0.131, −0.093 and −0.072); the one quadratic term that reached significance curved the wrong way for a sweet spot, and the authors state that their results conflict with the inverted U ([Korkor et al. 2025](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/32249/4/FullText.pdf)). In the reference study the quadratic term added about one point of R² over the linear decline (26.3% against 25.3%) ([Gold et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/237088229.pdf)), and the notes' arithmetic on its coefficients puts the fitted peak about 1.2 standard deviations below the average melody. A third study found the quadratic indistinguishable from a straight line ([Bianco et al. 2019](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10087105/1/s41598-019-53510-w.pdf)). The inverted U survives in a large 2025 choice experiment on generated melodies ([Mas-Herrero & Marco-Pallarés 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12304940/)) and at the level of single notes in artificial-grammar melodies, where medium-probability notes beat low-probability ones by d = 0.85 ([Zioga et al. 2024](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/37446/8/s41598-024-70027-z.pdf)). Both of those used stimuli more surprising than real repertoire. The position that fits all of it: real tonal melodies sit on the descending limb, so for mean surprise the judge should charge heavily above the kept corpus's upper quartile and lightly, if at all, below its lower quartile. The current symmetric charge punishes candidates for being slightly more predictable than the median hit, which the listener evidence says is where liking is highest.

Two cautions travel with this. Context moves liking by more than any feature: the same melodies shifted by about 0.6 scale points depending on what they were heard beside, and model surprise had no effect on liking once context varied (p = 0.33), although it still tracked how predictable listeners said the melodies were ([Albury et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1175682/full)). A judge's absolute scale therefore means nothing; only rank inside one comparable pool does. And minimising surprise is not the goal, because melodies generated to be probable under the reference model were rated 2.5 to 3.2 on a 7-point scale against 6.0 for originals ([Pearce 2005](https://core.ac.uk/download/42628558.pdf)). The one-sided charge belongs beneath a floor that rejects the degenerate.

Entropy deserves its own column. It is computed from the same corpus model (the spread of the next-note prediction, weighted by note length), and in repeated listening it carried more than surprise did ([Gold et al. 2019](https://core.ac.uk/download/237088229.pdf)). The interaction between the two should stay out. Cheung's chord study found surprise pleasant under low uncertainty ([Cheung et al. 2019](https://cms.mus.cam.ac.uk/publications/cheung-uncertainty-surprise/)); Gold's melody study found predictability most pleasing under high uncertainty, at p = 0.06; the 2025 study found larger surprises preferred under high entropy ([Mas-Herrero & Marco-Pallarés 2025](https://www.ub.edu/brainvitge/publication/predictive-processes-shape-individual-musical-preferences)). Those are three different patterns, and no study has tested any of them on melody with chords together, which is the engine's actual object.

### Shape terms are better supported than averages

Endings that confirmed the learned pattern were liked more than altered endings in every one of seven behavioural studies of melodies in an unfamiliar scale (β = 0.05 to 0.10) ([Kathios et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.17.496615.full.pdf)), and listeners' expectation that a phrase is about to close rises steadily toward a cadence and peaks about 130 ms before a perfect cadence arrives ([Sears et al. 2020](https://www.music.mcgill.ca/~caplin/sears_2020_expecting-the.pdf)). A reading that compares the surprise of each phrase's last one or two notes with the phrase mean, and wants it lower, has the best support of any shape term on the melody side.

On the harmony side the support is the Billboard quartile result. Top-quartile songs had rarer chords in verses (5.26 against 4.60 bits, d = 0.33) and no difference in choruses; the verse-to-chorus change was larger (d = 0.42), and where the verse was the more surprising section the drop into the chorus was 2.15 against 0.95 bits, **d = 0.96** ([Miles et al. 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00263/full)). The fine print matters. The measure is raw chord frequency with no context; first- and second-order transition surprise showed nothing; the genre-controlled song-level effect is a partial eta squared of 0.027; the follow-up found no quartile difference before 1975 ([Miles et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8121146)); and a patent on the method exists ([US 11,048,747](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11048747)). It is still the only replicated note-level separator of the top from the bottom of the chart, it is computable on the kept corpus today, and it argues against assuming a context-aware chord model will rank better than plain rarity for this outcome.

Balance is the one shape property with a direct experiment on liking. In 92 short melodies built to be extreme on balance, contour, symmetry or complexity, more balanced melodies (events spread evenly, climax central) were liked more, note density with pitch variety raised liking (d = 0.33), palindromic melodies were liked less, and jaggedness of contour had no reliable effect; individual listeners' slopes ranged across zero on every property ([Clemente et al.](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/70303251/ana_clemente_et_al_evaluative_judgement_across_domains_publishersversion.pdf)). The positive complexity effect does not contradict the surprise decline: these four-second fragments start from a very sparse floor, so "more notes and more pitches" and "less statistical surprise" are separate readings.

### Rhythm, repetition and memory give ceilings and gates

For pleasantness the newest syncopation test found a linear decline and no significant curve: low and medium did not differ, and medium beat high; the inverted U held only for the urge to move ([Céspedes-Guevara & Witek 2023](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/189045021/Rhythm_syncopation_movement_and_pleasantness.pdf)). In full-texture music the effect of syncopation was 0.08 points on a 5-point scale and vanished when the bass was removed ([Duncan & Orgs 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10204599/1/Move%20your%20Body.pdf)). The supported rule is "do not exceed medium", a one-sided ceiling.

Repetition has the best general curve in the field and no usable number. Fifty of 57 studies fit an inverted U of preference against familiarity or complexity ([Chmiel & Schubert 2017](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/back-to-the-inverted-u-for-music-preference-a-review-of-the-liter/)), and inserting repeats into atonal music raised enjoyment ([Margulis 2013](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/aesthetic-responses-to-repetition-in-unfamiliar-music/)), yet in orchestral excerpts detected repetition and preference loaded on separate factors with near-zero cross-loading ([Touizrar et al. 2021](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.673706/full)). The reading that fits both is that repetition helps until the material is parseable and pays little after. Familiarity grows with the logarithm of the number of hearings while liking peaks and falls ([Kathios et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.17.496615.full.pdf)), which suggests crediting the second and third statement of a phrase and little beyond the fourth; that application to within-song repeats is the notes' inference and is untested. Among lyrics, each extra chorus repetition raised the odds of a number one over a bottom-of-chart song by 14.5% in a study read only through a press summary ([Pacific Standard](https://psmag.com/?p=30843)), and simpler lyrics charted higher among 14,661 songs ([Varnum et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0244576.html)).

Memorability research has not moved the ceiling. The newer work is on audio and reports single-feature R² of about 0.01, with the listener's own sense of recognising the song far stronger than any feature ([Bechtold et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1642561/full)). The symbolic terms from the earlier rounds stand as weak, replicated priors: phrases that survive oral transmission are shorter (−0.30), less surprising (−0.24) and more repeated, at a marginal R² of 0.05 ([Janssen et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5403935/)).

### What has no evidence at all

Three ideas that sound like they should be vectors are not, as of this search. No study shows that any tension-curve shape predicts liking; the best automated tension model correlates 0.6 to 0.7 with listener ratings, but loudness and roughness do most of the work and the note-based tonal part contributed little ([Barchet et al. 2024](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_3565372_1/component/file_3565373/kog-24-rim-01-tenseMusic.pdf)), and rated tension runs against pleasantness at −0.69 ([Turrell et al. 2025](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/106400/2/Building%20the%20anticipation_Javadi_AAM.pdf)). No controlled experiment inserts or removes an appoggiatura, a leap-then-step or a chord change and measures chills; the one removal study cut whole climactic passages defined by texture and dynamics, in 24 listeners, significant only when three pieces were pooled ([Bannister & Eerola 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02046/full)). And no experiment varies how long a surprise waits for its resolution. No corpus study links text-setting features (stress on beats, syllables per note, melisma, rhyme placement) to graded success either, although the data to run one exist.

## Graded labels already sit inside songs that have notes

The earlier rounds concluded that every pop corpus samples only successes. That remains true, but success is graded inside three sources, and the grading is the new raw material.

| CoCoPops Rolling Stone | melody, chords | Rolling Stone "500 Greatest" list rank in every file | 200 | Rock-heavy; only 15 songs shared with the Billboard half |

Sources: [CoCoPops Billboard README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Computational-Cognitive-Musicology-Lab/CoCoPops/HEAD/Billboard/README.md), [CoCoPops Rolling Stone README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Computational-Cognitive-Musicology-Lab/CoCoPops/HEAD/RollingStone/README.md), [Sheet Sage data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chrisdonahue/sheetsage-data/main/hooktheory/Hooktheory.json.gz) with the [Hot 100 archive](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/utdata/rwd-billboard-data/main/data-out/hot-100-current.csv), [Chordonomicon](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ailsntua/Chordonomicon/raw/main/README.md), [Hymnary export](https://hymnary.org/search?qu=textAuthNumber%3Alove_divine_all_love_excelling_joy_of_he%20publicationDate%3A1950-2025%20in%3Atunes&sort=matchingInstances&export=csv).

The Hooktheory join is the research team's own count from the public files, not a published figure: 2,207 songs with melody matched the Hot 100, of which 388 reached number one, 694 peaked from 2 to 10, 647 from 11 to 40 and 478 from 41 to 100, weighted toward the 2000s and 2010s. That is the first corpus of melodies with chords large enough to detect a quartile difference of about d = 0.12, at the cost of amateur accuracy and a match that needs cleaning. Chordonomicon is the only resource large enough to test, in chord symbols with genre and year controls, the typicality optimum that the audio studies found.

Three labels on three audiences are now available, and they are different targets. Critics and the public do not agree: Rolling Stone critics' ratings of 100 songs correlated r = −0.0009 with undergraduates' ratings, and critics were insensitive to familiarity while lay listeners rated familiar songs higher ([Lundy et al. 2019](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2058448832)). A critics' rank is therefore a second axis that is cleaner of exposure effects, not a noisier copy of the chart. Musicians differ again: their liking peaks at higher harmonic complexity than non-musicians' ([Witek et al. 2023](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/185767621/journal.pone.0281057.pdf)), and what guitarists look up is led by "Hallelujah" and "Wonderwall", not by the streaming leaders ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/guitars/timeless-songs-that-translate-beautifully-to-guitar-ed-sheeran-is-the-king-of-the-guitar-tabs)). No one has published the direct test, fitting the same readings to a chart label, a critics' label and a musicians' label and comparing the signs. The 198 MusicBrainz IDs in the CoCoPops melody files are the key that would let cover counts and present-day streams be joined to the kept corpus.

Long-run survival is a fourth label, and a loosely related one. Among 1,176 number-one hits, present-day stream counts span more than three orders of magnitude within a single decade ([Mush from the Wimp](https://mushfromthewimp.substack.com/p/the-most-and-least-streamed-1-hits)), and weeks on chart did not correlate with later influence ([O'Toole & Horvát](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07754)). The folk and chart literatures also disagree in one instructive place: the top chart quartile has rarer chords in verses, while in oral transmission the less surprising phrase survives. If rarity helps a song stand out at release and predictability helps it be retained and re-sung, a single "greatness" label hides two opposed pressures. No study has yet predicted decades-later streams, covers or canon membership from notes while controlling for original chart performance. That regression is unrun, and the data now exist.

Two warnings on the label side. Tune-family size and number of settings scale with fame (marginal R² 0.26 for settings against 0.001 to 0.008 for any melodic feature in 9,378 session tunes), so they will mostly rediscover popularity unless age and exposure are controlled ([Street et al. 2022](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1207558)); that dataset's licence also bars processing with language models ([TheSession-data LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adactio/TheSession-data/HEAD/LICENSE.md)). And a December 2025 scrape of Spotify popularity for about 256 million tracks exists but was taken outside the service's terms ([copy of the release post](https://docs.bbchallenge.org/other/Backing%20up%20Spotify%20-%20Anna%E2%80%99s%20Blog.html)); it is noted here and not recommended.

## Learned judges fail on notes, and selection itself misleads

### Every rating-trained predictor needs audio and rewards the wrong thing

A September 2025 survey found no symbolic per-piece quality predictor validated against human ratings; symbolic assessment remains "manual or rule-based" ([Kader & Karmaker 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00051v1)). The audio predictors look strong in-distribution, with rank correlations near 0.90 on SongEval, where a bare vocal-range feature alone reaches 0.70 ([SongEval](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10793)). On generators they have not seen, the picture changes. In the ICASSP 2026 song-aesthetics challenge the best of 18 entries reached a rank correlation of **0.678**, the organisers reported models "frequently under-scoring top-tier tracks", and one system with the highest linear correlation (0.706) identified the top tier correctly only 12.4% of the time ([challenge report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07237v1)). The newest open metric is "selectively sensitive to signal-level artifacts" and insensitive to musical-structural distortion ([MuQ-Eval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22677)). Rendering a thousand candidates through one synthesiser and scoring the audio should therefore return little more than noise about composition; that is an inference, since no study has tested same-render candidates.

Pairwise preference is no easier. The largest model in music, trained on 285,000 user choices between 20-second clips, predicts held-out choices at about **60%**, the authors' own team agreed with the crowd 60% of the time, and the model failed to beat chance when trained from scratch instead of from a pretrained checkpoint ([MusicRL](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.04229)). Cropping its input to 3 to 5 seconds cut accuracy to 56%, direct evidence that preference lives in structure longer than a few bars. Human ratings of generated symbolic music are themselves unreliable, with inter-annotator alpha of 0.33 to 0.44 in the one study that reported it, and in that study rule checks such as scale adherence did not track human judgement while divergence from a reference corpus did, at the level of whole systems ([arXiv 2606.05522](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05522v1)). One idea from this literature is worth a cheap trial and is untested on notes: a small scoring head on frozen pretrained features gave "usable correlation" from about 150 labelled clips and supports a personal evaluator trained on one listener's choices ([MuQ-Eval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22677)). The symbolic analogue would be a linear comparator on feature differences trained on about 150 of the songwriter's own keep-or-reject decisions, the pairwise linear form that beat neural comparators for popularity ([Vötter et al. 2023](https://dbis-web1.uibk.ac.at/sites/default/files/2023-10/voetter_smc_2023.pdf)). That uses a listener, though only one and only once.

### Picking the top one of a thousand overstates the winner and hunts the judge's blind spots

Choosing the maximum of noisy scores guarantees disappointment. With ten equally good alternatives the chosen one's score overstates its value by 1.54 noise standard deviations ([Smith & Winkler 2006](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/2006-smith.pdf)); the research team's extension of the same formula to a pool of 1,000 gives 3.24. Its simulation under idealised assumptions (unbiased noise, normally distributed true quality) shows what a weak judge buys: if a fifth of the score's variance is real quality, the top pick averages 1.4 standard deviations above the pool against 3.2 available, and lands in the true top ten about one time in six. A blunt judge is better than none, its top score says nothing about how good the pick is, and improving the judge is worth far more than enlarging the pool.

Real critics are worse than that idealisation because their errors are systematic, and selection searches for them. With a learned reward model as proxy and a much larger one as truth, true quality under best-of-n selection rises and then falls as n grows, confirmed out to n = 60,000, while the proxy's own score keeps climbing ([Gao, Schulman & Hilton 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760)). In translation, reranking 200 candidates by a single learned quality estimator raised the related automatic metric while human adequacy fell and major errors rose from 139 to 204 ([Fernandes et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00978)); a metric used as its own selector chose translations with wrong numbers at up to 16.6% against 4.4% for a simple baseline, and retraining it to fix that changed its headline correlation with humans by at most 0.006, so the standard validation statistic revealed neither the defect nor the repair ([Amrhein & Sennrich 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.05148)). In symbolic music, post-training a chord generator on coherence rewards alone made it "repeat simple, high-scoring chords", with diversity falling from 29.5 to 21.0 ([GAPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17879v2)).

The remedies with numbers behind them are four. Combine several critics built on different information: selection by an ensemble of metrics cut human-rated translation errors by about 16% where single-metric selection was sometimes worse than no selection ([Kovacs et al. 2024](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03524)); averaging the ranks of three verifiers improved an independent grader's score from 84.29 to 88.18, with the warning that one harmful member dragged the ensemble below the best single critic on another task ([Ma et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09732v1)); and taking the worst case across an ensemble "practically eliminates" over-optimisation for best-of-n ([Coste et al. 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/dda7f9378a210c25e470e19304cce85d-Abstract-Conference.html)). Never judge the winners with the critic that chose them, because a metric overestimates its own selections ([Kovacs et al. 2024](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03524)). Return a set instead of an argmax: choosing at random from the top fraction bounds the cost of the judge's errors ([Taylor 2015](https://intelligence.org/2015/11/29/new-paper-quantilizers/)), and submitting one candidate per behavioural cluster lifted AlphaCode's solve rate from 16.9% to 21.0% at 1,000 samples ([Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07814)). And watch diversity beside the score: adding an adversarial realism reward that is retrained only when the generator starts winning restored diversity to 26.6 while keeping the harmony gain, and twelve expert musicians rated that system highest ([GAPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17879v2)).

How many candidates are worth generating has a cross-field answer. The best candidate present keeps improving with the logarithm of the pool size, but without a true verifier realised selection stops improving early: from 100 to 10,000 samples a reward-model selector rose from 38.7% to 39.8% while the share of pools containing a correct answer passed 95% ([Brown et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21787v2)), and reranked image quality improved up to about 32 candidates "after which returns diminish" ([Ramesh et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.12092)). Where human raters were used, good reranking moved 5-point ratings by 0.1 to 0.2 ([Fernandes et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00978)). A thousand candidates per lyric is already past the point a blunt judge can use. In lyric-to-melody work the same rules bought +0.05 as a reranker of finished songs and +0.31 as soft rewards during the search ([ReLyMe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.05688)), which remains the strongest reason to move scores into construction.

## The judge the evidence supports has four layers and few fitted numbers

### Separate conformity from direction

The central design error in the present judge is that it mixes two different statements in one tally. "This reading is outside where real songs sit" is a conformity statement and is rightly two-sided. "More of this is better" is a quality statement and needs a direction. Unit-weighted sums work when each term is oriented so that it relates positively to the outcome, with signs set in advance ([Dawes 1979](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/dawes/the-robust-beauty-of-improper-linear-models-in-decision-making.pdf)); two-sided charges are not of that kind, and summing them is what leaves survivors tied at zero.

| Layer | Built from | Evidence base | What it can claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Gates | Range, key fit, true stress clashes, phrase length, cadence present, per-note surprise below a high percentile of real songs, minimum repetition, syncopation ceiling | Craft rules and wrong-note detection; partial verifiers remove most of a pool cheaply | "Not broken" |
| 2. Joint typicality | One distance over all readings together (shrunk-covariance Mahalanobis, plus Isolation Forest as a second opinion), turned into a percentile against held-out real songs, charged at both tails | Typical-set results; conformal guarantee; one-class benchmark | "Sits where real songs sit" |
| 3. Direction | Unit-weighted sum, by family, of the few readings with a known sign: low mean surprise (one-sided), phrase-end predictability, verse rarer than chorus, balance, motif repetition, short phrases | Listener experiments and graded chart contrasts above | "Leans the way the weak evidence leans" |
| 4. Selection | Rank-average or minimum across separately built critics; shortlist of the top few percent that every critic accepts; one pick per cluster; one critic withheld | Ensemble and best-of-n studies | "Not chosen by exploiting one critic" |

**Layer 2 replaces the quartile charges.** Compute a single distance from the corpus centre using all readings and their correlations, then ask whether a candidate's distance falls where held-out real songs' distances fall. Under a simple model, real songs with 20 readings sit in a shell well away from the centre, and almost none sit as close in as a quarter of the average squared distance, so a candidate that is too central is flagged just as one that is too far. The research team's simulation, which is not a literature result, found that candidates with correct individual readings but broken relationships among them were invisible to quartile charges (AUC 0.51 to 0.56) and visible to the joint distance (0.73 to 0.96), and that 150 songs rank nearly as well as 750 but inflate distances by 11 to 18%, so thresholds must come from held-out real songs and never from a textbook table. The conformal recipe does that with a finite-sample guarantee for any score, though with 150 calibration songs the smallest attainable p-value is 1/151 and the realised false-alarm rate wobbles (at a nominal 10% it can exceed 15% with 100 calibration points) ([Bates et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.08279)). Sample covariance is unreliable at this ratio of songs to readings, and shrinkage estimators are the standard repair ([scikit-learn documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/1.4/modules/covariance.html)); Isolation Forest degraded least when irrelevant features were added in a benchmark of 25,685 datasets, and one-class SVMs were weakest ([Emmott et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.01158)). This layer is a gate with a soft edge. It certifies resemblance and has no power to order the best few dozen of a thousand conforming candidates.

**Layer 3 must stay small.** Fitted weights need roughly 15 to 20 cases per predictor before they beat equal weights ([Dawes 1979](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/dawes/the-robust-beauty-of-improper-linear-models-in-decision-making.pdf)), and for weak signals the requirement runs to well over a hundred cases per parameter ([Riley et al. 2020](https://keele-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/464599)). With 159 to about 2,200 songs and outcomes this weak, the defensible forms are no fitted weights at all, or one to three fitted numbers, such as a single quadratic in the layer-2 distance against chart outcome, the test Askin and Mauskapf ran on audio. Equal-weight versions of nine election models beat the fitted originals in six cases ([Graefe 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v68y2015i8p1792-1799.html)). Readings should be grouped into families (surprise, shape, repetition, harmony contrast, prosody), averaged within a family and then weighted equally across families, so that twenty restatements of note density do not cast twenty votes. A reading whose sign cannot be stated from prior evidence stays out of the sum. Corpus likelihood stays out entirely, and the existing real-versus-engine classifier stays a diagnostic: under positive-unlabelled learning theory its score ranks by quality only if the kept corpus were a random sample of all good songs the engine could write, which it is not ([Bekker & Davis 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04820)).

**Layer 4 changes the last step.** The current "zero charges, then nearest medians" is an argmax on one critic at maximum pressure. The supported replacement is to rank by each critic separately, keep candidates that are in every critic's upper group, and return a few that differ from one another. A consensus pick (the candidate most similar on average to the rest of its pool) matched or beat mode-seeking with as few as 30 samples in translation ([Eikema & Aziz 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.10283)); it selects the pool's centre of mass, so it belongs as one member or an outlier filter, not as the selector.

### A listener-free battery, and what each test can prove

Machine-translation research supplies a mature kit for testing a judge without new human ratings, and its lesson is that average correlation hides the blind spots selection will find. Seven tests follow from it, in rising order of what they prove.

The pipeline should return chance when outcome labels are shuffled, because any tuning or feature choice made outside the cross-validation loop produces above-chance results on pure noise, still detectable at 1,000 samples, and across 55 published studies reported accuracy fell as sample size rose (r = −0.70) ([Vabalas et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6837442/)). Shuffling features within class tests whether a critic uses relationships among readings or only their separate values ([Ojala & Garriga 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/ojala10a.html)). Held-out real melodies should then be damaged at rising doses, with the judge required to prefer the original in a high share of minimal pairs and to respond monotonically, reported per damage type as the translation suites do across 35 perturbation types ([Karpinska et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13746)); the present judge already passes a version of this. Comparing the score drop per unit of damage across types exposes blind spots before selection finds them. For each held-out real song, the engine's pool for that song's own lyric and chords should be scored by a judge that never saw the song, and the real melody's percentile recorded: a calibrated typicality layer puts real melodies uniformly, a quality layer should put them high. The judge's top picks across many pools should be tabulated against real songs on every reading, since systematic drift (shorter, flatter, narrower) is the engine's version of the wrong-number errors. A withheld critic's score for the winner, plotted as the pool grows from 10 to 1,000, shows without any listener whether selection is still helping and where the curve bends. Last, with the judge frozen, its rank correlation with each outcome label should be computed on corpora not used to build it, with permutation p-values, signs written down beforehand, and splits made by song and artist.

Every revision made after seeing a validation number spends that number, since the bias from tuning against a finite validation set is "often of comparable magnitude to differences in performance between learning algorithms" ([Cawley & Talbot 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html)). A small vault per outcome label, opened rarely and logged, is the defence. The first tests show the judge responds to known damage and uses joint structure. The real-among-generated test can be passed by detectability alone and must be read with inputs matched. Only the last test touches "good", and its ceiling is low: a rank correlation near 0.1 that keeps its sign across the chart label, the critics' label and a musicians' label would be a real result, and anything near 0.5 should be treated as a leak until shown otherwise.

## Conclusion

The search for vectors that point at greatness returned something more useful than a new vector: the finding that the present judge's geometry is the main thing wrong with it. Nearly every reading with evidence is one-sided or a contrast between parts of a song, the graded-outcome studies put the optimum near the norm and off it, and the statistics of typical sets say a candidate with no charges resembles no real song. Turning the two-sided charges into one joint typicality test, and keeping a separate short list of directed readings, costs no new data and removes the built-in pull toward the median. Whether it lifts the Spearman above zero is a separate matter, and on 159 songs neither a zero nor a small positive value would settle anything; the 2,200-song Hooktheory join and the 739 chord files are where that question becomes answerable.

The deeper change is in what the judge is asked to deliver. The record supports a judge that rejects the broken, certifies the typical, leans a shortlist in the direction of weak replicated priors, and hands over a few varied candidates with honest uncertainty. It does not support a judge that crowns one song. Charts, critics and players reward different things, release-time success and long survival pull against each other on surprise, and the experts who rate generated music barely agree with one another. Three open tests would add knowledge no one has published: the same readings fitted to a chart label, a critics' label and a players' label; text-setting features against graded chart outcome on the CoCoPops lyrics; and decades-later survival predicted from notes with original chart performance held fixed. A songwriting engine that runs them will know more about its own judge than the literature currently knows about any.
