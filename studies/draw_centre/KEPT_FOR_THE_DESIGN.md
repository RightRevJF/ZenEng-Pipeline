What a design can take from it:

- **Within a line, every part is alike** (a middle of 2.2 to 2.3). One figure serves.
- **Between lines the parts differ a little:** choruses hold their lines closest (1.3), verses and bridges 1.5, pre-choruses widest (2.0).
- **Where the height goes differs by part, and this is the part worth having.** A verse tends to rise: its highest line is in its last third 42 times in 100, and its last line sits a little above its middle. A chorus tends to start high and settle: its highest line is in its first third 44 times in 100, its last line a little under. A pre-chorus climbs most of all (the last line +1.8, the highest line in the last third 53 times in 100), as one would hope of the part that leads to the chorus.
- So "each line its own centre" should not be a centre drawn at random about the part's. It has a shape by part, and the kept songs give it.

A caution on the middle row: a line's offset has a standard deviation of 2.2 to 3.1, larger than the "spread between lines" above it, because that row pools every line of every section and the row above is the middle section's own spread. A design should be set by the first row and checked by it.
