# Mood from the written notes — predictions written 19 Nov 2026 before measuring

Found after the fact on 18,024 recordings: songs still loved and covered sound less cheerful (Spotify valence, -0.09)
and less made for dancing (danceability, -0.08) than other hits of their year. Question: can that be read from the
written tune, and does the written reading grade songs?

Step 1 (no grades used). In each source (Hooktheory, Lakh MIDI, lyrics-melody MIDI), a ridge reader learns Spotify's
valence and danceability from written-tune features (pace, rhythm variety, off-beat share, span, step, leaps,
repeated notes, falling share, phrase slope, minor lean, time on scale degrees, long notes, gaps, tempo where the
file gives one, and the 64 phrase regions). Artists are split in two; each half is read by the reader fitted on the
other. That gives every song a written cheerfulness Vw and a written danceability Dw it had no hand in fitting.
Reported: how well Vw and Dw track Spotify's own readings.

Step 2. Vw and Dw against G on the yardstick songs (2005 or earlier), holding year, note count and chart peak.
 Vw  written cheerfulness     -
 Dw  written danceability     -
Pass, for each: negative in all three sources, p < .05 in at least two, pooled p < .025.
Reported besides: Spotify's own valence and danceability against G on these same songs, as the mark to reach.
If step 1 is weak (written readings track Spotify's under 0.2), step 2 cannot be expected to pass, and that is itself
the finding: the mood is in the performance, not the page. Nothing goes into the engine on this study.
