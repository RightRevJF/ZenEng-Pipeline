# Round two: the physicist

## 1. The two strongest ideas from the others

**(a) The hymn-tune laboratory with tunes and texts crossed (sceptic 1; also evolutionist 2, songwriter 2).** Three people reached it independently and it is the only design that restores what the Hot 100 removed: the full range of the control variable. My field adds two things.

- *A correction.* "Number of hymnals printing the tune" is itself a rich-get-richer count, because editors compile from earlier hymnals. The tune effect b_j will be inflated by cumulative advantage exactly as cover counts are. Fit it as a fitness: for each new hymnal h published at time t, P(tune j included) is proportional to eta_j * (k_j(t) + 1), where k_j is the count of earlier hymnals carrying j; eta_j is the grade. The rival-tune contests (one text, two tunes of the same metre) are immune and should be the headline.
- *An addition.* This is the fair trial of the owner's exponent. On a population that includes the forgotten, distance from the critical point varies, so the prediction is sharp: eta_j is an inverted U in S, peaked near 1, and flat among the survivors alone. If S does not grade even here, criticality is not the price of admission either.

**(b) The conserved core (evolutionist 3, with the sceptic's and songwriter's wild ideas).** It is my stiff-sloppy idea done at the right resolution: note by note, where mine was feature by feature. The conservation c_i is a per-site stiffness, and bimodality of {c_i} is the right statistic (two phases: pinned and free). Corrections:

- Dynamic time warping manufactures agreement. The null must be the same alignment run against versions of *different* songs of matched length and key.
- Fix the number of versions per work (the sceptic's point) or cover count leaks into the cloud's shape.
- The predictor in 3c will mostly learn "long notes on strong beats are kept". Unless c_i is predicted beyond metric weight and duration, core shape collapses to note density, which is "fewer events" again.

## 2. The weakest idea in the room

My own Idea 6 (order parameter, Binder cumulant, corrections to scaling). It will fail for two exact reasons. First, a tune of 200 to 400 notes gives perhaps twenty independent windows, and a fourth-moment ratio on twenty samples is estimator noise; the correction-to-scaling fit asks for three parameters from under one decade of scale. Second, it is a single-song scalar on the page among survivors, which my own diagnosis says cannot grade. Close behind, for a shared reason: entropy production on the page (my Idea 5, mathematician 6). With about 20 states and 300 transitions the estimator is biased upward by empty reverse cells, and the bias scales as 1/length, so unshuffled it measures song length.

## 3. A new idea that needs several papers at once: the pinned string

Songwriter's appointments say *where* a song matters; the evolutionist's conservation says *what covers keep*; my identity information says *what makes it itself*. Conjecture: these are the same few sites. A great song is a string pinned at a handful of points and free between them.

Per note i compute three numbers:
- a_i, the appointment score (songwriter 3: the minimum of the standardised pitch, duration, metric, harmonic-arrival and stability streams);
- c_i, conservation across a fixed number of independent Lakh versions (evolutionist 3);
- k_i, identity stiffness: the drop in log p(song | tune), against the 20,000-tune confusion set, when note i is replaced by the corpus-typical alternative.

Tests: (i) do a_i and k_i predict c_i after partialling out metric weight and duration; (ii) song level: pinning concentration = share of sum k_i held by the top 5% of notes, and mean free length = notes between pins; (iii) alignment = cosine of the a and k vectors. Prediction: high concentration, long free length and high alignment grade the cover half of the yardstick (the evolutionist's breeding value). All data are in hand. Wrong if c_i is explained by metric weight and duration alone, or the three song-level numbers are flat. Odds 20%. It matters because it is the only route by which the "star-shaped cloud" could be predicted from one specimen before any cover exists; without that, the cloud idea is a restatement of the yardstick.

## 4. Votes

## 5. The theory the room is converging on

*A great song is a small rigid core with a large free periphery, a precisely specified invitation, and a great record leaves audible room around that core.*

I half believe it. For the "covered" half I give it about even odds as a predictive claim, with the warning that it is near-circular until the core is predicted from a single specimen. For the "still loved" half I doubt it: the neuroscientist's point that a record is loved for detail memory cannot exhaust describes a rich fibre, which is the opposite of an empty one. And seven readers of one brief agreeing is weak evidence; we were all handed "sparse" and "covered" and drew the same picture.
