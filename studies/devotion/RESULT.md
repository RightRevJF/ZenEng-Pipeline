# Devotion: the songs people return to (19 Nov 2026). Study only; nothing built.
last.fm listeners (breadth) and plays; devotion = plays per listener with listeners and release year held. Voetter et al. HSP-S (7,357 tracks, previews)
and HSP-L (64,507 tracks, whole recordings, all genres, hits and non-hits). Rules in PREDICTION.md; the genre-held reading was made primary after HSP-S and before HSP-L.

D1 concentrated spectrum against devotion. HSP-S: -0.036 as first stated (FAIL), +0.050 with genre held. HSP-L, genre held: -0.002 (FAIL); not held: -0.146.
   It does go with breadth a little (+0.044 genre held). The sparse, concentrated sound that marks lasting Hot 100 hits does not mark the tracks last.fm listeners replay.
D2 mood classifiers. Happy: -0.049 on HSP-L (PASS), -0.044 on HSP-S genre held. Sad: nothing. Dark timbre +0.095, danceable -0.038.
D3 a reader over 53 sound means, half to half, against devotion: +0.221 genre held (+0.335 not held) on HSP-L; +0.141 (+0.204) on HSP-S. PASS.
   The same reader built for breadth: +0.152 (+0.236). Sound says more about who comes back than about how many came.
Single readings for devotion on HSP-L, genre held, same sign in both halves: sound events per second -0.117; Essentia danceability -0.113; spectral strong peak -0.111;
   chord changes per second -0.095; spectral complexity +0.095; erb-band crest -0.065.
Agrees with the Hot 100 findings: fewer events per second, less made for dancing, less cheerful. Disagrees: here devotion goes with a fuller, more complex spectrum, not a sparser one.
Caution: last.fm's listeners are a particular crowd (scrobblers of the 2000s), and Essentia's genre classes are crude.
