## By the rule

- **Named (up): memory, for the spread within lines** (inside the kept middle half before, outside after, in four songs of six).
- **Not named by the letter: borrow.** Before its rung the spread between lines is just over the top of the kept middle half in four songs (2.40 against 2.38), so "inside before, outside after" is not met; it goes from just over to just inside the bottom. I report it as the finding and say plainly that the rule did not name it.

## What it means

The verse is not narrow because the draw is timid. The draw alone ranges as widely as kept songs. The verse is narrow because **the engine repeats where kept songs vary**: to reach the kept share of bars that sing a figure already sung, it has a line sing an earlier line again at the same pitch, and it does so in most verses. A kept song reaches that share another way too. A figure in the engine's own measure is a pattern of intervals, and a pattern of intervals is the same pattern a third higher. Kept lines answer each other at different heights; the engine's answer in the same place.

That would explain, at one stroke, the lines all starting on the same note, the verse's notes piled on few pitches, the two-chord verses having nowhere to go, and song 50's pool. And it says what the mend is not: not a wider register, not a looser chord, not line centres. **It is borrow done as kept songs do it: the earlier line's figure, at a new height.**
