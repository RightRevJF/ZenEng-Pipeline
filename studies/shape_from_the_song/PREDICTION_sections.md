# Does Luthier's own rule of movement hold in kept songs? The rule, written before the data is read

**The theory (the paper, Part XII, the melodic contour checklist).** "(5) Verse uses mostly stepwise motion. (6) Chorus includes at least one leap of a fourth or fifth on a weighted word." And (1) to (3): the verse sits in the middle of the range, leaving room to rise; the highest pitch is kept for the chorus or bridge.

If the engine is to decide a tune's movement from its own theory, the theory should be true of the songs people kept.

**Data.** The kept 4/4 songs with both a verse and a chorus marked, each with at least 30 moves in each.

**Read, song by song:** the share of moves by step (one or two semitones) in the verses and in the choruses; the share of leaps (five semitones or more); the share of repeated notes; and each part's highest note.

**The rule.**

- "Verses step more" holds if the verse's step share is over the chorus's in at least 60 songs in 100, and the middle difference is at least 0.02.
- "Choruses leap more" holds if the chorus's leap share is over the verse's in at least 60 in 100, and the middle difference is at least 0.01.
- "The top is the chorus's" holds if the chorus's highest note is at or over the verse's in at least 60 in 100.

A part of the theory that holds is built into how the engine moves. A part that does not hold is not built, and the paper's checklist is corrected to say what the kept songs do.
