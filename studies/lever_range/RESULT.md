The rule: the lever may take a line of n syllables if kept verse lines that long take one bar at least as often as 8-syllable lines do (9 in 100). Lines of 9, 10 and 11 stand at 4, 5 and 4: under it. At a slow tempo, lines of 10 and 11 stand over the slow 8's 21, but on 24 and 17 lines; 9 does not (15, on 26).

**What follows for song 61** (80 beats a minute, a bar of 3 seconds, verse lines of 8 to 11): by the rule over all tempos the lever should not reach its lines. By the slow-tempo row it might reach the 10s and 11s, which is too thin to build on. Its remedy is more likely the second carrier (bars with nothing sung, 749.144) or the lyric.

**Two things the table says that were not asked.**
- **The form takes 8-syllable lines far more often than kept songs do.** It gives two to four of a verse's eight lines one bar. Kept 8-syllable verse lines take one bar 9 times in 100, 6-syllable lines 28. Song 49, the hold-out, has verses of 8-syllable lines, and its words' fit fell (`studies/busy_sections/RESULT_build.md`).
- **At a fast tempo the form should hardly act at all:** kept 7 and 8-syllable verse lines take one bar 3 and 1 times in 100 when a bar is under 1.8 seconds (over 133 beats a minute). No song run so far is that fast.

**Against what I expected:** the share falls as lines grow, as I expected; I did not expect the slow-tempo rise at 10 and 11.

**A fault of mine, caught before it was reported.** My first run read the kept corpus's own line index, which marks longer phrases, and found that no 8-syllable line ever takes one bar. That was the wrong measure of a line, the same kind of fault as 749.128. The study now reads the transcribers' lyric lines, as `studies/metre` does and as a Luthier lyric's lines are.
