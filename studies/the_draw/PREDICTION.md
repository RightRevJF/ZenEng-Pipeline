# The draw: two readings, their rules written first (749.182, 749.183)

25 November 2026. The list is `songs/p68/fixlist_v49.md`. Seen before writing: v48's results; the engine's code for the planted peak (fix 950) and for note lengths. Nothing of either reading has been run.

Both readings hold the fit still (749.179): each v47 record's own fitted spec is run again, a thousand builds, the seed the record used. Songs 55, 54, 58 and 49. Song 59 is not read here.

## A. Where the leaps past an octave are (749.182)

**The builds.** The octave gate off, every other gate on. For each living build, each leap past an octave inside a line, as sung:
- the part (verse, chorus, bridge) and how the line was made (fresh, a copy, a design);
- where in the line (its first interval, its last, between);
- whether it is one of a pair (another such leap within the next two intervals, back the other way);
- the far note (the one away from the line's body: the higher of an upward leap out and back): stressed or weak, its length, and **whether it is the build's highest pitch**.

Two runs a song: the spec as it is, and the spec with the planted peak taken out (`target.peak` removed), so the only difference is fix 950.

**The kept songs,** read the same way (`kept_corpus.kept_songs()`): how many hold such a leap, pairs, the far note's stress and length, whether it is the song's top.

**My guess, and the rule that would name it the cause.** Fix 950 plants the song's peak in one chorus line by raising that line's top while its centre stays, and the line's copies in later choruses carry it again. I think that is where most of these leaps are. The plant is named the cause if both hold:
1. in at least two songs of three (55, 54, 58), half or more of the builds' leaps have their far note on the build's highest pitch in a chorus line;
2. in at least two songs of three, with the plant taken out, the share of living builds holding any such leap is at or under the kept share of songs holding one (28 of 160, 17.5 in 100).

If only the first holds, the plant is a source and not the whole of it. If neither, the reading is reported and no change is tried on it.

**If the plant is the cause, the change to the draw** (to be tried under its own checks, written after the reading and before any run): the line that carries the peak is drawn until it reaches the peak *and* holds no leap past an octave, so the tune climbs to its top. Not a gate: it is the plant's own aim made narrower.

**What I expect.** Rule 1 to hold. Rule 2 I put at even: v46 had no octave gate and its builds were what the session first counted.

## B. Why a weak word is held long in a title line (749.183)

**The builds.** The spec as it is (the ban on, no title gate). For each living build, each title line (the spec's hooks), as sung without second notes of melismas: the longest note (the first of the longest), and if it is a weak syllable with another word after it:
- the syllable's kind (a one-syllable function word; a weak syllable of a longer word);
- its place in the line (first note; later);
- whether the line is fresh or a copy fitted to new words;
- whether it is the note before the widest gap between onsets in the line (so the rhythm left a hole after it), and whether a stressed syllable in the line is as long as it lacks by a sixteenth, an eighth, or more.

**The kept songs:** the kept title lines whose longest note is a weak syllable before the last word (9 in 100): the same four. And for all kept title lines and all the builds' title lines, the mean length by syllable kind (stressed, weak, function word).

**Rule.** A source is named if it holds half or more of the builds' cases in at least two songs of the four, and is at most half as common among the kept cases. Otherwise the reading is reported and nothing is tried on it.

**What I expect.** Most cases to be one-syllable function words sitting before a hole in a kept two-bar rhythm, in a line that is a copy fitted to new words (a rhythm made for other words). I hold this loosely: I have been wrong twice about this gate's cost.
