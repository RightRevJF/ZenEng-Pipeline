# Agent 01: Line and sound (rhyme, line craft, sound patterning)

Round 2, 25 November 2026. Sources found with Consensus (allowance ran out after 6 searches) and then WebSearch/WebFetch. No lyrics or poem lines reproduced; numbers and paraphrase only.

## 1. Summary

The best-supported line-and-sound effects are about the **listener's processing**, not about acclaim. In controlled experiments, rhyme (and to a lesser degree metre and other repetition, which these papers call "parallelism") makes verse feel more beautiful, more moving, easier to understand and easier to remember. Rhymed statements also feel truer (the "rhyme-as-reason" effect). Chills tend to come at line, stanza and poem endings, where a rhyme closes. These results hold across German, Romanian, Croatian and English texts. But nearly all of them use lay readers, and nearly all measure liking, memory or felt emotion. **No study I found links rhyme type, rhyme density, line length or enjambment to critical acclaim or canon status in song.** In literary poetry the expert signal runs the other way. Award-level contemporary poets use much less perfect end rhyme and alliteration than amateurs. Lay readers, meanwhile, prefer AI poems, which rhyme heavily and regularly. This repeats the two-channel split from round 1: rhyme serves the audience channel, and what critics prize is something else. Three findings matter most for our process:
- **Bending syntax to land a rhyme costs both liking and comprehension.** Rhyme's own benefit is larger than that cost, so the answer is to keep rhyme and refuse the forced inversion.
- **Stock rhyme pairs are measurable and are more common in #1 hits.** That makes them an audience-channel trait, not an acclaim trait.
- **Rhyme density is a genre and writer fingerprint, not a quality score.** A machine beat the best human rappers on density.

Sound symbolism (individual phonemes carrying emotion) is contested, and word meaning swamps it. Line-length rules and Pattison's stable/unstable rhyme-scheme theory are untested craft lore.

**Correction to round 1 (reading.md, section B):** Kao & Jurafsky (2012) did not find professionals use "richer sound devices". They found professional poems had significantly *less* perfect end rhyme (odds ratio 0.12 per SD) and *less* alliteration (OR 0.53). Slant rhyme, assonance and consonance did not differ.

## 3. Testable next (rule first, on data with line breaks)

All five need a lyrics corpus that keeps line breaks (our round-1 corpus lacked them), plus a phonetic dictionary (CMUdict) and a rhyme tagger (Hirjee-style probabilistic scoring or RhymeTagger).

1. **Stock rhyme-pair rate.** Measure: share of line-end rhyme pairs that fall in the corpus's top 200 pairs (frequency, tf-idf against prose). Data: Billboard set with lines, plus the Rolling Stone flags. Predict: lower for acclaimed writers across writers (like stock phrases), null within writer. Check our drafts' percentile.
2. **Forced-rhyme deviation.** Measure: share of rhymed line-ends where the rhyme word sits out of canonical order (parser flags an inverted object or adjective, or a filler auxiliary such as an emphatic "do"). Data: same corpus plus our drafts. Predict: lower in acclaimed songs, and higher in machine drafts than in human hits.
3. **Rhyme-type mix.** Measure: perfect : slant : none at line ends, plus internal-rhyme rate per line. Data: same. Predict two-sided (exploratory): acclaimed writers use more slant rhyme than other hit-makers. Expect strong genre and era confounds, so control for decade and genre.
4. **Sense-carrying rhymes.** Measure: semantic relatedness (embedding cosine) between rhyme partners, against random same-song word pairs. Data: same. Predict: acclaimed songs' rhyme pairs show more meaningful relation (link or contrast, measured as higher relatedness or a set antonymy share) than other hits. This rests on Rubin & Wallace and on Lea et al.
5. **Closure placement.** Measure: share of a song's highest-arousal words (affective norms) that land on stanza-final rhymes rather than mid-line. Data: lined corpus plus Warriner arousal norms. Predict: higher in acclaimed songs (based on Wassiliwizky). This also checks Jason's "pathos" note on our drafts.
