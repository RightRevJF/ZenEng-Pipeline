## Rulings in force
- **Power floor (R06-5):** a test counts toward the shared bar (.05 / number of counted tests) only if its power is 0.2 or more, from fake outcomes on the real design before the outcome is joined. Under the floor it is an estimate.
- **Range kill (R07-3):** a counted test is killed only if its prediction lies outside the estimate's 95% range; otherwise a non-pass is "unsettled".
- **Pass:** p < .05/K, predicted sign, same sign in both halves (hash of composer, or of the tune for traditional tunes), and the second model (Poisson on the artist count) of the same sign.
- A test written after seeing results is never in K; it is reported as a lead.

## THE NEW DATA (notes side; no outcome has ever been joined to any measure on it)
TheSession.org (github.com/adactio/TheSession-data): Irish and related traditional dance tunes, posted by members as ABC transcriptions; many tunes have several transcriptions ("settings") by different people. Melody only (single line), no reliable chords. Parsed by `run10/experiments/abcparse.py` (99.2% of 55,838 settings ok).
- Import `run10/experiments/common10.py` (add its folder to sys.path). `common10.tunes()` gives 23,089 tunes: per tune `type` (reel, jig, polka, waltz, hornpipe, slip jig, march, barndance, strathspey, slide, mazurka, three-two), `meter`, `mode` (e.g. "Gmajor", "Edorian"), `tonic_pc`, `mode_name`, `year` (first posting), `composer` (None for traditional), `split` (A = this pass's tests, 14,511 tunes; C = confirmation; H = sealed), `mel` = the first setting, expanded with repeats: dict of lists `pitch` (MIDI), `dur` and `onset` (quarter notes), `bar` (bar index), `beatpos` (position in the bar in beats of the meter; quarter for x/4, dotted quarter for 6/8, 9/8, 12/8); `written` (as written, repeats not expanded); `other` = a second setting's expanded melody or None (use it for stability checks); `n_settings_ok` (NEVER use as a measure or control: it is downstream of popularity).
- `common10.baseline(mel, tonic_pc)` gives the renaming baselines: ln_notes, density (notes per bar), n_pitch, n_pc, range, int_entropy, n_int, step_share, leap_share, unison_share, pc_entropy, tonic_share, bar_rep. `common10.rows('A', measure_fn)` builds rows; `common10.renaming(R, col)` gives Spearman with each baseline and R^2 on all of them; `common10.stability(measure_fn, key=col)` gives the Spearman between a tune's first and second settings.
- Outcomes (held by the chair, never by you): y_cov = log(1 + distinct recording artists of the tune) (covers by other artists, the primary yardstick) and y_love = log(tunebooks) (members who put the tune in their tunebook, for tunes with 10 or more; musicians choosing to learn it). Model: OLS of z(y) on z(measure) with fixed effects for type, meter, mode, first-posting year and composer (2+ tunes), cluster SEs by composer. With 14,511 tunes the SE is about 0.009, so power at alpha .05/8 is about 0.6 for an effect of 0.03 SD per SD and near 1 for 0.05.
- Prior evidence on this same site (run 02's card 2, McBride & Leroi): tunes differ durably in take-up, prestige of recordings explained about 16 points of it and melody about 3; simpler tunes (fewer distinct pitches, fewer notes) spread a little better. This pass's card 2 (McBride & Fitch): across versions, common pitches, strong beats and small steps are conserved; variation concentrates at bar ends and weak beats.
