# Judging research, week 0 (read by hand, 20 November 2026)

The free papers from the writer's list, read before the weekly runs began. Each is in ledger.json with how much was read.

1. Levitin, Chordia & Menon (2012): notated rhythm is 1/f-like across four centuries; shuffling the order flattens it; composers differ in rhythm far more than in pitch. Luthier's "rhythm scaling" is the same family of reading, and its songs fail it together.
2. Pearce (2018): IDyOM's surprise predicts expectation, memory, similarity and phrase boundaries; more predictable passages read more pleasant, the inverted U is unresolved.
3. Salimpoor et al. (2011): anticipation (caudate) and the peak (nucleus accumbens) release dopamine at different moments; what in the music builds anticipation is not identified.
4. Jacoby et al. (2024): rhythm priors peak at small-integer ratios everywhere; which ones matter most depends on local music.
5. McPherson et al. (2025): consonance preference grows with exposure; roughness aversion is shared.

Common thread: none measures greatness. Together they say what a score can carry that listeners respond to: ordered rhythmic structure across scales, calibrated surprise, built-up anticipation before a peak, simple-ratio rhythms, low roughness. The cultural part (consonance, which ratios) is learned.

Leads for the weekly agent: the 2017 and 2016 originals (paywalled), Gingras et al. 2015 (timing slows before high-surprise notes; tension), Sauvé et al. 2018 (rhythm predictability and valence), Agres et al. 2018 (memory and predictability), Cheung et al. 2019 (uncertainty and surprise in chord pleasure, Current Biology).
