The rule asked one cause to hold half in song 55 and half in the other five. A holds 72 in 100 in song 55 and 35 in the others; D the reverse (21 and 64). Not named.

**A, as expected, is song 55's.** A copied line is its source's notes as they stand; where the plan gives the return another chord, a long note that sat well under the first chord sits a semitone from a tone of the second, and no rule looks. Song 54 has it too (56 in 100 of a smaller count).

**D is not what I thought it might be.** I wrote that if D were the largest I would not understand where the source's clash came from, since the draw forbids one on a long note. It comes from after the draw. Counted once D had shown itself: nearly all of D is on **a melisma's second note** (848), the scale step below the first, added after the tune is chosen with no look at the chord. Where that second note is a beat long or more and lands a semitone from a chord tone, it is a clash that neither the draw's rule nor `again()`'s ever sees. It is carried into every return of the line, which is why it shows here as "the same chord as the source's". It is 60 in 100 of the other five songs' returning-line clashes and all of song 50's. It must be in fresh lines too (`studies/borrow_kept/clash_read.json`: fresh lines' clashes "long only as sung", 0.03 to 0.07 per 100 notes).

B does not happen: where `again()`'s rule looks, it finds a chord tone. C hardly happens.

**What follows, for the thread to agree, since the rule named neither:** two forms, each small, each with its own marks first.
1. **The copy:** the clash rule of 833 applied to a copied line's long notes where the chord under them differs from the source's.
2. **The melisma's second note:** where the step below would be a clash a beat long or more, take the step above if that is clear of the chord, else leave the syllable on one note (the melisma moves to another candidate, so the song's share of melismas is kept).
