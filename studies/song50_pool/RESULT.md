That is a reading with a named stage, and one step of guess beyond it (952). It wants its own reading before anything is changed: how kept folk songs' verses spread their repeated notes over pitches, against what 952 makes of the family's share. It should be done before song 50 is made for real, as the list says, and it bears on songs 58 and 49 as much.

**The chosen tunes' words' fit is a draw.** Song 50's builds fit their words at 0.193, 0.248, 0.299 (quartiles) under 964 and 0.183, 0.248, 0.292 under v47. With a pool of four or five there is little to choose among, and the choice is by range and order, not by fit. The fall from 0.399 to 0.209 is v47's lucky draw more than 964's unlucky one.

## B. The nit in 964 (749.196): it is not rare, and I would leave it

Of the notes the draw weighs with a note before them, a side has pitches past the octave and no octave among its candidates 9.6, 16.3 and 19.1 times in a thousand (songs 55, 54, 58). The session's line for "leave it" was 1 in 1,000. It is well over.

But I do not think the octave's share should pass to the pitches beyond it. Fix 964 gives each kind of leap its own kept odds: the octave its share of the bin, the pitches past it the rest. Where a rule has taken the octave away (a clash with the chord, mostly), the pitches past it still have exactly their kept odds. Handing them the octave's share as well would offer the ninth and the tenth at the weight kept songs give to the octave and everything beyond together, for that note: the fault of 749.192 again, in small. An octave that cannot be sung there is like any other pitch a rule removes; its weight is simply not drawn.

So: counted, not rare, and left as it is on purpose. The comment in the code says so. If the session rules the other way, it is a change to the draw and wants its marks first, read both ways.
