# Hymn tunes: does the session finding carry over? — rules written 19 Nov 2026 before measuring

Data: Hymnary.org's own tune export, every tune listed under 27 common metres: 11,532 tunes, each with the number of hymnals it is printed in
(median 2, top 2,430: OLD HUNDREDTH) and its incipit, the opening 15-20 notes as scale degrees. No rhythm and no notes past the opening: a thin page.
Yardstick: the number of hymnals. Three centuries of editors choosing, no recording, no star, and the words separable (a tune is listed apart from any text).
Held in every reading: the metre class, major or minor, and the incipit's length. Tunes are compared only within their metre.

The session-tune parts, with the signs they had there, computed on the incipit, nothing refitted:
 pentatonic share +, repeated notes +, time on the tonic +, interval variety -, pitch variety -, leaps -
 H1  "plainness" = the equal-weight mean of those six (each standardised, signs as above): partial rank correlation with hymnal count > 0, p < .001.
 H2  each part keeps its session sign (reported; four of six with p < .01 would count as carrying over).
Known weaknesses, stated first: no dates in the export, and older tunes may be both plainer and in more hymnals; where the composer's birth year is given it
is held as a check. Hymnal counts also grow by editors copying editors. Nothing goes into the engine on this study.
