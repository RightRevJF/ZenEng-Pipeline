## What it is

- The three songs' verses have **two chords**, with half the bars on one. Songs 55 and 54 have six, a quarter of the bars on the commonest. (`chords.json`. Song 61's verse has two; song 60's three; song 59's five.)
- The pitch piled on is **the fifth of the key** in seven builds of ten: the tone the verse's two chords share (I and V; in song 50, iii and I and V). It sits a semitone under the verse's centre. The verses use six different pitches where songs 55 and 54 use eight.
- The harmony reaches the tune by two roads I can name, and they carry about half of it between them: the draw's pull to chord tones on the beat, and fix 833's clash rule (no long note a semitone from a chord tone). Either alone does little; both off, 30 to 41. The rest comes by a road I have not found.

## What I had wrong, in order

1. That it was the low voice (`song50_pool`): no, the pitch is mid-span.
2. That it was 952's family share of repeated notes: no, folk and pop are one family and ask the same share.
3. That it was the chord's pull: halved, or gone, it barely moves.

The genre label was a decoy. "Folk" matters only because the chord writer gives these songs' verses two chords.

## Do kept songs do this?

A little, and nothing like as much. In kept verses the share of notes on the commonest pitch runs 0.26, 0.31, 0.36 at the quartiles and 0.44 at the 90th. In the quarter of kept verses with the stillest harmony (most notes under one chord) it runs 0.27, 0.34, 0.42, and 22 in 100 are over 0.44; in the quarter with the most moving harmony, 5 in 100. So still harmony does make kept melodies sit on a pitch more, by about 0.04 at the middle. The engine's verses under still harmony sit at 0.46 to 0.50 at the middle, and 56 to 65 in 100 are over.

So two chords in a verse is ordinary (the kept middle verse has 43 notes in 100 under its commonest chord; these have 50 bars in 100), and a ceiling of 0.44 is fair to ask even of still verses (78 kept still verses in 100 meet it). **The fault is the melody's answer to still harmony: about three times too strong.**

## What I would do next, and what I would not

- **Not** loosen the ceiling (956) for still verses: kept songs do not need it loosened.
- **Not** make the chord writer avoid two-chord verses: they are what kept songs have.
- **Read first:** in kept verses, how the melody treats a chord that stays. When the same chord holds for bars, do kept melodies keep landing on its tones on the beat as often as the engine's 2.6 to 0.3 assumes, or do they move off them the longer it holds? If the kept pull weakens as a chord stays, the engine's should; that is a table (the kept share of on-beat chord tones by how long the chord has held) and then a change to the draw, with marks first. The same reading should look for the road I have not found (the figure memory of 923 is where I would look: under one chord the same figures are the favoured ones, and it favours what has been sung).
- Song 50 should wait for that before it is made for real. Songs 58 and 49 will gain by it too.
