**What it does show.**
- A kept song's line that repeats, repeats at the same pitch. So "sing the borrowed line at a kept height" has no kept ground, and I withdraw it as the form.
- The borrow (818) is fitted to 0.8 in all six records, yet in songs 55, 54 and 59 only 3 to 5 lines in 100 come out as a repeat by this measure. The engine's "sing it again" changes the line (its notes are re-set to the chords and the words) until it is no longer the same figure; what it keeps is the line's height. So the borrow, as built, gives kept songs' sameness of height without kept songs' sameness of figure.
- The three songs over the kept 75th (58, 50, 49) are the three whose verses sit on one pitch (749.196). There the borrowed line does survive as a repeat, and a quarter to near half of the verse's lines are the same line at the same pitch. This is seen after the fact and is not a named result.
