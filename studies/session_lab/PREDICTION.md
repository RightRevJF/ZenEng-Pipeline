# The no-recording laboratory: Irish session tunes — rules written 19 Nov 2026 before measuring

Why here: a session tune has no canonical record, no star, no chart and no words; it lives only as a page and in players' hands.
Data in hand: The Session's public dump. 23,385 tunes (first submitted setting used as the page), and for each
 tunebooks  = how many players have saved it to their tunebook (listed from 10 up; unlisted counted as under 10)
 recordings = how many commercial recordings include it (0 if none)
Yardstick: Gs = mean of the two, each ranked. This is "still played and recorded by many", the nearest thing here to "loved and covered".
The whole range is present, from tunes nobody plays to Drowsy Maggie; among Hot 100 hits it was not.
Known confound, stated first: famous tunes were submitted to the site earliest. Every reading is given with the tune's submission order held as well
as tune type (reel, jig, ...), mode and length; holding submission order takes out some real signal too, so that figure is a floor.

 L1  A ridge reader over about twenty plain page measures (range, step size, leaps, repeated notes, turns, pitch/interval/rhythm variety, long notes,
     notes per bar, where the top note falls, time on tonic and fifth, ending on the tonic, bars repeated, compressibility, part contrast, pentatonic
     share, arpeggio share, scaling exponent S), fitted on half the tunes and judged on the other half, both ways:
     partial rank correlation with Gs > 0, p < .001, with submission order held. If it is under +0.05 the page fails here too.
 L2  (the physicist's fair trial of S = 1 + g/pi) Gs against S is an inverted U peaked between 0.8 and 1.2: a negative squared term, p < .01,
     with the fitted peak in that range, on tunes of 128+ notes.
Reported, no pass rule: each measure alone with its sign in both halves; tunebooks and recordings apart; tunes submitted after 2010 only.
Nothing goes into the engine on this study.
