## 1. What learned aesthetic or quality predictors exist, what is the label, how well do they track humans, and are they validated per piece?

### Takeaway
Every rating-trained per-piece predictor found is audio-only, and on unseen generators its per-piece rank correlation with experts falls from about 0.9 (in-distribution) to about 0.62 to 0.68. The newest open metric (MuQ-Eval) is explicitly reported as sensitive to signal artefacts and insensitive to musical-structural distortion, which is the opposite of what a judge of identically rendered melodies needs. A 2025 survey confirms no validated symbolic per-piece quality predictor exists.

### Cited Findings
- [AUDIO] MuQ-Eval (Zhu & Li, March 2026): frozen MuQ-310M encoder with attention pooling and a two-layer MLP, trained on MusicEval (clips from 31 text-to-music systems, expert MOS). Utterance-level SRCC 0.838, system-level SRCC 0.957. Adding training objectives or adaptation did not meaningfully beat the frozen baseline; encoder choice mattered more than architecture. A controlled degradation analysis found the metric "selectively sensitive to signal-level artifacts" and insensitive to musical-structural distortions. LoRA adaptation from as few as 150 clips gives "usable correlation" and supports personalised evaluators from one listener's annotations (no number in abstract). Open-source code and weights. — [arXiv 2603.22677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22677)
- [AUDIO] ICASSP 2026 Automatic Song Aesthetics Evaluation Challenge (built on SongEval): over 70 teams registered; 18 valid Track 1 submissions, 16 Track 2. Track 1 (overall musicality) is scored on a Regular set (5 seen generators) and a Hard set (5 seen plus 6 unseen generators), final = 0.2 Regular + 0.8 Hard. Winner (Hachimi; MuQ + WavLM fused by two-stage bidirectional cross-attention): LCC 0.703, SRCC 0.678, Kendall tau 0.538, top-tier accuracy 0.383, final 0.575. Baseline (UTMOS-style, from SongEval): LCC 0.631, SRCC 0.622, tau 0.491, top-tier accuracy 0.295, final 0.510. Track 2 winner (LeVo, mixture-of-experts over pretrained backbones): per-dimension composite scores 0.588 to 0.697, final 0.655 versus baseline 0.574. The organisers state top-tier accuracy was "generally modest" and that models were "frequently under-scoring top-tier tracks". Caveat: the fetched tables give one value per team, not split by utterance/system level or by Regular/Hard, and the column-to-dimension mapping for Track 2 was ambiguous in extraction. — [arXiv 2601.07237](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07237v1)
- [AUDIO] SongEval's own in-distribution figures for comparison: 2,399 songs (2,199 train, 200 test plus 50 real songs), generated by DiffRhythm, Suno, YuE, Mureka, Udio and others. Best utterance-level LCC/SRCC: coherence 0.917/0.898, musicality 0.916/0.901, naturalness 0.909/0.896; best system-level SRCC 0.970. Off-the-shelf correlations with the same human ratings: vocal range 0.702, Audiobox CU 0.662, PQ 0.630, CE 0.614, PC 0.408. The paper is inconsistent on annotators (16 professionals in the abstract; four per song in section 3.2). — [arXiv 2505.10793](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10793)
- [AUDIO, text, lyrics] CMI-RewardBench (Ma et al., ICML 2026, PMLR 306): a reward-model benchmark covering musicality, text-music alignment and compositional instruction alignment; CMI-Pref-Pseudo has 110k pseudo-labelled samples; CMI-Pref is a human-annotated corpus (size not given in the abstract); the parameter-efficient CMI-RM family "correlates strongly with human judgments" and "enables effective inference-time scaling via top-k filtering". No numbers were retrievable; inputs described are text, lyrics and reference audio, not symbolic. — [PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/ma26am.html)
- Survey of evaluation metrics for music generation (Sept 2025): symbolic evaluation "lacks standard benchmarks, representations and validated features" and symbolic quality assessment is "manual or rule-based"; the survey identifies no symbolic per-piece predictor validated against human ratings. It names PAM (reference-free; audio-language model contrasting "clear sound" vs "noisy sound" prompts), Audiobox Aesthetics (97,000 clips) and SongEval as the learned per-sample predictors, all audio. It reports that KAD (MMD-based) and MAD (MAUVE on MERT embeddings) "have shown better correlation with human preferences than FAD", that CLAP-score, FAD and KLD "often align poorly with human preferences", and that aesthetic predictors favour particular content such as "punchy kick" or "synth" (Zhang et al. 2025). No correlation values are given in the survey itself. — [arXiv 2509.00051](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00051v1)
- [SYMBOLIC, system-level only] In an ABC-notation generation study (9 LLMs, 180 human-rated samples, three annotators), the only automatic metric significantly tracking human style accuracy across models was KL divergence to a reference corpus (r = -0.73, p < 0.05, n = 9 models); scale adherence (r = -0.41) and ABC syntax accuracy (r = -0.06) were not significant. Inter-annotator Krippendorff alpha for the human ratings themselves was low: structure 0.437, harmoniousness 0.430, emotion 0.325. The authors note automatic metrics can be satisfied while outputs are stylistically unrecognisable. — [arXiv 2606.05522](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05522v1)
- [SYMBOLIC] GPT used as a judge on symbolic input can only modestly detect annotation errors: beat tracking 65.20%, chord extraction 64.80%, key estimation 59.72% accuracy (above a random baseline; accuracy rises with the amount of concept information in the prompt). This is error detection, not quality rating. — [arXiv 2501.13261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13261v1)

### Inferences
- The "Hard" track is the honest number for per-piece ranking by an audio aesthetic model on output from a generator it has not seen: SRCC about 0.68 for the best of 18 entrants, with the top tier specifically under-scored. For choosing the single best of 1,000 candidates, under-scoring the top is the worst kind of error.
- MuQ-Eval's degradation result plus SongEval's "vocal range alone gives 0.70" result point the same way: these models reward production, vocal and signal properties. With all 1,000 candidates rendered through one synthesis chain, the between-candidate variance these models can see is small, so render-then-score should be expected to be near noise for composition quality. This is an inference; no study has tested it on same-render candidates.
- The useful new idea from MuQ-Eval is not the metric but the data-efficiency claim: a head on frozen pretrained features can be personalised with about 150 labelled items. The symbolic analogue (a small head on CLaMP 2/3 or similar symbolic embeddings, trained on about 150 of the user's own keep/reject decisions) is untested in the literature but cheap.
- Ratings of generated symbolic music have alpha around 0.3 to 0.44 in the one study that reported it, which caps any predictor's attainable per-piece correlation well below the audio figures.

### Gaps
- CMI-RewardBench numbers (pairwise accuracy, CMI-Pref size, top-k gains) not retrieved: the arXiv page (2603.00610) was blocked and the PMLR and Hugging Face pages show the abstract only.
- No per-piece validation of CLaMP 2/3 similarity, per-song FMD, or any contrastive/discriminator score against human ratings was found; searches for a CLaMP 3 based symbolic quality score returned nothing relevant.
- AudioMOS Challenge 2025 results were not retrieved.
- PAM's own correlations were not retrieved.
- No LLM-as-judge study rating symbolic melody quality against human ratings was found; the two LLM studies found address error detection and generation, not rating.

## 2. Hit-song science on symbolic or score-derived features: accuracy, nulls and critiques

### Takeaway
No study in the window predicts chart success or streams from melody, harmony or rhythm features alone with a clean held-out test and a usable effect. Where modalities are ablated, artist metadata carries the prediction, intrinsic content (audio, lyrics, chords) adds a few percent over a mean-only baseline, and the headline accuracies that survive in the press have been shown to be leakage.

### Cited Findings
- Leakage audit (Kapoor & Narayanan, Princeton, 16 Aug 2023) of the June 2023 "97% accuracy" hit paper: the original data had 24 samples (one per song; three features averaged over 33 listeners); the authors oversampled to 10,000 synthetic samples before the train/test split, so train and test were nearly the same data. After fixing the split order and testing on the original data, accuracy was "little better than random". The sensors were cardiac although the paper says "neural" or "brain" about 70 times; no code was released. — [Princeton reproducibility page](https://reproducible.cs.princeton.edu/predicting-hits.html)
- [AUDIO + LYRICS + META] Eurovision as a hit-song-science test (Adamska & Reiss, TISMIR 8(1), 2025, pp. 93-107; contests 2008 to 2024; seven feature sets): intrinsic song features (audio and lyrics) alone "did not accurately predict rankings", explaining only a small share of variance; YouTube daily views were significant but possibly inflated by post-contest exposure; the best model combined intrinsic, public-appeal and contest-specific features (previous vote ratio, reciprocation, running order). Abstract gives no error metrics. — [TISMIR / DOAJ record, DOI 10.5334/tismir.214](https://doaj.org/article/d791cfe05c8240dcb6b0172419c41102)
- [AUDIO + LYRICS + META] "Lyrics Matter" (Choudhary, Rao & Bhattacharyya, IIT Bombay, arXiv Dec 2025): 74,206 tracks from SpotGenTrack; target Spotify popularity normalised to 0 to 1; 80/20 split. Test MAE by modality: all modalities 0.0772; without metadata 0.1082 (+40.2%); without lyric embeddings 0.0852 (+10.4%); audio only 0.1196; single modalities: metadata 0.0968, lyric embeddings 0.1193, low-level audio 0.1229, high-level audio 0.1272. Predictions are compressed toward the mean: no test prediction exceeds 0.8 although 1.2% of real songs do; artist popularity is the dominant SHAP/LIME feature. No R² reported. — [arXiv 2512.05508](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05508v1)
- [AUDIO descriptors] "Beyond Beats" (Jung & Mayer, Innsbruck, arXiv 2403.12079): about 30,000 Spotify tracks 1957 to 2020 (20,588 in the OLS). Test MAE on the 0 to 100 popularity score: mean-only baseline 17.55; OLS with genre 16.64; OLS without genre 17.05; XGBoost 16.47; random forest 16.31 (7.08% better than the mean). In-sample R² 8.7% with genre and year, 4.6% without. Genre and year are the strongest predictors; artist effects uncontrolled. — [arXiv 2403.12079](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12079)
- [AUDIO] Votter, Mayerl, Zangerle & Specht (SMC 2023), ordinal popularity classification on Essentia low- and high-level features, HSP-S (7,736 songs, balanced Hot 100 hits vs non-hits) and HSP-L (73,482 songs), 5-fold CV: Cohen's linear weighted kappa at 5 quantile classes is 0.255 (logistic baseline) to 0.276 (linear pairwise comparator) on HSP-S and 0.255 on HSP-L; at 40 classes 0.225 to 0.264. A pairwise comparator (which of two songs is more popular) with linear models beat MLP comparators. — [SMC 2023 PDF](https://dbis-web1.uibk.ac.at/sites/default/files/2023-10/voetter_smc_2023.pdf)
- [AUDIO + META] CNN on spectrograms plus Spotify features (Falah et al., arXiv 2505.07280, 2025): 1,967 songs from the top 100 artists; reports 95.68% accuracy and F1 0.978 "for the specified threshold" that is never defined, with artist popularity and followers among the inputs (artist popularity correlates 0.45 with track popularity); no R², AUC or cross-validation. — [arXiv 2505.07280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07280v1)
- [SYMBOLIC, chords] Hooktheory chord progressions plus Spotify features (Campagna, MSc thesis, Univ. Pisa, 2016-17; over 900 instances, 21 progressions): cross-validated R² about 0.51 to 0.55 for popularity regression and about 59% three-class accuracy (chance about 33%), but artist popularity dominates every model, Lasso shrinks nearly everything else to zero, and chord progressions were never chosen as a split; the author concludes they do not meaningfully affect popularity. Possible leakage: songs with several progressions become several rows. Older than the window; included as the only chord-symbol popularity test found. — [CNR IRIS PDF](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/fbf0c0f5-7fee-4112-817e-b8fed5ecf53d/prod_425777-doc_153760.pdf)
- [SYMBOLIC, MIDI] Wang (Stanford CS229 project, 2014; origin of the MIDI n-gram approach): 1,752 MIDI songs, 50/50; positive = ever top-10 on any Billboard chart since 1950, negative = artist never in any top 100. Features: instrument program numbers, pitch 2- and 3-grams tagged by instrument, inter-onset tempo values. 10-fold CV at cutoff 0.5: melody+instrument features precision 0.596, recall 0.633, F1 0.612; beat features 0.600 / 0.618 / 0.607. Ensemble precision 0.882 only at cutoff 0.998 with recall 0.279. Top 100 coefficients showed "no clear pattern". No held-out test; the negative class is defined by artist, so the task partly identifies artists and eras. — [CS229 PDF](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2014/Kedao%20Wang,%20Predicting%20Hit%20Songs%20with%20MIDI%20Musical%20Features.pdf)
- [SYMBOLIC-derived chord and melody features] Çalışkol (MSc thesis, Boğaziçi, 2019): hit vs non-hit benchmark sets for dance and rock; adding chord-progression features (from tonal harmony theory) and hand-designed melody features to Spotify features "may improve" prediction, with a "significant improvement" for rock using random forest. The record page gives no numbers. — [Boğaziçi archive](https://digitalarchive.library.bogazici.edu.tr/items/28d403b5-8927-4a4d-b17b-9148a0d8b2f0/full)

### Inferences
- The modality ablation in "Lyrics Matter" is the cleanest recent decomposition: removing metadata costs 40%, removing lyrics 10%, and content-only models (MAE about 0.12) are barely distinguishable from each other. Combined with Beyond Beats (7% better than predicting the mean), the realistic ceiling for content-only popularity prediction is a few percent of variance.
- Balanced-class symbolic results (Wang: F1 about 0.61 against 0.50 chance) are the best available estimate of how much a notes-only model can separate "hits" from "non-hits", and even that figure is inflated by artist- and era-defined negatives and by the lack of a temporal hold-out.
- All these models compress predictions toward the mean and under-predict the top. A judge that must pick the top 1 of 1,000 is working in exactly the region where these models are least informative.
- Popularity labels are therefore a poor training target for the judge. They may still serve as a weak prior in the pairwise form Votter et al. use (a linear comparator on feature differences), which is the formulation that performed best there.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, temporally held-out, notes-only (MIDI or lead-sheet) hit predictor was found for 2019 to 2026. The JNMR article 10.1080/09298215.2023.2282999 surfaced in search but was not opened; I do not know its content.
- Çalışkol's actual accuracies were not retrieved (thesis PDF not opened).
- Recalled, not re-verified this session (and already in earlier notes): harmonic-surprise findings on Billboard songs (Miles, Rosen & Grzywacz) and melodic-complexity trends (Hamilton & Pearce). No new numbers added here.
- TikTok/short-form virality prediction from musical content: nothing found.

## 3. Predicting human pairwise preference between two melodies or two generated songs

### Takeaway
The largest pairwise-preference model in music (MusicRL, 300,000 pairs) predicts held-out choices at about 60%, and the authors' own team agreed with the crowd's choices only 60% of the time, so about 60% looks like the noise ceiling for single anonymous pairwise votes on 20-second clips. No model predicting pairwise preference between two symbolic melodies was found.

### Cited Findings
- [AUDIO] MusicRL (Cideron et al., Google DeepMind, ICML 2024): about 300,000 pairwise preferences collected from users (two 20-second clips per prompt; kept only when both were played); 285,000 train / 15,000 evaluation pairs. A Bradley-Terry reward model over caption text and audio tokens, initialised from the MusicLM checkpoint, reaches about 60% accuracy on held-out pairs; trained from scratch it failed to beat chance. In an internal check of 156 comparisons the authors agreed with the dataset's preference 60% of the time. — [arXiv 2402.04229](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.04229)
- What does and does not explain those preferences (same paper): the clip with the higher text-adherence (MuLan) score matched the preferred clip 51.6% of the time; a quality predictor matched 53.3%; removing the text input left reward-model accuracy unchanged; cropping the audio from 10 s to 3 to 5 s cut accuracy from 60% to 56%. The authors conclude preference "is influenced by music elements which go beyond text adherence and audio quality". — [arXiv 2402.04229](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.04229)
- Using the weak reward still helped at the system level: against the MusicLM baseline, human raters (101 prompts, three raters each) preferred MusicRL-R (text adherence + quality rewards) 83% of the time, MusicRL-U (the 60%-accurate user-preference reward) 74%, and MusicRL-RU 87%; MOS 3.07 (baseline), 3.54, 3.54, 3.82. — [arXiv 2402.04229](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.04229)
- [AUDIO] Music Arena (2025, v2): live pairwise battles between anonymised text-to-music systems; 1,420 battles, 373 users, 1,051 votes from 28 July to 31 August 2025; Bradley-Terry "Arena Score"; minimum listening time (about 4 s per track) before voting; 193 of 373 users voted once. The paper does not test any automatic metric against the votes (listed as future work) and excludes symbolic generation. — [arXiv 2507.20900](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20900v2)
- [AUDIO] Pairwise popularity comparator (Votter et al. 2023): predicting which of two songs is more popular from the difference of Essentia feature vectors with a linear model gave the best ordinal results (kappa 0.276 at 5 quantile classes on HSP-S) and beat MLP comparators. — [SMC 2023 PDF](https://dbis-web1.uibk.ac.at/sites/default/files/2023-10/voetter_smc_2023.pdf)
- Context dependence of melody liking (Albury, Bianco, Gold & Penhune, 2023): the same melodies were rated differently for liking and predictability depending on which other stimuli they were heard alongside; repertoire excerpts were more liked and judged less predictable when mixed with experimenter-composed melodies than when heard alone, and the experimental melodies showed the opposite shift. (Abstract only; no numbers retrieved.) — [PubMed 38034280](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38034280/)

### Inferences
- A reward that is right 60% per pair still moved system-level preference to 74%. Weak per-pair signal aggregates when it is applied across a whole distribution, but 60% per pair cannot reliably identify the single best item: used to rank 1,000 candidates, its top pick is only modestly better than a random draw from the upper part of the pool. The practical use is shortlisting (cut 1,000 to perhaps 50), not crowning.
- The crop result (60% to 56% when shortened to 3 to 5 s) is direct evidence that preference depends on structure over at least 10 seconds; any pairwise model for melodies should see whole sections, not bars.
- Pretraining was necessary (from-scratch reward at chance with 285,000 pairs). A symbolic pairwise model trained from scratch on a few hundred of the user's choices should be expected to fail unless it sits on pretrained symbolic embeddings or hand-built features with a linear comparator, the form that won in Votter et al.
- The context effect means pairwise or pool-relative judgments are not stable properties of a melody: a judge calibrated on "engine output vs. engine output" may not transfer to "engine output vs. real songs". This supports scoring against the fixed kept corpus as the reader already does.

### Gaps
- No published model predicting human pairwise preference between two symbolic melodies or lead sheets, and no accuracy figure for one.
- CMI-RewardBench pairwise accuracies not retrieved (see section 1).
- Music Arena meta-evaluation of automatic metrics had not been published in the version read.

## 4. Which feature families carry the signal when a signal exists?

### Takeaway
In the studies read this round, the content features that carry any out-of-sample signal are coarse (genre, era, loudness, instrumentalness, lyric semantics), while chord-progression identity carried none and melody n-grams showed no interpretable pattern. The only robust, replicated content-level regularity with a meaningful effect size is the inverted-U relation between note predictability and pleasantness, which the reader's surprise model already uses.

### Cited Findings
- Chord-progression identity carried no popularity signal once artist popularity was in the model (never selected as a regression-tree split; Lasso kept only artist popularity); instrumentalness was the second most influential feature, negative. — [Campagna thesis](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/fbf0c0f5-7fee-4112-817e-b8fed5ecf53d/prod_425777-doc_153760.pdf)
- MIDI pitch n-grams, instrument programs and inter-onset tempo features each gave about 60% precision on a balanced task; combining melody/instrument with beat features helped more than either alone; the 100 largest coefficients showed "no clear pattern". — [Wang, CS229](https://cs229.stanford.edu/proj2014/Kedao%20Wang,%20Predicting%20Hit%20Songs%20with%20MIDI%20Musical%20Features.pdf)
- Lyric semantic embeddings alone (test MAE 0.1193) are about as informative as low-level audio (0.1229) or high-level audio descriptors (0.1272); lyrics plus metadata (0.0805) nearly match the full model (0.0772), so low-level audio is largely redundant given lyrics. — [arXiv 2512.05508](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05508v1)
- Among Spotify descriptors, genre and release year dominate; loudness is positive; energy, instrumentalness and duration negative; danceability ranks lowest in both tree models; total gain over a mean-only predictor 7%. — [arXiv 2403.12079](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12079)
- Longer context matters for preference: a pairwise reward's accuracy fell from 60% to 56% when its input was cut from 10 s to 3 to 5 s, while text adherence (51.6%) and a quality predictor (53.3%) were near chance at predicting which clip users preferred. — [arXiv 2402.04229](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.04229)
- Predictability and pleasantness, note level (Zioga, Harrison, Pearce, Bhattacharya & Luft, Scientific Reports 2024; 40 non-musicians, 18 artificial-grammar melodies, IDyOM information content): pleasantness is an inverted U over note probability, quadratic effect F(1,39) = 21.967, p < .001, partial eta² = .360 (nine bins) and F(1,39) = 24.394, eta² = .385 (three levels); medium vs low d = 0.853, medium vs high d = 0.525. People who preferred medium-probability notes also composed melodies judged more creative (probability × creativity quadratic F(1,33) = 9.754, p = .004, eta² = .228). Rated pleasantness and rated creativity of the participants' own compositions correlated r = 0.907; four expert judges, ICC = 0.56. — [Scientific Reports 14:19048](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/37446/8/s41598-024-70027-z.pdf)
- Structure-aware inputs are what challenge entrants added to beat the baseline for song aesthetics: one top Track 1 system added a SongFormer structure branch to SSL backbones; another used multi-scale features with ranking-aware optimisation. — [arXiv 2601.07237](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07237v1)
- Divergence of a generated set's feature distribution from a reference corpus was the only automatic symbolic metric that tracked human style judgments (r = -0.73 across nine systems); rule checks (scale adherence, syntax validity) did not. — [arXiv 2606.05522](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05522v1)

### Inferences
- New vectors suggested by this evidence, beyond the reader's three existing instruments:
  1. Lyric-side features. Lyrics are the one content modality whose popularity signal equals audio's and is available without rendering. A lyric-embedding or lyric-feature term is cheap, though it scores the lyric, not the melody, so it only discriminates candidates if the 1,000 candidates differ in text setting.
  2. A structure branch: explicit section/repetition descriptors as judge inputs, since both the challenge winners and the MusicRL crop test point to multi-second structure as where preference lives.
  3. A linear pairwise comparator on feature differences, trained on the user's own choices, in place of an absolute score.
  4. A small personalised head on frozen pretrained embeddings from about 150 labels (MuQ-Eval's recipe transferred to symbolic embeddings).
- Chord-progression identity and local pitch n-grams look like dead ends as popularity predictors; they may still matter as constraints.
- The inverted-U result is at note level with artificial melodies; it supports a two-sided (mid-band) charge on surprise, which the reader has, and does not by itself rank whole songs.

### Gaps
- No study found this round quantifying the out-of-sample contribution of repetition structure, compressibility, syncopation or lyric-melody alignment to popularity or rated quality in symbolic pop data. Earlier notes cover the correlational literature (Nunes on chorus repetition, Hooked/catchiness features); I did not find a predictive, held-out test of those families for 2019 to 2026.
- No ablation by feature family exists in the audio aesthetic-predictor papers; they use learned SSL embeddings.

## 5. Failure modes when a generator optimises against a predictor, and published remedies

### Takeaway
Two failure modes are documented with numbers in music: collapse onto a few safe, high-reward outputs (diversity roughly halved) and reward over-optimisation that degrades the objectives not being optimised. The published remedies are an adaptive adversarial "realism" reward that is updated only when the policy starts winning, combining several complementary rewards, hard rule penalties, entropy bonuses, and early stopping monitored on a held-out reward.

### Cited Findings
- [SYMBOLIC, melody-to-chord] GAPT (Wu, Brade, Ma, Fowler, Yang, Banar, Courville, Jaques & Huang; arXiv Nov 2025): RL post-training with coherence rewards alone (ReaLchords) "learns to repeat simple, high-scoring chords". Vendi diversity on the test set fell from 29.491 (MLE-only model) to 20.968 (ReaLchords) while note-in-chord harmony rose from 0.368 to 0.484; on out-of-distribution melodies diversity fell from 16.401 to 8.417. Ground-truth human data: harmony 0.727, diversity 27.922. The authors report that a KL penalty to the pretrained model did not prevent the collapse. — [arXiv 2511.17879](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17879v2)
- GAPT remedy and result: add a Transformer discriminator separating policy chords from dataset chords, with reward R_adv = -log(1 - D(y)); discriminator updated once per five PPO updates for 200 warm-up steps, then only when the three-update moving average of R_adv exceeds 1.0 (otherwise frozen); label smoothing 0.1; entropy bonus 0.01; rule penalties for invalid format, more than 4% silent frames, early end, and the same chord more than four times. Outcome: harmony 0.497 with diversity 26.645 on test (versus 0.484 / 20.968 without), out-of-distribution diversity 11.295 (versus 8.417). With 12 expert musicians, GAPT had the highest mean on all three Likert items; gains over ReaLchords on adaptation speed and control were significant (p < 0.05). The same system without the adversarial term scored harmony 0.476, diversity 20.814. — [arXiv 2511.17879](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17879v2)
- The other rewards in that system are label-free and symbolic: a contrastive (InfoNCE) melody-chord encoder pair scored by cosine similarity, a discriminative reward that classifies real melody-chord pairs against randomly re-paired ones, and rhythm-only variants with pitch stripped to reduce pitch-shift bias. — [arXiv 2511.17879](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17879v2)
- [AUDIO] MusicRL over-optimisation: fine-tuning on the quality reward alone eventually lowered the separate user-preference reward's scores after about 10,000 steps ("prone to reward over-optimisation"); over-optimising the user-preference reward can drastically reduce text adherence; combining rewards (MusicRL-RU) beat either alone head-to-head (66% vs R, 62% vs U). The user-preference reward data were off-policy (collected on a different model version), which the authors list as a limitation. — [arXiv 2402.04229](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.04229)
- Predictor content bias as an attack surface: independently trained aesthetic predictors favour specific content such as "punchy kick" or "synth", and human preference datasets often misalign with them. — [survey, arXiv 2509.00051](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00051v1)
- Predictors under-score the top tier: in the 2026 song-aesthetics challenge top-tier accuracy was at most 0.383 for the winner and 0.124 for a system with the highest LCC (0.706), so high correlation did not imply correct identification of the best items. — [arXiv 2601.07237](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07237v1)
- Rule metrics are satisfiable without quality: scale adherence and syntax accuracy did not track human judgments (r = -0.41 and -0.06, not significant) and outputs could satisfy automatic metrics while being stylistically unrecognisable. — [arXiv 2606.05522](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05522v1)

### Inferences
- GAPT is the most directly transferable published remedy for a symbolic chord/melody engine: (a) keep the realism discriminator as one term among several, never the sole judge; (b) retrain it only when the generator's realism reward rises above a threshold, which stops it going stale without letting it overpower the other terms; (c) track a diversity statistic (Vendi score over the candidate pool) beside the quality score and treat a fall as a failure even if the score rises. The reader's real-versus-generated classifier fits role (a) as a constraint, consistent with its known status as a detectability measure.
- The LCC 0.706 / top-tier accuracy 0.124 pair is a concrete warning against validating a judge by correlation alone when the job is top-1 selection. Validate with a top-k hit rate: how often the judge's top 10 of 1,000 contains items the human would put in their top tier.
- MusicRL's pattern (each reward, pushed alone, damages what the others measure) argues for selecting by several weak, differently built scores with a held-out one used only for monitoring, and for stopping when the held-out score turns down.
- For selection from a fixed pool of 1,000 (no gradient feedback into the generator), the Goodhart risk is milder than in RL fine-tuning: the main danger is the winner's curse, where the top-scoring item is the one that best exploits the judge's errors. Remedies consistent with the evidence are rank aggregation across independent scorers and choosing from the intersection of their top deciles; this is my inference and has no direct published test for music.
