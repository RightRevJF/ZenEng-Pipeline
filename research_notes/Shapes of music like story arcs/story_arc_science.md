## 1. What exactly did Vonnegut claim, and what did the 2016 paper find?

### Takeaway
Vonnegut proposed (in a master's thesis in anthropology that the University of Chicago rejected, later in a well-known lecture) that stories have simple shapes drawable on a graph whose vertical axis is good/ill fortune and horizontal axis is beginning to end, with named shapes such as "Man in Hole", "Boy Meets Girl" and "Cinderella". Reagan et al. 2016 measured sentiment over 1,327 Project Gutenberg books with the hedonometer, found via SVD, Ward clustering and a self-organising map that six arcs dominate (Rags to riches, Tragedy, Man in a hole, Icarus, Cinderella, Oedipus), and reported that Icarus, Oedipus and a Man-in-a-hole-type mode had the highest median downloads, with heavy caveats.

### Cited Findings

Vonnegut (what he proposed)
- Vonnegut wrote a master's thesis in anthropology at the University of Chicago arguing that "stories have shapes which can be drawn on graph paper" and that a society's story shapes are as interesting as its pots or spearheads; he said it was rejected because it was "so simple and looked like too much fun" (quoted from his autobiography Palm Sunday). The year is not given in the source. — [Open Culture, 2026](https://www.openculture.com/2026/06/kurt-vonnegut-diagrams-the-shape-of-all-stories-in-a-masters-thesis-rejected-by-u-chicago.html)
- He called the thesis his "prettiest contribution" to culture (Palm Sunday). — [Big Think, 2022](https://bigthink.com/high-culture/vonnegut-shapes/)
- Axes: Reagan et al. describe Vonnegut's axes as "Beginning–End" and "Ill Fortune–Great Fortune", and note his thesis drew a strong similarity between Cinderella and the Old Testament origin story, which motivated their search for groupings. — [Reagan et al. 2016, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- In Maria Popova's framing of the lecture, the vertical is "the 'G-I axis' of Good Fortune and Ill Fortune" and the horizontal "the 'B-E axis' of Beginning and Entropy" (this "Entropy" gloss is Popova's wording; Reagan et al. say Beginning–End). — [The Marginalian, 2012](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/11/26/kurt-vonnegut-on-the-shapes-of-stories/)
- "Man in Hole": "somebody gets into trouble, gets out of it again"; "the story needn't be about a man or a hole"; "It is not accidental that the line ends up higher than where it began." Source is the lecture, transcribed in A Man Without a Country ("Here is a lesson in creative writing"). — [The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/11/26/kurt-vonnegut-on-the-shapes-of-stories/)
- Kafka's Metamorphosis: a young man who is "not very personable" with "disagreeable relatives" wakes up "and he has turned into a cockroach"; Vonnegut calls it "a pessimistic story" (a line starting low and dropping off the graph). — [The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/11/26/kurt-vonnegut-on-the-shapes-of-stories/)
- Hamlet: Vonnegut says Hamlet's situation "is the same as Cinderella's, except that the sexes are reversed", but the ghost's news is neither good nor bad; "we know so little about life, we don't really know what the good news is." — [The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/11/26/kurt-vonnegut-on-the-shapes-of-stories/); Big Think adds that Vonnegut said the rise-and-fall pattern "pretends that we know more about life than we really do" and that a true masterpiece cannot be fit onto it (2004 Case Western Reserve lecture). — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/high-culture/vonnegut-shapes/)
- "Boy Meets Girl" appears as a diagram on The Marginalian page without textual description in the excerpt I saw; Big Think lists "Boy meets girl" and "Man turns into a cockroach" among the plotted shapes. — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/high-culture/vonnegut-shapes/)

Reagan, Mitchell, Kiley, Danforth & Dodds 2016 (EPJ Data Science 5:31; arXiv 1606.07772)
- Corpus: started from roughly 50,000 Project Gutenberg books, filtered to 1,327 stories ("mostly, but not all, fictional"): English only, 20,000–100,000 words, more than 40 downloads (also tested 10, 20, 80 with qualitatively unchanged results), Library of Congress classes PN, PR, PS, PZ; titles with "poems", "collection", "essays" excluded; front/back matter stripped by regex (worked on 98.9% of books). Appendix C states the threshold as "more than 20 downloads", inconsistent with the main text's 40. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Sentiment: the Hedonometer with the labMT word list; sliding window of 10,000 words, treated as the minimum for meaningful scores; x-axis is percent of book (0–100). The authors state that "dictionary-based methods for sentiment analysis usually perform worse than random on individual sentences" and that brief emotional moments are not captured. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Normalisation: arcs mean-centred before distances and clustering; for sorting by SVD mode, coefficients normalised within each book to sum to 1. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- SVD: first 12 modes explain 80% of variance (mean-centred arcs) and 94% (raw arcs); mode 1 alone about 30%; largest singular value 34.5. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Ward hierarchical clustering with mean absolute difference across windows as distance; silhouette coefficients used; Ward clusters approximately Man in a hole (~30% of stories), Tragedy (~32%), Rags to riches (~5%), Oedipus (~31%). — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Self-organising map: 8×8 grid (64 nodes, about 5% of the number of books); found seven spatial groups representing five arcs. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- The six arcs: Rags to riches (rise); Tragedy / Riches to rags (fall); Man in a hole (fall–rise); Icarus (rise–fall); Cinderella (rise–fall–rise); Oedipus (fall–rise–fall). SVD modes 1–3 and their negations correspond to these. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Null models: "word salad" (shuffled words) and "nonsense" (2-gram Markov text trained on each book). Null texts give a flatter SVD spectrum, much lower Ward linkage cost (about 1,400 vs about 7,000 for real books) and incoherent SOM nodes, so the authors conclude the arcs are not a method artefact. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Downloads: the authors name Icarus (−SV2), Oedipus (−SV3) and a Man-in-a-hole mode (SV4) as the most successful. Median downloads (Fig. 7): SV4 ≈ 105.5, −SV2 ≈ 97, −SV3 ≈ 94, SV3 ≈ 89, SV1 ≈ 80. Variances are huge (SV4 ≈ 1.9 million, −SV3 ≈ 1.3 million), and the authors discount means because a few heavily downloaded books drive them. They call downloads "only a rough proxy for success." — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Stated limitations: emotional arc is not plot; dictionary sentiment unreliable on short passages; crude metadata fiction filter; downloads a weak proxy; higher SVD modes carry little variance. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Online appendices with per-book arcs exist at the UVM Computational Story Lab. — [UVM Story Lab appendices](https://www.uvm.edu/storylab/share/papers/reagan2016b/)

### Inferences
- Vonnegut's central idea (a one-dimensional "fortune" signal over normalised time, with a small vocabulary of shapes) maps directly onto any scalar time series, including a song's tension/energy curve. His Hamlet caveat (great works resist the template) is an early warning that shape conformity and quality may be orthogonal.
- Reagan's "six" is not a single clustering result: Ward gave four dominant clusters, the SOM five arcs, and SVD six by pairing three modes with their sign-flips. The "six" is partly a modelling convention (counting a mode and its negation as two shapes).
- The download differences between modes are modest (medians roughly 80 to 105 downloads) against variances in the millions; no significance test or controls for author, era, genre or canonical status were reported in the part of the paper I read.

### Gaps
- The year of Vonnegut's thesis submission (commonly given as 1947, sometimes 1965 for a later re-submission story) was not stated in any source I saw; unverified.
- Exact verbatim description of "Boy Meets Girl" and "Cinderella" shapes from the lecture not retrieved (video/transcript not fetched).
- Whether SV4 is best described as one Man-in-a-hole or "two sequential Man in a hole" arcs: my fetch reported "Man in a hole (SV4)"; I did not verify the exact wording in the published EPJ version.
- I did not see whether Reagan et al. ran any formal significance test on downloads by arc.

## 2. What are the strongest critiques, and has the six-arc result replicated?

### Takeaway
The strongest critiques are (i) lexicon sentiment is unreliable and unvalidated against human readers, (ii) smoothing choices create shapes (Swafford's Fourier "ringing"; Schmidt's demonstration that Fourier filtering forces endings back toward beginnings), (iii) valence alone is not plot, and (iv) "success" proxies are weak. The six-arc taxonomy has been reproduced in one large film study (Del Vecchio et al., 6 clusters chosen by k-means on 6,174 films), but that study chose k=6 itself, so it is a re-application rather than an independent discovery; no independent test I found validated arcs against human judgement at scale, and a 34-model ensemble study found sentiment models disagree substantially.

### Cited Findings

Jockers' Syuzhet and the Swafford debate (2015)
- Annie Swafford's March 7, 2015 post "continuingsyuzhet" critiqued the Syuzhet package; Jockers replied on March 9 ("Is that your Syuzhet Ringing?") and March 24, 2015 ("A Ringing Endorsement of Smoothing"). — [Jockers, 2015](https://www.matthewjockers.net/?p=1408)
- Jockers conceded "there really *can* be ringing artifacts", especially with a very low low-pass setting; Swafford's extreme example produced "phantom" humps. He defended the goal (simple "shapes", not exact emotional maps), said the default low-pass value of 3 was chosen "by trial and error" and is tunable, called her description of discarding all but the lowest terms "rather misleading", and suggested a higher low-pass value plus a lowess smoother to reduce ringing. — [Jockers, 2015](https://www.matthewjockers.net/?p=1408)
- Swafford showed Syuzhet cannot detect negation or irony and that Fourier smoothing distorts results; Syuzhet does not weight words in context, e.g. "He had a good hearth and a good mind" scores the same (+1.35) as "He had a good hearth" (example as reported by Rebora). — [Rebora 2023](https://iris.univr.it/bitstream/11562/1115884/2/Rebora%20-%202023%20-%20Sentiment%20Analysis%20in%20Literary%20Studies.%20A%20Critical.pdf)
- Ben Schmidt: because Fourier transforms assume periodicity, the filtered plot's start is forced to align with its end; an artificial plot rising from −5 to +5 looks wavy under Syuzhet's filter but not under loess or a rolling average; on State of the Union addresses since 1960 the Fourier curves were pulled back toward their starting values. He argues this rules out arcs like Vonnegut's "From bad to worse" and recommends loess or moving averages. — [Schmidt, 2015](https://benschmidt.org/2015/04/03/commodius-vici-of-recirculation-the-real-problem-with-syuzhet/)
- Syuzhet later offered a Discrete Cosine Transform option (Del Vecchio et al. used syuzhet with DCT, sub-sampled to 100 points), which avoids the forced start=end periodicity of the plain DFT. — [Del Vecchio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)

Broader critiques
- Rebora's 2023 critical review says most sentiment tools "still lack a validation"; describes Syuzhet as among the least advanced tools; says the basic-shapes conclusion of Jockers and Reagan "was based on a very problematic assumption" that tracking positive vs negative emotion reveals plot; cites Hammond (2017) as a critic of Reagan et al.; notes that valence-only approaches lose distinctions between emotions. — [Rebora 2023](https://iris.univr.it/bitstream/11562/1115884/2/Rebora%20-%202023%20-%20Sentiment%20Analysis%20in%20Literary%20Studies.%20A%20Critical.pdf)
- Human agreement is itself low: Sprugnoli et al. (2016) found low inter-annotator agreement on sentiment in historical speeches; Rebora (2020) found annotator agreement stayed low though valence still correlated significantly with reader responses (coefficients not reported in the review). — [Rebora 2023](https://iris.univr.it/bitstream/11562/1115884/2/Rebora%20-%202023%20-%20Sentiment%20Analysis%20in%20Literary%20Studies.%20A%20Critical.pdf)
- Elkins, The Shapes of Stories: Sentiment Analysis for Narrative (Cambridge Elements, 2022) compares more than 36 computational methods for building arcs (per Rebora; numbers not given). — [Rebora 2023](https://iris.univr.it/bitstream/11562/1115884/2/Rebora%20-%202023%20-%20Sentiment%20Analysis%20in%20Literary%20Studies.%20A%20Critical.pdf); [Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/elements/shapes-of-stories/3047F01956467FA981DB9F14A18A6725)
- Chun 2021 (SentimentArcs, Kenyon College): ensemble of 34 sentiment models over 25 narratives (840 corpus:model combinations), ensemble median used as synthetic ground truth; existing approaches "produce unpredictable results", sometimes "wildly inaccurate" vs expert judgement; eight Transformer models "exhibited consistent disagreement between each other"; smoothing was z-score standardisation then a 10% simple moving average (window chosen because 5% or 10% are most common in the literature). Peaks and valleys were identified by a human expert. The paper does not test the six-shape claim. — [Chun 2021, arXiv 2110.09454](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2110.09454)

Replication / re-application
- Del Vecchio, Kharlamov, Parry & Pogrebna (2018 arXiv; published as "Improving productivity in Hollywood with data science", Journal of the Operational Research Society, 2020): 6,174 films from OpenSubtitles; syuzhet sentiment (Nebraska Literary Lab lexicon), DCT, 100 points; k-means on functional data (L2) testing k = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, choosing 6. Clusters: Rags to Riches 632, Riches to Rags 1,402, Man in a Hole 1,598, Icarus 1,113, Cinderella 804, Oedipus 625. — [Del Vecchio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221); [JORS 2020 version](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/files/95602489/Del_Vecchio_et_al_2020_Improving_productivity_in_Hollywood_Journal_of_the_Operational_Research_Society.pdf)
- Reagan's own null models (shuffled text, Markov text) argue the structure is not purely a method artefact. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Boyd et al. 2020, using function-word dimensions rather than sentiment, found a consistent shared narrative shape across ~40,000 traditional narratives (see Q3), which is supportive of the existence of regular arcs but is a different signal and does not test the six. — [Boyd et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)

### Inferences
- The core technical critique for any transfer to music is about the smoothing operator: a DFT low-pass imposes periodicity (start forced toward end) and ringing (spurious humps), so it can manufacture "Man in a hole" or "Cinderella" shapes. Use loess, moving average, Savitzky–Golay, or DCT, and test with synthetic monotone signals.
- The "six" is fragile to method choices (Ward gave four, SOM five, k-means set to six). Treat the number of clusters as a modelling decision to be justified (silhouette, gap statistic, stability), not a discovery.
- The field's validation gap (no large human-annotated ground truth for arcs) is the analogue of needing listener tension ratings for music.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Swafford's posts directly; her exact examples and numbers are known only through Jockers, Schmidt and Rebora.
- Hammond (2017) critique not retrieved; content unknown.
- No independent study I found re-derived the six arcs from scratch on a new corpus with unsupervised k selection and reported agreement with Reagan's shapes. Elkins (2022) may contain relevant comparisons but I only saw a secondary description.
- No information found on post-2023 replications (e.g. LLM-based sentiment arcs) as of Nov 2026; not searched exhaustively.

## 3. How strong is the evidence that shape predicts success, and does shape plus quality beat shape alone?

### Takeaway
Evidence that arc shape predicts success is weak to modest and confounded. Reagan's download differences are small relative to variance with no controls; Del Vecchio's "Man in a Hole earns ~$6.5M more" is a cluster-dummy regression without budget/genre controls, shrinking to borderline significance (p = 0.055 domestic, 0.088 worldwide) once budget is included, and Man in a Hole films are rated slightly lower, i.e. "talked about" not "liked". Boyd et al. found no link between narrative-arc shape and ratings for movies or romance novels. The best-controlled successor (Toubia et al. 2021, PNAS) finds small but significant effects of semantic "speed" on IMDb ratings after controlling for topics, length, genre and year (about 0.05 rating points per SD). The Bestseller Code's 80% accuracy mixes theme, style and plot features, and its plot-only contribution is not published.

### Cited Findings

Reagan et al. 2016 (books, downloads)
- Median downloads by mode ranged about 80 (SV1) to 105.5 (SV4), with variances in the millions; authors call downloads "only a rough proxy for success" and say results are influenced by individual popular books. — [Reagan et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)

Del Vecchio et al. 2018/2020 (films, box office)
- Man in a Hole has the highest mean domestic gross, $37.48M (median $13.45M) vs overall mean $32.61M; next Cinderella ($33.63M) and Oedipus ($31.44M). — [Del Vecchio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)
- Regression with cluster dummies only (no budget or genre controls): Man in a Hole coefficient 6.5613 ($M), p < 0.001; Riches to Rags −3.4599, p < 0.05; other arcs not significant. — [Del Vecchio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)
- Budget subsample (3,051 films): domestic coefficient 5.217 (SE 2.713, p = 0.055); worldwide 12.02 (SE 7.04, p = 0.088). Budget data are estimates the source itself calls "difficult to find and unreliable." — [Del Vecchio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)
- Man in a Hole's mean IMDb user rating is 6.45 vs 6.52 overall (coefficient −0.0910, p < 0.001); metascore coefficient also negative (p < 0.05); but rating count, user reviews and critic reviews are positively associated. User rating is negatively associated with domestic revenue in their Table 3 (−7.8031, p < 0.01). The authors conclude the arc produces "talked about" rather than "liked" films ("Man in a Hole succeeds not because it produces most 'liked' movies"). — [Del Vecchio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)
- Man in a Hole outperformance is significant within several genres (SciFi, Mystery, Thriller, Animation, Adventure, Fantasy, Comedy, Family) and not explained by budget band. Associations only; no causal claim. — [Del Vecchio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)
- R² for the arc-only model was not reported in what I read.

Boyd, Blackburn & Pennebaker 2020 (Science Advances 6:eaba2196, "The narrative arc: Revealing core narrative structures through text analysis")
- Corpora: 2,523 Gutenberg novels; 2,092 short stories; 14,419 TAT stories; 18,074 movie transcripts (with ≥100 IMDb ratings) of 19,970; 639 Smashwords romance novels; NYT science articles (18,312 in Methods vs 28,664 in Results, an internal inconsistency); 2,226 TED talks; 1,580 Supreme Court opinions. Abstract: ~40,000 traditional and ~20,000 nontraditional narratives. — [Boyd et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)
- Three LIWC-based dimensions: staging (articles, prepositions; 75 words), plot progression (pronouns, auxiliary verbs, negations, conjunctions, nonreferential adverbs; 448 words), cognitive tension (LIWC2015 cognitive process words, 393 words, e.g. "think", "believe", "understand", "cause"). Each text cut into five equal segments; scores standardised within each text; results held for 3 to 10 segments. — [Boyd et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)
- Prototypical shape: staging highest at the start then drops; plot progression low early, rising, levelling off; cognitive tension an inverted U in fiction, peaking midway to late. — [Boyd et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)
- Effect sizes (partial η²) for traditional stories are "modest": segment main effect 0.031; process × segment 0.085; staging linear 0.028; plot progression linear 0.069; cognitive tension quadratic 0.018. Patterns are "unlikely to be visible to the human eye." — [Boyd et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)
- Success: movies grouped by IMDb rating (≤5.0: 2,546; 5.1–7.0: 10,204; >7.0: 5,324): "No meaningful main effect or interaction emerged with rating group." Romance novels grouped by average rating (149 / 348 / 142): no meaningful interactions. Competing interest: Pennebaker owns LIWC2015 (profits to UT Austin). — [Boyd et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)

Toubia, Berger & Eliashberg 2021 (PNAS 118: e2011695118, "How quantifying the shape of stories predicts their success")
- Data: 4,118 movies (subtitles; outcome IMDb rating), ~12,400 TV episodes (closed captions; IMDb rating), ~29,300 academic papers from 22 journals 1990–2019 (outcome log(1+citations)). — [Toubia et al. 2021, author PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/How-Quantifying-the-Shapes-of-Stories-Predicts-their-Success.pdf)
- Method: text cut into ~250-word windows (sentence boundaries kept); each window = mean 300-d word2vec vector, giving a path through semantic space. Speed = mean Euclidean distance between consecutive windows; volume = size of minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid (geometric mean of axis lengths, normalised by dimensionality); circuitousness = actual path length / shortest path through the same points (modified travelling-salesman). All logged. — [Toubia et al. 2021](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/How-Quantifying-the-Shapes-of-Stories-Predicts-their-Success.pdf)
- Controls: year and genre fixed effects; duration (movies), channel FE (TV), journal FE and pages (papers); log words, log sentences; 100 LDA topic intensities. Lasso with bootstrapped CIs; overall R² 0.306 (movies), 0.326 (TV), 0.364 (papers), which includes all controls. — [Toubia et al. 2021](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/How-Quantifying-the-Shapes-of-Stories-Predicts-their-Success.pdf)
- Standardised effects: speed +0.048 (movies), +0.072 (TV), −0.125 (papers); volume 0 (movies), −0.082 (TV), +0.095 (papers); circuitousness 0 (movies), 0.006 n.s. (TV), +0.070 (papers). For movies/TV a 1-SD speed increase = +0.048 / +0.072 rating points on scales with SD 1.01 / 1.08; for papers 1-SD speed ≈ −12% citations, volume +10%, circuitousness +7%. For TV, effects are driven by changes toward the end. — [Toubia et al. 2021](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/How-Quantifying-the-Shapes-of-Stories-Predicts-their-Success.pdf)
- The paper positions its semantic-path measures as complementary to emotional trajectories and does not directly compare with Reagan et al. — [Toubia et al. 2021](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/How-Quantifying-the-Shapes-of-Stories-Predicts-their-Success.pdf)

Archer & Jockers, The Bestseller Code (2016)
- University of Nebraska press release: about 4,500 recent English novels (the book and reviews cite ~20,000 used in development); ~28,000 features extracted per novel, ~2,800 selected; 80% correct classification of bestsellers vs non-bestsellers in "class balanced cross validation experiments"; features include theme/topic, plot structure and pacing, word choice and sentence construction. Classifier type not named. — [UNL Research, 2016](https://research.unl.edu/blog/supercomputer-helps-crack-the-code-for-bestselling-books/)
- New Republic review: reports the "over 20,000 contemporary novels", 2,800 features and 80% claim, plus findings such as bestsellers having "three-part plot shapes"; critiques that untold flops share much of the same "genome", and gives base rates (over 50,000 new fiction works/year; ~200 reach the NYT list, under 0.5%; big five publishers own ~80% of bestsellers). — [New Republic, 2016](https://www.newrepublic.com/article/137280/whats-purpose-bestseller)

### Inferences
- "Success" is measured inconsistently across the literature: Gutenberg downloads (Reagan), domestic box-office gross (Del Vecchio), IMDb ratings (Boyd, Toubia, Del Vecchio), self-published reader ratings (Boyd), citations (Toubia), bestseller-list status (Archer & Jockers). These measure different things (reach vs liking), and Del Vecchio shows they can move in opposite directions for the same arc.
- When controls for topic, length, era and genre are included (Toubia), shape-type features have small effects: about 0.05 SD of rating per SD of the feature. When controls are absent (Reagan, Del Vecchio main table), effects look larger but are confounded with budget, marketing, author fame, canon status and genre.
- Archer & Jockers' 80% is on a class-balanced sample; given a real base rate under 1%, precision in deployment would be far lower. Plot shape's unique contribution relative to topic and style was not separately reported in any source I saw, so "shape plus writing quality beats shape alone" is plausible but not demonstrated with published effect sizes.
- For music: expect at best small, confounded associations between curve shape and popularity, and possible dissociation between "engagement/streams" and "liking/quality ratings".

### Gaps
- No published, peer-reviewed estimate of shape's incremental predictive value over writing quality/style for books was found; The Bestseller Code is a trade book and its model was not peer-reviewed in what I saw.
- Del Vecchio did not report R² or out-of-sample prediction for arc dummies in what I read; no marketing or star-power controls.
- I found no follow-up that tested Reagan's download result with controls.

## 4. What is the step-by-step method that could be applied to a different time series (e.g. a song's tension or energy curve)?

### Takeaway
The pipeline common to Reagan, Jockers, Del Vecchio, Boyd and Toubia is: define a scalar (or vector) signal, compute it in windows over the work, resample to relative time, smooth with a non-periodic smoother, standardise, reduce dimensionality (SVD/PCA), cluster (Ward, k-means on functional data, SOM) with explicit k selection, validate against null models and human judgement, then link cluster membership or continuous shape features to an outcome with controls. Each step has a documented pitfall.

### Cited Findings (method components as used in the sources)
- Signal: valence from a lexicon (labMT hedonometer, Reagan; Syuzhet/NRC/Nebraska lexicons, Jockers, Del Vecchio); function-word and cognitive-word rates (Boyd); semantic embedding paths (Toubia). — [Reagan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772); [Del Vecchio](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221); [Boyd](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736); [Toubia](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/How-Quantifying-the-Shapes-of-Stories-Predicts-their-Success.pdf)
- Windowing: 10,000-word sliding window (Reagan); ~250-word windows (Toubia); sentence-level scores (Del Vecchio/Syuzhet); five equal segments, robust to 3–10 (Boyd). — same sources as above
- Relative-time normalisation: percent of book 0–100 (Reagan); sub-sample to 100 points (Del Vecchio). — [Reagan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772); [Del Vecchio](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)
- Smoothing: Fourier low-pass (Syuzhet default 3 components; ringing/periodicity artefacts), DCT (Del Vecchio), loess/moving average recommended by Schmidt and Jockers' own later suggestion, 10% SMA after z-scoring (Chun). — [Jockers](https://www.matthewjockers.net/?p=1408); [Schmidt](https://benschmidt.org/2015/04/03/commodius-vici-of-recirculation-the-real-problem-with-syuzhet/); [Chun](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2110.09454)
- Standardisation: mean-centring (Reagan), within-text z-scoring (Boyd, Chun). — [Reagan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772); [Boyd](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413736)
- Dimensionality reduction: SVD of the works-by-time matrix (12 modes = 80% variance). — [Reagan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Clustering: Ward hierarchical with silhouette; 8×8 SOM; k-means on functional data with L2 distance, k compared over 4–12. — [Reagan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772); [Del Vecchio](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02221)
- Null models: shuffled-token and Markov-generated surrogates. — [Reagan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07772)
- Validation against people: absent at scale; expert reading of peaks/valleys (Chun, Jockers); low inter-annotator agreement noted. — [Chun](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2110.09454); [Rebora](https://iris.univr.it/bitstream/11562/1115884/2/Rebora%20-%202023%20-%20Sentiment%20Analysis%20in%20Literary%20Studies.%20A%20Critical.pdf)
- Outcome linking: compare medians by cluster (Reagan); regress outcome on cluster dummies, then add budget and genre (Del Vecchio); continuous shape features with fixed effects, topic controls, lasso and bootstrap CIs (Toubia); ANOVA of rating group × segment (Boyd). — sources as above

### Inferences (transferable recipe for a song tension/energy curve; my synthesis, not from a source)
1. Signal: choose a defensible scalar per time step (e.g. a tension model, loudness/energy, harmonic tension, or listener continuous ratings). Validate it on a subset against human continuous-response ratings, since the story-arc field's biggest weakness was unvalidated sentiment.
2. Window: pick a window large enough to be stable (Reagan's 10,000 words was the minimum for reliable valence), e.g. a bar or phrase rather than a beat; report sensitivity to window size.
3. Relative time: resample each song to a fixed N points (e.g. 100) over 0–100% duration; consider also aligning by structural landmarks (verse/chorus boundaries), since songs, unlike novels, have strong sectional form that linear time-warping may blur.
4. Smooth: use loess, Savitzky–Golay, moving average or DCT; avoid plain DFT low-pass (forces end toward start, creates ringing humps). Test the smoother on synthetic monotone ramps and step functions to confirm it does not invent shapes.
5. Standardise: z-score within song if the question is shape; keep raw level as a separate feature, since shape and level are confounded.
6. Reduce: SVD/PCA on the songs-by-time matrix; report variance explained by each mode; treat a mode and its sign-flip as possibly distinct shapes, but say so.
7. Cluster: Ward, k-means on functional data, or SOM; select k by silhouette/gap statistic and bootstrap stability, and compare methods (Reagan's methods disagreed on 4/5/6).
8. Null models: shuffle time order within songs (equivalent of word salad) and generate surrogates preserving autocorrelation (phase-randomised or Markov), and show real clusters beat them.
9. Outcome: define success explicitly (streams, chart peak/duration, listener ratings, critic scores) and expect reach and liking to diverge (as in Del Vecchio). Control for artist popularity, label, release year, genre, duration, and marketing proxies; use continuous shape features (as Toubia did) rather than only cluster dummies; estimate out-of-sample performance on class-imbalanced data with realistic base rates.
10. Report effect sizes in outcome units and as standardised effects, not just p-values; expect small effects (Toubia ~0.05 SD; Boyd found none for ratings).

Pitfalls list (from the critiques): measurement error in the signal; smoothing artefacts; arbitrary k; corpus selection bias (Reagan's download threshold, Gutenberg canon); success-proxy mismatch; confounding by budget/fame/genre; class-balanced accuracy overstating real-world prediction; patterns too subtle to be perceptible (Boyd); great works deliberately defying templates (Vonnegut's Hamlet point).

### Gaps
- Papers that explicitly apply story-arc (Vonnegut/Reagan-style) methods to music or song lyrics: none found in my searches. Searches for lyric emotional arcs returned only studies of long-run trends in lyric sentiment across decades (e.g. [Scientific Reports 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-55742-x)), not within-song arcs; I did not read these in detail and they are out of scope.
- I did not verify whether Reagan et al. tested sensitivity to the 10,000-word window or to the number of resampled points (N not stated in the portion I read).
