## Q1. Is there an established music analogue of Vonnegut's story shapes (a small set of whole-piece shapes found by data)?

### Takeaway
No. There is no established, data-derived small set of whole-piece musical shapes comparable to Reagan et al.'s six story arcs. The closest things are (a) lyric-sentiment arcs of songs clustered into Reagan's six story shapes (Rösch & Rauch 2025), which measure the words, not the music; (b) phrase-level contour typologies (Huron's nine types, cosine contours), which a 2026 study argues do not form real clusters at all; and (c) audio studies of pop songs showing a recurring "local cycles, global accumulation" pattern, with the arousal peak about three-quarters of the way through, but without clustering into shape types. Theoretical typologies exist (Almén's four Frye-derived narrative archetypes), but none has been quantified or tested on corpora.

### Cited Findings

**Reference point: the story-shape result being sought**
- Reagan et al. (EPJ Data Science 2016, 5:31), "The emotional arcs of stories are dominated by six basic shapes" — [Reagan et al. PDF](http://www.uvm.edu/~cdanfort/research/2016-reagan-epj.pdf); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/304469919_The_emotional_arcs_of_stories_are_dominated_by_six_basic_shapes) (title only fetched via search; the six shapes are named in Rösch & Rauch below)

**(a) Lyric sentiment arcs of songs, clustered into story shapes**
- Rösch & Rauch (2025), "'Do songs tell stories?' An empirical analysis of the effect of emotional arcs on success in a national song contest," *Journal of Cultural Economics* 49, 603–637: line-level lyric sentiment via the R package `syuzhet`, smoothed with a discrete cosine transform, normalized to 0–100% of song time; k-means gave six clusters matched to Reagan et al. (2016) / Del Vecchio et al. (2021) archetypes: Oedipus, Man in Hole, Tragedy/Riches to Rags, Rags to Riches, Cinderella, Icarus — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10824-025-09532-6)
- Data: 176 Bundesvision Song Contest songs (2005–2015) plus a reference corpus of US weekly Top-50 chart lyrics 1977–2022 (84,098 songs before cleaning, 6,587 after; the abstract says 6,763, which conflicts with the body text) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10824-025-09532-6)
- Shape frequencies: Tragedy/Riches-to-Rags was most common in contest songs (~23%); Icarus rarest (8.52% contest vs 13.62% chart songs). Spotify audio features (energy, valence, etc.) were used only as controls, not to define arcs — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10824-025-09532-6)
- Kleinberg & McFarlane (arXiv 1911.01324, ~2019), UK drill lyrics: sentiment trajectories standardized to 100 narrative-time bins with a DCT, k-means with k = 2 (chosen by elbow/silhouette): mirror-image "positive" (53%, n = 289) and "negative" (47%, n = 261) clusters with the standard lexicon; with a slang lexicon, two negative clusters differing in intensity; 550 songs, 105 artists — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01324). This is the only other lyric-arc clustering found, and it found only two shapes.

**(b) Melodic contour typologies (phrase scale, not whole piece)**
- Huron (1996) typology: compares average pitch of three segments, giving nine types; four are frequent (ascending, descending, convex, concave), the other five each "≤1.2%" of data — as reported by [Cornelissen, Zuidema, Burgoyne & Honing, arXiv 2604.13119 (April 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13119v1)
- Other typologies reviewed by the same paper: Densmore (1918, five classes for Lakota songs), Adams (1976, deductive, ordering of four boundary pitches, 15 orderings, "attracted few followers"), Lomax's Cantometrics, CantoCore (Savage et al. 2012), Goldstein et al. (2025, four k-means shapes) — [arXiv 2604.13119](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13119v1)
- **"Melodic contour does not cluster" (Cornelissen et al., arXiv preprint, 13 April 2026):** nine datasets (Essen German folksongs: Erk 1,700 songs and Böhme 704; 152 Nova Scotia songs; Chinese Han, Shanxi, Natmin subsets; Gregorian chant from GregoBase); contours resampled to 50 pitches, eight representations including cosine contours, Euclidean/DTW/UMAP distances, and a "dist-dip" test (Hartigan's dip test on pairwise distances). No representation, metric or dataset showed clustering in real phrases; the test did detect clusters in a synthetic clustered control (with UMAP) — [arXiv 2604.13119](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13119v1)
- Same paper: Huron type frequencies depend on the tolerance ε (earlier studies used 0.2 semitones; entropy-maximizing value 1.4 semitones); Goldstein et al.'s four shapes are attributed to k-means/PCA artefacts, because PCA components approximate cosines, so k = 4 lands on ascending, descending, convex, concave. On average, German and chant phrases tend toward arches; Chinese phrases are descending or horizontal-descending, not arched. Data and code: github.com/bacor/shapes-of-music — [arXiv 2604.13119](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13119v1)
- **Cosine contours (Cornelissen, Zuidema & Burgoyne, ISMIR 2021):** step contour sampled at 100 points, DCT-II, constant term dropped for transposition invariance; first coefficient c1 = "descendingness," −c2 = "archedness"; DCT closely approximates PCA; 95% of variance needs 1 dimension for motifs, 9 for phrases, 61 for whole songs. German (Erk, 9,782 contours) and Chinese (Han, 7,601) phrases were significantly more descending and arched than random segments from the same melodies (p < 0.001). Code: github.com/bacor/cosine-contours — [ISMIR 2021 paper](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000016.pdf)

**(c) Audio-based section arcs in pop songs (no shape clustering)**
- Lyu (arXiv 2609.15675, 2026, Communication University of China): 100 Chinese pop songs (QQ Music top-60 artists), 1,046 manually annotated sections; valence/arousal estimated every 0.5 s by frozen audio models (MERT + openSMILE features, trained on PMEmo). These are model estimates, not listener ratings — [arXiv 2609.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15675)
- Section means: verse lowest arousal (A −0.088), pre-chorus 0.107, chorus 0.215, bridge 0.270 (highest), outro 0.104. Chorus exceeded verse in arousal by +0.279 in 99% of songs; chorus exceeded pre-chorus by +0.200 in all 38 songs with a pre-chorus — [arXiv 2609.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15675)
- Repetition escalates: from first to last occurrence, arousal rose for verse (+0.165), pre-chorus (+0.156), chorus (+0.225), interlude (+0.260) — [arXiv 2609.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15675)
- Peak placement: average valence peaks at normalized position 0.75, arousal at 0.78; the highest-arousal section sits at mean 0.733 (median 0.755) and is in the final third in 79/100 songs; it is a chorus in 53 songs but the *final* chorus in only 31. The largest adjacent jump is entering the outro (44 songs, mostly drops) or entering a chorus (27) — [arXiv 2609.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15675)
- Whole-song paths were far from monotonic (directness 0.242); the author summarizes the pattern as "local cycles–global accumulation." No trajectory clustering was performed — [arXiv 2609.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15675)

**(d) Musical narrative theory (theoretical typologies, unquantified)**
- Almén (*A Theory of Musical Narrative*, Indiana University Press; publication year 2008 from background, not verified this session) defines musical narrative as tracking the "transvaluation of hierarchical relationships within a temporal span," i.e., the shifting balance of order (unmarked, expected material) and transgression (marked, unexpected); it draws on Frye's four archetypes via James Liszka — as summarized in [Wu, UGA DMA dissertation 2018](https://openscholar.uga.edu/record/6775/files/wu_crystal_w_201805_dma.pdf); publisher description at [PhilArchive record](https://dc2.philarchive.org/rec/ALMATO-2)
- The four archetypes cross order/transgression with victory/defeat: **Romance** = victory of order over transgression; **Tragedy** = defeat of a transgression by order (sympathy with the transgression); **Comedy** = victory of transgression over order; **Irony** = defeat of order by transgression (often a parody of romance). The names do not imply genre or fixed emotion (a comic narrative need not be humorous) — [Wu 2018](https://openscholar.uga.edu/record/6775/files/wu_crystal_w_201805_dma.pdf) (secondary summary of Almén)
- Hatten's markedness (which Almén builds on): marked entities have "greater specificity of meaning," narrower distribution and lower frequency than unmarked opposites; e.g., minor mode marked vs major unmarked, correlating with tragic vs non-tragic meaning — [Wu 2018](https://openscholar.uga.edu/record/6775/files/wu_crystal_w_201805_dma.pdf)
- Fred Everett Maus, "Music as Drama," is a canonical narrative/dramatic analysis (Beethoven Hammerklavier, Op. 95) — cited in [Wu 2018](https://openscholar.uga.edu/record/6775/files/wu_crystal_w_201805_dma.pdf)
- **Empirical narrative work (Margulis lab), the closest thing to quantification:** listeners' imagined story *events* cluster at specific moments rather than being spread evenly, event timing correlates with moments of salient change in continuous tension ratings, and the alignment is stronger for highly "narrative" excerpts (16 instrumental excerpts) — [Margulis, Williams, Simchy-Gross & McAuley 2022, *Cognition* 226:105180](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/when-did-that-happen-the-dynamic-unfolding-of-perceived-musical-n/)
- Narrative engagement is driven mainly by "Topicality" (culturally learned extramusical associations), not by "Contrast": lowering topicality reduced narrative engagement, lowering contrast did not; tested with US and rural Chinese listeners — [McAuley, Wong, Bellaiche & Margulis 2021, *Music Perception* 38(5):509–521](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/what-drives-narrative-engagement-with-music/)
- Related titles (not fetched): "Do you hear what I hear? Perceived narrative constitutes a semantic dimension for music" (*Cognition*, 2021) and "An exploratory study of narrative experiences of music" — [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/do-you-hear-what-i-hear-perceived-narrative-constitutes-a-semanti/), [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/an-exploratory-study-of-narrative-experiences-of-music/)

**(e) Golden-section form proportions (claimed shape regularity)**
- Putz (reported in *American Scientist* 84:118, 1996): Mozart sonata movements split into exposition vs development+recapitulation near the golden ratio, e.g., Sonata No. 1 in C, movement 1: 38 + 62 bars (0.613 vs 0.618); movement 2 also fits, movement 3 does not, and several sonatas deviate considerably; no evidence of conscious use — [Science Frontiers 107 reprint](https://science-frontiers.com/sf107/sf107p14.htm) (secondary)

### Inferences
- The honest answer for a report is "no Vonnegut-equivalent for music yet." The most promising candidate method already exists: Cornelissen's cosine-contour/DCT representation is exactly the Reagan/Rösch pipeline (resample to fixed length, DCT, cluster) applied to pitch, and the 2026 result that phrases do not cluster is a warning that k-means on DCT/PCA coefficients will *always* return ascending/descending/arch/U-shaped "types" even when the data form a continuum. Any whole-song shape typology (tension, loudness, register) should use a clusterability test (dip test, synthetic controls) before claiming discrete shapes.
- What the data do support is a continuous tendency rather than types: phrases lean toward arch/descending (Western folk, chant), and pop songs show local section cycles (verse dip, chorus lift) that accumulate toward a late peak at roughly 0.73–0.78 of duration. That late-peak figure sits between the golden section (0.618) and the end, which is relevant to any "climax placement" target, but it comes from one 100-song Chinese-pop audio-model study.
- Almén's archetypes are defined over *marked vs unmarked material and its fate*, not over a curve of tension, so they are orthogonal to tension/energy shape. They could be operationalized (e.g., marked = high IDyOM surprisal / minor / chromatic material; victory = whether it is absorbed or prevails at the end), but nobody appears to have done it.
- Lyric arcs and musical arcs are different objects. Rösch & Rauch's six shapes describe words; a songwriting engine wanting a "story shape" for a song could legitimately borrow them for the lyric layer and pair them with a separate musical energy/tension plan.

### Gaps
- No study was found that clusters whole-piece *musical* tension, loudness, register or emotion curves into a small set of shapes with a clusterability test. A paper titled "Continuous Affect Responses to a Large Diverse Set of Unfamiliar Music: Bayesian Time-Series and Cluster Analyses" exists ([search result](https://aminer.org/pub/647f18c0d68f896efa1b03cd/continuous-affect-responses-to-a-large-diverse-set-of-unfamiliar-music-bayesian); PMC copy blocked by captcha) and may cluster continuous affect responses; its content, authors and findings are unverified and it should be checked first.
- Huron's original 1996 Essen numbers (background: ~6,000+ Essen melodies, convex/arch the most common phrase type at roughly 40%, with whole-melody arch tendencies) were not verified; only the secondary summary above was read. *The Melodic Arch in Western Folksongs* (Computing in Musicology 10, 1996) should be checked.
- Contour typologies of Kolinski, Marvin & Laprade (contour-space / COM matrices, 1987), and Schmuckler's contour similarity (Fourier/time-series contour models, 1999 onward) were not covered by any fetched source this session (background only).
- Newcomb ("Once more 'between absolute and program music': Schumann's Second Symphony," 1984; "Action and agency in Mahler's Ninth," 1992; plot archetypes), Hatten's *Musical Meaning in Beethoven* (1994) and Meyer's *Emotion and Meaning in Music* (1956) / *Explaining Music* (1973) were not read directly this session. No computational quantification of any of these was found.
- Almén's book itself, and a scholarly review (Project MUSE review blocked), were not read; archetype definitions come from a 2018 DMA dissertation's summary.
- Spotify's former "Audio Analysis" endpoint (per-segment loudness curves) was used in many informal "song shape" analyses; no peer-reviewed clustering of those curves was found.

## Q2. Which tension or energy measures can be computed from symbolic scores, and which have open code?

### Takeaway
Three families are computable from notes/chords with open code: (1) Herremans & Chew's spiral-array "tension ribbons" (cloud diameter, cloud momentum, tensile strain), implemented in `midi-miner` (MIDI) and in `partitura` (`estimate_tonaltension`); (2) Lerdahl & Krumhansl's tonal tension model, automated by AuToTen but requiring GTTM prolongational trees as input; (3) IDyOM surprisal (covered in the earlier note), which a 2026 study links to continuous tension ratings. Farbood's 2012 model is mostly symbolic (harmony via Lerdahl, pitch height, onset frequency, tempo) except loudness, which comes from audio, and it has no released code that I found; TenseMusic (2024) is its open, fully automatic but audio-only successor. Newer generative systems (MorpheuS, tension-conditioned VAE, 2025 dual-level beam search) steer symbolic generation toward a target tension curve.

### Cited Findings

**Lerdahl & Krumhansl (2007) tonal tension model**
- Components: sequential tension (between consecutive chords: Tonal Pitch Space distance plus surface factors such as scale degree of the top voice, inversion, and each note's role in the chord's basic space); global/hierarchical tension (same computation plus tension "inherited" through the GTTM prolongational tree); and attraction (voice-leading pull computed from basic spaces and inter-voice intervals) — [Ruiz-Marcos, Willis & Laney 2020, "Automatically calculating tonal tension," AIMC 2020](https://core.ac.uk/download/337616552.pdf)
- Validation: the model was tested against listener tension judgements by multiple regression on global tension and attraction; Ruiz-Marcos et al. report an average adjusted R² of .72 for their re-implementation against listener judgements, "comparable with the original study"; AuToTen's outputs vs the original hand calculations: global tension R² = .61–.80, attraction R² = .71–.92 — [Ruiz-Marcos et al. 2020](https://core.ac.uk/download/337616552.pdf)
- **Code:** AuToTen (Automatic Tonal Tension) is public at https://doi.org/10.21954/ou.rd.13026578; input is MusicXML plus GTTM analyses (generated by the authors with the external Interactive GTTM Analyser); output is global tension and attraction per event — [Ruiz-Marcos et al. 2020](https://core.ac.uk/download/337616552.pdf)
- A copy of the 2007 paper is indexed as "Modeling Tonal Tension" — [Scribd listing](https://www.scribd.com/document/392854820/ModelingTonalTension-pdf) (not read)

**Farbood (2012) parametric temporal model**
- Citation: M. M. Farbood, "A Parametric, Temporal Model of Musical Tension," *Music Perception* 29(4), 387–428, DOI 10.1525/mp.2012.29.4.387 — [Farbood 2012 PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_2012_Musical_Tension.pdf)
- Features and sources: harmony = Lerdahl's tonal tension (symbolic; interval-class dissonance ranking for atonal Schoenberg); melodic expectation = Margulis (2005) model (symbolic), but dropped from the final model because it "did not correlate in a clear directional way with tension"; loudness = Jehan's psychoacoustic model on rendered audio (dB); pitch height of melody, bass and inner voices, onset frequency and tempo = symbolic step functions. Meter, rhythmic regularity and syncopation were tested and had no effect — [Farbood 2012](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_2012_Musical_Tension.pdf)
- Combination: slope of each feature over an attentional window (best 3 s), weighted (dynamics and melodic pitch height weight 3; tempo and onset frequency 2; harmony, bass and inner pitch height 1; normalized to sum 1); a memory window (3 s) multiplies the trend by constant b (b = 1 if the memory trend and current trend differ in sign, "ideal" b = 5 if they agree); evaluated every 0.25 s, smoothed with a decaying moving average, then integrated over time — [Farbood 2012](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_2012_Musical_Tension.pdf)
- Data: Experiment 1 web study, N = 2,661 from 108 countries, 207 short stimuli, choice among nine tension-curve shapes; Experiment 2 lab study, 33 usable subjects, slider at 60 Hz, 10 stimuli including Beethoven Symphony 1, Brahms Piano Concerto 2, Schoenberg Op. 11 — [Farbood 2012](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_2012_Musical_Tension.pdf)
- Fit (Pearson r, model vs mean ratings): .67 (Beethoven) to .99; Bach–Vivaldi .88, Schoenberg .93, Brahms .95. Linear and quadratic regression models generalized poorly (overfitting). Inter-subject agreement was modest (mean r = .47). In Exp. 1, dynamics and pitch height had the strongest isolated effects — [Farbood 2012](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_2012_Musical_Tension.pdf)
- Earlier versions: Farbood's MIT dissertation, "A quantitative, parametric model of musical tension" (2006) — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/34182); [MIT Media Lab project page](https://opera.media.mit.edu/projects/tension.html)

**Herremans & Chew tension ribbons (spiral array)**
- Original paper: D. Herremans and E. Chew (2016), "Tension ribbons: Quantifying and visualising tonal tension," *Proc. TENOR 2016*, Cambridge UK — cited in [partitura source](https://partitura.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/partitura/musicanalysis/tonal_tension.html)
- Definitions: **cloud diameter** = largest distance between any two notes of a time-slice in spiral-array space (dissonance); **cloud momentum** = movement of the center of effect between consecutive windows (tonal movement); **tensile strain** = distance from the window's center of effect to the key's center of effect (distance from tonic/key) — [partitura source](https://partitura.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/partitura/musicanalysis/tonal_tension.html); [MorpheuS paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.04832)
- **Code 1, partitura (Python):** `estimate_tonaltension(note_info, ws=1.0, ss="onset", ...)` accepts a structured note array, `Part` or `PerformedPart`; estimates pitch spelling and key if missing; returns `cloud_diameter`, `cloud_momentum`, `tensile_strain` per onset — [partitura docs](https://partitura.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/partitura/musicanalysis/tonal_tension.html)
- **Code 2, midi-miner (Python):** computes the three measures from MIDI (default window one half note), estimates pitch spelling and key via spiral array, flags key changes (tensile-strain ratio > 2 for four consecutive 16-beat windows), and classifies melody/bass/harmony tracks with a random forest trained on 3,265 Chinese pop songs (F1 0.94/0.95/0.91). Repository https://github.com/ruiguo-bio/midi-miner; license not stated; no validation of the tension output reported — [Guo et al., arXiv 1910.02049](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.02049)
- Herremans' tension software is also linked from dorienherremans.com/tension — [MorpheuS paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.04832)

**MorpheuS (fit music to a target tension profile)**
- Variable neighborhood search assigns pitches to a template's fixed rhythm; objective = sum of Euclidean distances between candidate and target profiles for the three ribbon measures (weights 1); moves change1, swap, changeSlice; ~12% perturbation at local optima; repeated patterns found by COSIATEC/SIATECCompress (Meredith) and imposed as hard constraints; segment length default 1/8 note — [Herremans & Chew, arXiv 1812.04832](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.04832)
- Results: tension-profile correlations with the template of 0.9748, 0.9918, 0.9993 (vs 0.41, 0.21, 0.62 for random starts); runtimes 136 s (Kabalevsky) to 526 s (Rachmaninov); listener evaluation was only qualitative expert feedback (repetition without development, awkward cadences/transitions); implemented in Java, no source release mentioned — [arXiv 1812.04832](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.04832)
- Published in *IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing* — [Herremans site announcement (title)](https://dorienherremans.com/content/new-article-moprheus-music-generation-system-ieee-transactions-affective-computng); [KCL Pure record](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/morpheus-generating-structured-music-with-constrained-patterns-an/)

**Deep-learning and other newer tension models (2018–2026)**
- **Tension-controlled VAE** (Guo, Simpson, Magnusson, Kiefer & Herremans, arXiv 2010.06230, 2020; AIMC 2020 per Zenodo record): uses two spiral-array tension measures; user sets tension direction and level; generates pitch variations of a seed while keeping its rhythm — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06230); [Herremans site](https://dorienherremans.com/content/vae-music-generation-tension-control); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/4285344) (evaluation and code details not in fetched text)
- **Dual-level beam search for explicit tonal-tension conditioning** (Ebrahimzadeh, Bernardes & Stober, CMMR 2025, arXiv 2511.19342): tension from the Tonal Interval Vector (TIV) model (tonal distance + dissonance + voice leading), not spiral array; Transformer (FIGARO-style, REMI+ tokens) trained on 25,555 Lakh MIDI files; bar-level beams re-ranked by Pearson correlation with a user tension curve. Tension correlation rose from 0.16 (baseline) to 0.50 (0.62 after excluding degenerate cases; 0.42 for 16 bars); 18-listener test identified the intended curve in 4/10 samples clearly, 9/10 counting confusions among similar rising curves; ~7 min per 8-bar sample. Code: github.com/MaraalE/tension-beamsearch — [arXiv 2511.19342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19342)
- **TenseMusic** (Barchet, Rimmele & Pelofi 2024, *PLOS ONE* 19(1):e0296385): fully automatic, **audio-only** pipeline with loudness, pitch height, tonal tension (from polyphonic pitch estimates), roughness, tempo, onset frequency; 38 Western classical excerpts (60–90 s), 24 raters; cross-validated Spearman r = .61 (time-scale model). Loudness carried the largest weight; roughness predicted well; "the predictive power of tonal tension was small." Code: github.com/vivienbarchet/TenseMusic; data on OSF (osf.io/zspuw) — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0296385)
- **EDM build-up tension** (Bilious & Dahl, SMC 2026, Zagreb, pp. 273–279): Gestalt-based audio Tension Index (pitch proximity, timbral similarity, temporal density); 14 listeners, 12 EDM excerpts (breakdowns, build-ups, drops); mean r = .57 at a 2 s lag, but a simple **linear-ramp baseline did better on average** after detrending; "substantial track-specific tension remains unexplained"; no code listed — [Zenodo 24023747](https://zenodo.org/records/24023747)
- **IDyOM surprisal and tension (2026 neural study):** in continuous tension ratings of Beethoven 5/i, Brahms Piano Concerto 1/iii and a Schubert Lied, loudness correlated strongly with tension for all pieces, and long-term IDyOM surprisal correlated with tension for most pieces, whereas short-term (within-piece repetition) surprisal did not; a six-feature tension model (apparently Farbood/TenseMusic-type) matched mean ratings at r = .51, .65, .43 — [bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.01.15.699693](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.15.699693v1.full) (preprint; authors not shown in fetched text)
- Perceptual-feature-controlled harmonization (arXiv 2511.14600, title only) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14600) (not read)

**Melodic contour measures (symbolic, open code)**
- Cosine contours (DCT of a resampled pitch contour; c1 descendingness, −c2 archedness), code github.com/bacor/cosine-contours — [ISMIR 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000016.pdf); clustering/typology tests, code github.com/bacor/shapes-of-music — [arXiv 2604.13119](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13119v1)

### Inferences
- For a symbolic-score pipeline, the practical stack is: (1) spiral-array ribbons via `partitura.musicanalysis.estimate_tonaltension` (it takes a score `Part`, so MusicXML/MIDI from a LilyPond/MIDI export works) for harmonic tension over time; (2) Farbood-style surface features computed directly from the score (melodic pitch height, bass height, onset density, tempo, and a *notated* dynamics proxy in place of audio loudness); (3) IDyOM long-term surprisal per note/chord; (4) cosine-contour coefficients per phrase and per song for registral arch. All four are computable without audio.
- Weighting matters more than harmonic sophistication: every listener-validated model (Farbood, TenseMusic, the 2026 neural study, Granot & Eitan in Q3) finds loudness/dynamics and pitch height dominant, with tonal tension contributing little once those are in. A score-based engine without real loudness must therefore model dynamics/texture explicitly (number of voices, register span, notated dynamics, instrumentation density), or its "tension curve" will mostly miss what listeners report.
- Lerdahl–Krumhansl is the most theory-rich and best fit to ratings on tonal classical excerpts, but its dependence on hand or semi-automatic GTTM trees makes it the hardest to automate; spiral-array ribbons are the pragmatic substitute.
- The EDM result (a linear ramp beats a feature model) suggests that for build-ups, listeners largely report a learned schema ("tension rises until the drop") rather than tracking features moment-to-moment. A pop engine could treat build-up tension as a schema to be satisfied, not a quantity to be predicted.
- Targeting a tension curve during generation is now demonstrated three ways (VNS optimization, latent-space control, beam re-ranking), but reported adherence is moderate (r ≈ 0.5–0.6 for the Transformer approach) and none has shown that following a curve improves listener liking.

### Gaps

## Q3. What does the evidence say about which shapes listeners prefer (peak placement, rise–fall, build, repetition)?

### Takeaway
Evidence on *preferred* shapes is thin and indirect. What is well established is what listeners *track* (tension follows loudness, register and density; section ends coincide with tension peaks; chills coincide with crescendos, new-voice entries and unprepared harmonies) and how they *remember* (overall evaluations mostly reflect the average moment-to-moment intensity, with peak and end adding a smaller amount; the peak-end rule holds only weakly for music). Build-up–drop structures increase pleasure and movement; pop arousal peaks late (~0.73–0.78 of the song). No controlled experiment was found that manipulates whole-piece shape (e.g., early vs late climax, rise-fall vs build) and measures liking.

### Cited Findings

**Continuous tension ratings and what drives them**
- Krumhansl (1996), *Music Perception* 13(3), 401–432: listeners segmented, rated tension continuously, and marked new ideas in Mozart's K. 282/i; section endings coincided with tension peaks and slow tempos, new ideas with low tension; tension reflected melodic contour, note density, dynamics, harmony and tonality; versions with flattened dynamics and/or tempo produced largely similar tension curves, differing mainly near major section ends — [Scilit record (abstract)](https://scilit.com/publications/8d3965b5bfb58299a82af9527b9bf04c) (abstract only)
- Granot & Eitan (2011), *Music Perception* 28(3), 219–245: 132 participants (60 trained); loudness change, pitch direction and register affected overall tension, with **lower** register more tense; tempo had no main effect; parameters interacted rather than adding; louder and lower are strong drivers that modify contour and tempo effects; interpreted through an ecological "threat cue" model — [HUJI CRIS abstract](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/musical-tension-and-the-interaction-of-dynamic-auditory-parameter/) (abstract only)
- Farbood (2012): dynamics and melodic pitch height carry the largest weights; inter-subject agreement mean r = .47 — [Farbood 2012](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_2012_Musical_Tension.pdf). Note the apparent conflict with Granot & Eitan on register (Farbood: rising melodic pitch raises tension; Granot & Eitan: lower register is more tense overall), likely reflecting change vs level.
- TenseMusic (2024): loudness largest weight, roughness strong, tonal tension small — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0296385)

**Continuous emotion over time**
- Schubert (reported 2004, *Music Perception*): 67 listeners moved a mouse in a two-dimensional emotion space (sampled once per second) for four classical pieces; arousal was predicted by loudness and, less, tempo across all pieces; happy–sad by pitch and number of instruments, only in the Rodrigo and Grieg pieces — [News-Medical summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/2004/05/21/1777.aspx) (secondary press summary)
- Heng, Wei & McAdams (2025, *Music Perception* 43(3), 235–264): 90 listeners (Chinese-trained, Western-trained, nonmusicians) continuously rated intensity and valence/arousal through a 15.5-minute Chinese orchestral work at 2 Hz; arousal and intensity agreed across groups, valence diverged (nonmusicians rated a high-arousal negative passage as positive); intensity tracked arousal (functional canonical ρ = .62), not valence; arousal correlated with RMS energy (r = .30), roughness (.21), onset density (.19); no valence curve correlated with any acoustic feature — [Heng et al. PDF](https://www.mcgill.ca/mpcl/files/mpcl/heng_2026_muspercept.pdf)

**Peak-end rule and remembered intensity**
- Schäfer, Zimmermann & Sedlmeier (2014, *Frontiers in Psychology* 5:911): 54 participants, 11 complete unfamiliar songs across genres, continuous felt-intensity ratings then delayed global ratings (507 song ratings). Average intensity was the best single correlate (r = .805), then multiple-peaks-end (.796), peak-end (.784), end (.704), peak (.701). In regression (R² ≈ .69–.70), average β ≈ .58–.59 vs peak-end β ≈ .25–.26; variation and number of peaks were not significant. Remembered intensity correlated with liking at r = .63 — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00911/full)
- Rozin, Rozin & Goldberg (2004, *Music Perception*), as summarized by Schäfer et al.: ~40 s extracts; remembered intensity correlated with peak (r = .82), peak-end (.81), average (.80); peak-end best for only 3 of 20 participants; a "slope effect" (large moment-to-moment changes predicted higher remembered intensity) — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00911/full) (secondary)
- Sloboda & Lehmann (2001), Chopin prelude: average of moment ratings vs global rating r = .50 — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00911/full) (secondary)
- Lyric arcs and success (contest votes, not liking per se): songs with sad endings (n = 69) averaged 4.055 points vs 3.348 for happy endings; higher emotional peaks and happy endings were associated with fewer points; Man-in-Hole and Icarus shapes did better, Tragedy and Rags-to-Riches worse — [Rösch & Rauch 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10824-025-09532-6) (included because the main finding is a shape; success questions belong to the other researcher)

**Chills and climaxes**
- Musical events at chills (secondary summaries): Sloboda (1991) linked chills to "sudden dynamic or textural changes" and "unprepared harmonies"; Grewe et al. (2007, N = 38) linked chills to "entrances of a new voice or instrument" and found loudness correlated with skin conductance; Guhn, Hamm & Zentner (2007) are cited for null heart-rate results — [Bannister & Eerola 2021, *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity and the Arts*, accepted manuscript](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1234646)
- Bannister & Eerola propose two chill types, "vigilance" and "social" chills — [Bannister & Eerola 2021](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1234646) (title-level claim)
- Huron's ITPRA (*Sweet Anticipation*, 2006): five response systems, Imagination, Tension (pre-event arousal/attention), Prediction (reward for accurate expectation, used to explain mere exposure), Reaction (fast, ~150 ms, worst-case; linked to frisson, laughter, awe), Appraisal (slower conscious reassessment) — [Schwab-Felisch review, ZGMTH 10/2 (2013)](https://storage.gmth.de/zgmth/pdf/731). The review notes the book does not address large-scale form.

**Build-ups, drops and section energy**
- Solberg & Jensenius (2016, *Empirical Musicology Review* 11(3–4), 302–318): 16 people dancing to a 14-min house DJ mix; group motion dropped sharply in the breakdown and peaked at the drop, with post-drop motion higher than pre-breakdown; 78% rated the track with a strong break routine ("Icarus": layered build-up, rising pitch effects, 16th-note snare roll, then bass and kick return) quite/very pleasurable vs 46–53% for control tracks with little structural change; a filter-sweep-only build-up with continuous groove ("Ladykiller") drew a weaker response (69%) — [Solberg & Jensenius 2016](https://core.ac.uk/download/225893003.pdf)
- Pop section arousal (audio-model estimates): verse lowest, pre-chorus rising, chorus higher, bridge highest; repeats escalate; arousal peak at ~0.73–0.78 of song length, final third in 79% of songs — [Lyu, arXiv 2609.15675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15675)
- Nobile's telos principle (chorus onset as goal; prechorus as build) and Van Balen's chorus features are covered in the earlier note — see `music_as_language_theory.md` and [Nobile, MTO](https://www.mtosmt.org/ojs/index.php/mto/article/view/864/206)

**Narrative and tension alignment**
- Imagined story events cluster at moments of salient tension change, more so for highly narrative excerpts — [Margulis et al. 2022](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/when-did-that-happen-the-dynamic-unfolding-of-perceived-musical-n/)

**Predictability over time (building on the earlier IDyOM/Cheung note)**
- Long-term (style) IDyOM surprisal tracks continuous tension in symphonic/Lied pieces, short-term (within-piece) surprisal does not — [bioRxiv 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.15.699693v1.full)

### Inferences
- **Remembered value ≈ mean intensity + a modest peak/end bonus.** For design, that argues for sustaining engagement throughout (no long dead zones) rather than banking on one huge climax; the peak and ending still add measurable value, and Rozin's slope effect suggests contrast (large changes) helps memory.
- **Late peak is the norm in pop.** Combining Lyu's late arousal peak (≈0.73–0.78), escalation across repeats, and Nobile's telos principle, a defensible default shape for a verse–chorus song is a sawtooth (verse dip, pre-chorus ramp, chorus plateau) whose envelope rises across the song to a peak in the last third (bridge or final chorus), then a short release. This is descriptive of what is common, not proof listeners prefer it.
- **Climax design levers** with listener evidence: crescendo/texture increase, new voice entry, registral expansion upward in the melody, and an unprepared harmony at the peak (chills literature); breakdown-then-layered-build-then-drop (EDM); section ends at tension peaks followed by low-tension new material (Krumhansl 1996).
- **Golden section vs two-thirds vs three-quarters:** the only measured whole-song peak position found (pop, audio models) is later than the golden section (0.618). Mozart golden-section claims are post hoc and inconsistent. A tool should treat 0.6–0.8 as the plausible peak window, not 0.618 as a rule.
